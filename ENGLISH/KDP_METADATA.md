## Positioning

A contemporary psychological thriller with political-institutional stakes. The sales promise is not action spectacle but escalating pressure: a defensible exception works, becomes repeatable, and gradually shifts the standard itself.

## Book Description

**What if breaking the law is the right thing to do?**

Daniel Reuter is an investigator with Germany's Federal Criminal Police Office. He believes in evidence, procedure, and limits on state power—until intelligence points to an imminent attack. The warning is specific enough to be dangerous, but too thin to justify the measures that might stop it.

Daniel crosses a line.

And people live because he did.

What begins as a single exception starts to change the standard. Reviews widen. Decisions accelerate. Measures once considered disproportionate begin to look reasonable—especially when they produce results.

As the pressure mounts, Daniel is forced to confront a harder question than whether one breach was justified: what happens when an exception becomes a method, a method becomes policy, and the line you meant to hold is no longer where you left it?

*REASONABLE MEASURES* is a psychological thriller about national security, state power, and the quiet normalization of choices that become dangerous precisely because they work.
