## 37

The message arrived before Jonas had located Heller.

Daniel's phone vibrated on the table.

Once.

He looked at the display and already knew which channel it was.

Lena stood beside him.

"Don't touch it," she said.

"I have to read it."

"That's not what I meant."

Daniel opened the message.

Four lines.

No greeting.

**Preparation is continuing. Check the second service chain for the event setup. The new access expansion is only the visible part.**

Underneath was an internal identifier.

Then a vehicle reference Daniel knew from an older support fragment.

Final line:

**Once admission begins, the situation can no longer be corrected.**

Daniel read it twice.

The third time, he read only the identifier and the vehicle reference.

"Jonas."

Jonas came in from the adjoining room.

Daniel turned the phone toward him.

"Only those two things. Identifier and vehicle. The rest doesn't exist for you."

Daniel took a printout and did not copy the message.

He broke it apart.

**Second service chain.**

**Internal identifier.**

**Vehicle reference.**

**Access expansion only visible part.**

**Not correctable once admission begins.**

He left space beside each line.

Jonas pointed at the last one.

"What do I put there?"

"Nothing."

"Open?"

"Not testable in that form."

Lena said, "Pressure claim."

Daniel looked at her briefly.

"As a working category, yes."

He did not write it down.

Beside the service chain, initially only: **exists**.

Beside the identifier: **exists, significance open**.

Beside the vehicle: **legacy connection known, current relevance open**.

The claim about the access expansion received no status at all. Too broad. Too elastic.

"If all we end up with is two true nouns, we don't have a situation," Daniel said.

Jonas nodded.

"But two true nouns are more than zero."

"That's why we check."

Daniel covered Heller's final line with a blank sheet.

He did not want to forget it. He wanted to see whether the next decisions stayed the same without it.

The phone remained beside him.

Face down.

Daniel still knew exactly where it was.

Jonas read.

"Source?"

"Yes."

He looked at Daniel for only a moment. Then sat down.

"Okay."

Lena took a notepad.

"What do we already know independently?"

Daniel did not answer the entire message.

Only its parts.

The event was still going ahead.

Setup too.

From the storage find over the previous days, there were indications of improperly obtained access documents and technical preparation.

Nothing more.

The claim of a final threat still did not stand on its own.

Jonas typed.

"Identifier exists."

Daniel stepped behind him.

"Where from?"

"Event logistics. Added today."

"Status?"

Jonas opened the record.

"Not finalized yet. Different from yesterday."

Lena leaned closer.

"What exactly is it?"

"Temporary access expansion for a setup service provider. Several access points, time-limited."

"Threat connection?"

"None."

Daniel nodded.

He had already learned how quickly a normal authorization could turn into a threat inside his head.

"Don't mark it," he said.

Jonas looked up.

"I didn't."

"Good."

The vehicle reference took longer.

Ten minutes.

Then twelve.

Daniel did not pace the room.

He forced himself to sit.

Lena leafed through the earlier storage find.

"If Heller sent this, every true detail could be selectively chosen again."

Daniel looked at her.

"Yes."

"Including this identifier."

"Yes."

"And the final line is only pressure right now."

Daniel looked at **once admission begins**.

"Yes."

He said it without looking at the clock.

Jonas turned the screen.

"Vehicle is real."

"What does real mean?"

"Plate and keeper link from the older case match. And I've got a new independent hit."

Daniel leaned forward.

"Where?"

"Not from Heller's area. State office. Routine traffic stop this afternoon."

Lena put down the notepad.

"Location?"

Jonas named an area consistent with the event's logistics but not directly at the venue.

Daniel immediately felt the pieces wanting to connect.

He kept them separate.

"What did the stop produce?"

"Nothing illegal. Driver checked, allowed to continue."

"Connection to the network?"

"Through the vehicle from the old matter. Nothing more yet."

Daniel nodded.

True.

Not enough.

Lena looked at him.

"That's exactly the problem."

"Which one?"

"He doesn't have to lie."

Daniel picked up the phone and put it face down again.

"Then we treat the truth like any other tip."

"Meaning?"

"It has to earn its own justification."

Jonas looked up from the screen.

"That sounded pretty good."

Daniel looked at him.

"Forget it again."

Jonas went back to work.

Berg came in.

Lena closed the door behind him.

"Heller?" Daniel asked.

"Moving between two coordination points. Reachable."

"Don't contact him."

Berg looked at him.

"That's new."

Daniel gave him the short version.

It did not contain the entire evidentiary chain, but enough.

Heller strong source hypothesis. No formal proof. New message. Two genuine partial anchors. No sufficient confirmation of the claimed final threat.

Berg said nothing for a long time.

Then: "And if he's right?"

Berg pulled over a chair but did not sit.

"You have an ongoing event, a real vehicle hit, and a source you believe is manipulating you. I'll ask again: what's the consequence if you're too clean and this time he's simply right?"

Daniel looked at the clock.

Enough time left to become nervous.

Not enough to use nervousness as an excuse.

"The consequence can be real," he said.

"That wasn't my question."

"Yes, it was. Just not the answer you wanted."

Berg rested both hands on the back of the chair.

"I want to know how much uncertainty you're willing to tolerate now."

Daniel knew the formulation.

Weeks earlier, he would have talked about intervention thresholds. Then about prevented harm. Most recently about what happened if they scaled back too early.

Now the same question sat in front of him with Heller inside it.

"As much as necessary so the next measure is ours," Daniel said.

Lena looked at him but said nothing.

Berg answered, "That can cost time."

"Yes."

"And if there isn't enough time?"

Daniel pointed to Jonas's screen.

"Then the independent situation picture has to show me that. Not his final line."

For several seconds, nothing happened.

No new finding.

Only the ventilation and Jonas moving between two system states.

Since Daniel had learned to read waiting as a risk, every minute of stillness felt like a decision. That was Heller's strongest lever.

Not a lie.

Daniel's own memory of what delay could cost.

He let the clock run.

He stayed active and kept reviewing while it did.

Lena looked at Daniel.

Daniel knew the question.

It had followed him through half the case.

What happens if we don't act and we're wrong?

He had given it too much weight by now.

It still had not disappeared.

"Then we find out without him."

Berg twisted his mouth.

"How fast?"

"As fast as we can."

"That's not a time."

"No."

Daniel pointed at the identifier.

"We check the service chain through our own data. Jonas brings in only traces that arose independently. Anything that comes only from the message and can't be confirmed stays out."

"And if we need the project structure for that?"

Daniel looked at him.

There it was again.

The capability whose creation had now itself become part of the evidence against Heller.

"Then we use it for the review," Daniel said. "Not because the message tells us to."

Lena said nothing.

Neither did Berg.

A new response appeared on Jonas's screen.

He read it and looked up.

"I've got another connection."

Daniel walked over.

"Independent?"

"Yes."

Daniel waited.

"Sure."

Daniel checked provenance first and only then read the content.

Part of the new service chain did in fact touch a previously known branch of the perpetrator network. Not enough to prove the claimed end picture. Enough not to throw the warning away.

Lena stood behind him.

"More?"

"Not yet."

Daniel closed the source message.

He did not delete it.

He did not mark it confirmed either.

Daniel had Jonas note explicitly which claims still had **no** independent anchor.

The access expansion remained open.

So did the assertion that the situation would become uncorrectable once admission began.

Two genuine partial hits did not turn the message into a complete proof.

That distinction had to survive until the next decision.

"Keep checking," he said.

Jonas nodded.

Daniel looked at the clock.

This time not because Heller had shown it to him.

Because it was still running.
