## 23

The man's name was Nils Weber, and at first glance he was interesting only because his name appeared too often.

Three times in access lists.

Twice in phone connections.

Once in a shift overview that placed him at a location where another part of the structure had already appeared.

None of the points was strong on its own.

Together they looked better.

Too good, Daniel thought.

Jonas had opened Weber on the large screen and immediately pulled the defined group around the same access points.

Eighteen people.

Not suspects.

People whose professional roles, access, or communication paths fell within the agreed criteria.

Daniel had the heading changed.

**Relevant persons** became **review group**.

"Better?" Jonas asked.

"Less wrong."

"Work phone," Lena said.

Jonas pushed the assignment across to her.

"Registered to Weber."

"Doesn't mean only Weber uses it. And the access?"

"He coordinates evening shifts for the subcontractor. Legitimate access."

Daniel looked at the timeline.

"But not at 1:14 a.m."

"If the timestamp is correct," Lena said.

"It is."

"Then the question remains why."

Weber got his own line in the working view.

It was not red or highlighted; he sat at the top only because he had the most overlaps.

Jonas opened the first connection.

A call from Weber's work phone to a number that had entered the case through Mika.

Duration: forty-seven seconds.

"Who was on call?" Daniel asked.

"Still open."

"Then leave it open."

Second connection.

A nighttime access event.

Weber was listed as shift supervisor.

"Was he scheduled?"

"No. Off."

Lena looked at Daniel.

"That's better than the call. Not good—better."

On the second screen, the remaining hits from the new project structure were arriving.

Most fell apart quickly: spelling variants, shared on-call devices, delayed synchronization, legitimate shifts, regular vehicles. Jonas crossed them off. The list grew longer before it got shorter.

"Not exactly magic," he said.

Daniel looked at the open hits. "Good."

"You're the first person I've met who's happy about bad data."

"I'm happy about explainable data."

"Almost the same thing."

Daniel stayed with Weber.

"What's the harmless explanation?" he asked.

Lena answered immediately. "Work phone handed off. Shift swap. Error in the access assignment. Private connection to someone who only stands out because of Mika."

"And the dangerous version?"

"That he's using access he legitimately has for something else."

Daniel nodded.

A few weeks earlier, he would have stayed longer with the first list.

Now he thought of the security guard.

The minibus.

Three minutes between the wrong access and the useful one.

"Not just Weber," he said. "Check the defined group around the access points too."

Lena looked at him.

"That brings in people we have less on than Weber."

"I know."

"And that was not the original idea."

"Yes, it was. That's what we built the structure for."

"For faster cooperation. Not so a weak suspicion can strengthen itself simply by widening its reach."

Daniel looked back at Weber.

"We're not deciding guilt. We're looking for connections."

"And every search creates a record about people," Lena said.

"Logged and limited."

"That doesn't make it invisible."

Daniel did not answer.

It was not a victory.

Only a decision.

He authorized the defined review group.

Over the next two hours, almost all the remaining hits were explained. Weber stayed open. Daniel still removed him from the top line: standing out relative to others was not an additional hit.

The same four points applied to everyone in the review group: current timing, functional access, an independent second connection, and whether a harmless explanation remained open or had been refuted. For Weber, two were strong and two remained open.

"So Weber's still ahead," Jonas said.

"Yes. But on the same criteria as everyone else."

Lena reviewed the list. "And what happens when we get a hit?"

"Nothing automatically."

Then Jonas found a residual hit he had initially set aside.

"I marked this one weak."

Daniel stepped behind him.

A temporary driver for another service provider had been registered at two locations within nine days that also appeared in Mika's handovers. The first time was tied to a normal job. The second had no matching assignment.

"Why did you set it aside?"

"Because temporary driver, service provider, two places. That's almost the definition of driving around."

"Correct."

"But the second stay is after the end of his shift."

Daniel read the times.

9:38 to 10:06 p.m.

No assignment.

"Could be coincidence," Jonas said.

"Check it."

The hit did not lead to Weber or to the same subcontractor.

The first response was ordinary.

The driver claimed he had picked up a colleague.

The colleague confirmed it.

Daniel was ready to close the hit.

Then Lena asked, "Which vehicle?"

Jonas pulled the assignment.

It was one of the service provider's vans, not the driver's private car.

"Why use a company van to pick up a colleague privately?" Daniel asked.

"Still possible."

"Yes."

They checked the deployment schedule.

The van should already have been back at the depot by then. It was not returned until the next morning.

Small.

But real.

Daniel left the strand open.

The next response made it smaller again at first. The employer allowed drivers to take vans home privately in exceptional cases. No written approval for that evening, but no clear violation either.

"So almost nothing again," Jonas said.

"Almost," Daniel said.

They needed more.

An hour later, a second independently obtained fact arrived. That morning the temporary driver's vehicle had been stopped near a rented storage unit. Routine traffic stop. No arrest. Nothing unusual about the vehicle.

The storage unit, however, belonged to a company that connected, two steps away, to an already known support-network contact.

Daniel pulled the connection onto the screen.

Not from the new project data alone. Registry information and the current traffic stop confirmed it separately.

"Now?" Jonas asked.

Daniel looked at Lena.

"What's the alternative explanation?"

"The company rents storage space. The driver has a legitimate job or a private reason. The old support connection is historical and has no current relevance."

"And the second anchor?"

"Current vehicle at the current storage unit outside his duty schedule."

Daniel nodded.

So did Lena, once.

"Now."

The measures that followed were targeted.

No broad action against an entire group of people. No screen flashing red.

First, the actual user of the storage unit was confirmed.

Then the current vehicle movement.

Then a second person who had been there repeatedly over the past few days and had already appeared in a separate current review strand.

Only after that did the measure have enough basis.

Weber played no part in it.

Daniel noticed and pushed the thought away. Weber was not cleared yet. He simply was not the reason for this strand.

The first attempt to reach the user of the storage unit went nowhere. The listed business number went to voicemail. The rental company confirmed only that the contract existed and was paid.

For twenty minutes, the connection remained exactly that: an interesting address with a vehicle in front of it.

Then confirmation arrived from a separate current matter that the second person had in fact used the storage unit.

Only then did the colleagues on site request the targeted measure.

Daniel watched a weak residual hit become, step by step, something that could support a decision.

It took longer than a database hit.

And that was exactly why he trusted it more.

The storage unit was checked. Two people were found there. Documents and material inside were consistent with a preparatory step relevant to the event. Specialists took over the assessment.

The first report was cautious.

**Access documentation present. Origin unclear.**

Ten minutes later:

**Material likely connected to event logistics. Technical assessment ongoing.**

Jonas stood behind Daniel's chair.

"That looks good."

Daniel looked at him.

"For whom?"

Jonas sat down again.

"You know what I mean."

Daniel did.

At 6:42 p.m. the response came in:

**Direct relevance to the threat. Preparatory step interrupted in time.**

Jonas read the sentence twice.

"We wouldn't have seen that without the wider review group."

Daniel did not answer immediately.

He traced the path back.

Review group.

Temporary driver.

Second visit.

Company vehicle.

Current traffic stop.

Storage unit.

Independent company connection.

Measure.

No single magic hit.

A series of small things that gained meaning only beside one another.

"Probably not in time," Daniel said.

Lena looked at the chain.

"That's the point in favor of the wider review."

Jonas looked up. "You said that?"

"Yes."

"Should I write it down?"

"Enjoy the moment."

Daniel looked at the hit list.

Weber was still at the top.

His name had not disappeared.

But he had not led them here either. Even so, the success felt like confirmation that widening the review group had been the right decision.

Daniel allowed himself that feeling for a moment.

Outside, people had been prevented from entering a delivery area for which they had prepared material.

Another step had been stopped.

The new project route had made visible something the narrower review probably would have missed.

For a moment, that was enough.

He did not think about Weber.

Not yet.
