## 41

"Not the source," Daniel said. "What do we have ourselves?"

Jonas was out of breath.

"The vehicle from the current authorization has been stopped. The two people inside match our independently confirmed strand. Second location is a service entrance west of the event grounds."

Daniel stood beneath the canopy, now without a weapon.

Behind him, paramedics were working on Heller.

He did not look.

"How did you get the location?"

"From the vehicle. Regular documents. Delivery slot and access point. The organizer confirms the slot."

"Heller?"

"Not needed."

"What about his second service chain?"

"Partly overlaps."

"That wasn't my question."

A short pause.

"Part of it isn't confirmed."

"Then it stays out."

"Understood."

Daniel did not let Jonas leave the second location standing as a finished connection.

"Show me the path."

"From the stopped vehicle?"

"From the first independent point to here."

Jonas switched views.

First, the regular documents in the vehicle.

A delivery slot.

A service entrance.

Then confirmation from the organizer that the slot actually existed and had not been copied afterward from some source tip.

Then the identifier for the access point.

It matched the area they already knew from the advanced authorization.

"And which part of that comes from the anonymous message?"

"Nothing we need for this location."

Daniel pointed to a second marker.

"This one?"

Jonas hesitated.

"Would be faster. Heller mentioned a connection to another entrance."

"Independent?"

"Not yet."

"Then no."

"Even though it's closer?"

"Especially then."

Jonas removed the marker from the working window.

Daniel felt the lost seconds like physical resistance.

A shortcut lay visibly in front of them.

Maybe even the right one.

It stayed out anyway.

Lena said quietly, "This is the part you asked for earlier."

Daniel looked at her.

"Which part?"

"Making the information earn its justification."

He only nodded.

Lena came through the door.

Her hair was wet against her forehead.

She looked past Daniel toward Heller.

Then back.

"You're not hurt?"

"No."

"Heller?"

Daniel shook his head. "I don't know."

Neither said more.

On the channel, operational command reported the changed route.

The area with the eight employees was not entered through the old access point. A second team took over clearing them. The operational step shifted.

The changed route did not emerge from a single command.

First they confirmed which of the eight employees had already left the critical area.

Then the access route was reassessed.

One team held, another shifted.

Daniel heard only the outcomes, not every tactical intermediate step.

That was enough.

"Which part of this depends on our investigative picture?" he asked.

Jonas answered, "Service entrance, vehicle, access material. Not the exact route."

"Good."

Daniel did not want his professional role to become an operational command function in retrospect merely because he had triggered the withdrawal.

His task remained narrower.

Which information carried weight.

Which did not.

Which connection was strong enough to justify the next review point.

The first person at the second location could be connected through current access and the stopped vehicle.

The second could not immediately.

"Then treat them separately," Daniel said.

Jonas confirmed.

Berg asked over the channel, "Even now?"

"Especially now."

A few seconds later the next response came in.

The second person had no solid independent link to the older support strand. He was there through the same service provider.

Daniel felt something almost like relief.

The situation had not become smaller at all. What relieved him was that a counterhypothesis had survived.

The shared structure had made the name visible quickly.

Human review had kept visibility from becoming guilt immediately.

"Remove him from the offender attribution," Daniel said.

Jonas did.

Shortly afterward, the specialists confirmed the other part.

Material, access, and preparation fit together.

Not completely.

But enough.

Daniel looked at the time and realized he had not looked at Heller for several minutes.

He did not know whether that was concentration or escape.

It did not matter yet.

The situation was still live.

When it was finally established that the planned next step could no longer take place, Daniel stayed on the channel.

He had the open points read back to him.

People remained unclear, connections open, parts of Heller's warning unconfirmed.

The core was enough anyway.

Major harm had very likely been prevented.

And the unconfirmed parts remained unconfirmed.

Both had to go into the same file later.

Daniel had Jonas add one more thing.

"Timestamp every discarded lead."

"Why?"

"So nobody later pretends we saw only the right hits."

Jonas understood immediately.

The report would become longer and more uncomfortable because of it.

A false duplicate badge.

A plausible driver.

A person whose contact initially looked suspicious and did not hold up.

Beside them, the few connections that had actually supported measures.

Daniel watched the list form.

The working method had functioned.

But only because people had repeatedly decided **not** to act on what the system made visible.

That too belonged to the success.

It took only seconds, but Daniel heard every one of them.

"Vehicle at the second location," Jonas said.

"Confirmed?"

"Yes. Two at the vehicle, at least one more in the service area. Still no complete picture."

Daniel looked through the rain toward the lights of the grounds.

"Access documents?"

"Match the material from the warehouse strand."

"Independent?"

"Yes."

Lena stood beside him.

"You're asking that after every sentence now."

"Yes."

"Good."

Daniel listened to the next reports.

No images.

No heroic shouting.

Only short sentences.

Person checked.

Access secured.

Vehicle opened.

Specialists taking over material assessment.

Event operations on the affected service axis suspended.

Then nothing for almost a minute.

The minute without an update stretched because no one filled it.

Daniel heard only the rain, radio traffic, and somewhere behind the door the wheels of a stretcher.

He did not look.

A technical query came over the channel first, then silence again.

Jonas said, "Connection holds."

"Which one?"

"Vehicle to service entrance. Access documents to material from the warehouse strand. Person one to the independently confirmed support contact. Person two still open."

"Then leave him open."

Berg cut in.

"Through the shared structure we have a name with contact to both vehicles."

Daniel closed his eyes briefly.

"Direct?"

"A current contact point."

"So not direct. Counterhypothesis?"

"Professional contact through the service provider."

"Then no step based on that alone."

Berg exhaled audibly.

"Understood."

Daniel opened his eyes again.

That was exactly why they had limited the structure.

It could see faster.

It could not judge faster.

Daniel therefore had Jonas lead every new report with its origin.

Heller's message no longer decided which lead stayed on top. The order came from independent responses. If one of them collapsed, the situation picture had to shrink too—even now, even immediately before intervention. No hit could weigh more simply because it fit the final minutes.

"Service entrance, person one, person two?"

"Service entrance: documents from the stopped vehicle plus organizer confirmation. Person one: current access and independently confirmed support contact. Person two: shared service provider only."

"Then open."

Jonas began constructing the sentences that way himself before Daniel asked.

He phrased the next one more narrowly: *We have a current contact point; counterhypothesis: professional relationship.*

Daniel read the same line twice. The letters were sharp; only his mind was back beneath the canopy for two seconds: Heller's hand, the shot, wet concrete. Then he forced himself back to the screen.

Daniel pressed his left hand beneath the edge of the table until the shaking diminished.

"Continue."

Lena saw it.

She said nothing.

That helped.

The shooting could not suddenly make Heller's tips less credible or Daniel's own strands more credible.

Every report had to earn the same path it had three minutes earlier.

Precisely because Daniel himself was no longer the same person he had been three minutes earlier.

Seconds later, the first solid report arrived from the service area.

The first crack appeared in Heller's picture already.

One expected person was not there.

Part of the assumed connection was missing.

But the secured material, real access, and altered time window were enough to support the threat core.

Relief did not come. Almost worse was that reality was once again not clean enough to morally rescue either side.

Daniel's left hand trembled slightly beneath the table.

Berg came onto the channel.

"Daniel?"

"Here."

"Another hit from the project structure. Person in the defined access group, current contact to the stopped vehicle."

"What's the counterhypothesis?"

Berg was silent.

Daniel closed his eyes.

"Thomas."

"Professional contact through the same service provider."

"Then don't touch him until you have more."

"Time?"

"We have other anchors."

Berg said nothing more.

A few minutes later, operational command confirmed that both vehicles and the service area were secured.

The specialists gave their first assessment.

The secured material belonged to the same preparatory complex they had been finding piece by piece over the previous days. Combined with the access rights and the advanced time window, it created a concrete threat to the event.

The planned next step could no longer take place.

Daniel asked, "Major harm prevented?"

The answer did not come immediately.

"Based on what we know now: yes."

This time Daniel removed the earpiece completely.

Only for three seconds.

The world did not become quiet.

Rain on metal.

Footsteps behind him.

A door.

Then he put it back in.

Major harm had, on the current information, been prevented. The decisive contribution had come from the working method Daniel himself had continued and limited. Heller's final message did not explain that outcome any more than simple luck excused the problematic decisions.

Daniel had no formula for it.

Only the fact.

It had worked.

And none of that made the ground behind him any cleaner.

He took the earpiece out again.

Only from one ear.

Behind him, the stretcher was lifted.

Daniel looked.

Heller's face was white beneath the canopy light. An oxygen mask covered it. His eyes were closed.

No conversation.

No look.

The stretcher disappeared through the door.

Lena leaned back against the wall.

"It worked," Berg said from the speaker on the table.

No one answered.

Daniel heard Berg's sentence again in his head.

**It worked.**

He wanted to object immediately.

The sentence was not false. Daniel objected because it was too large.

What exactly had worked?

The independent confirmation before intervention.

The parallel review.

The limitation to the current access group.

The rejection of several false and ordinary hits.

The changed route after the new information.

Those parts did not all belong in the same moral equation.

Daniel said into the channel, "Document separately in the final status: which hits came from the shared structure, which measure rested on what, and which leads were discarded."

Jonas answered, "Will do."

"Heller's unconfirmed parts too."

"Separate."

Only then did Daniel allow the sentence.

Major harm had been prevented.

That remained true.

Daniel looked at the place where Heller had been lying.

Rain was slowly washing the blood toward the drain.

Then he put the earpiece properly back in.

"What's still open?"
