## 20

The problem was the number of hits, not the plate or the legacy records.

Daniel had recorded it in his note after checking B-QV 4172 against the protected records. Three hits. Limited to plate matches and existing associations. No expansion to persons.

Later the source had written:

**B-QV 4172. Three hits. You limited the search to the plate.**

Jonas sat opposite him with four versions of the same matter open.

"I froze the historical versions," he said. "Not just the current one."

Daniel nodded.

The first screen showed Daniel's original access note.

The second, the short operational version.

The third, the situation summary.

The fourth, a technical access record Daniel himself could read only in part.

"None of this was ever public."

"No."

"On the operations channel?"

"Only the lead we gained. Not how the query was run."

Jonas opened the log.

No number of hits. No limitation.

"In the operational summary?"

Daniel looked at Lena.

She stood at the window with her arms folded.

"Later," she said.

"How much later?"

"After the operation."

"Before 11:18 p.m.?"

Lena did not answer immediately.

Daniel noticed.

"Lena."

"The access was logged. The note existed. So did the technical access record."

"Who had them?"

"More than three people."

"Who exactly?"

"That's not the same question as who could have been the source."

"I didn't say it was."

"It sounds like it right now."

Jonas looked from one to the other and decided to look back at his screen.

Daniel turned his chair toward Lena.

"I don't want a suspect list. I want information paths."

Lena looked at him.

"Then say that."

"I am."

She unfolded her arms.

"Fine. The original note: you, Berg, me. Technical roles for access logging. Later, the separate internal review. The operational short version went to the situation group, but shortened."

Jonas put check marks beside the versions.

"Number of hits and the limitation?"

"In the original note."

"In the situation version?"

"No."

"Cross-agency coordination?"

Lena lifted one shoulder slightly. "Depends which working version was passed on."

"Heller?"

"Possible."

"Possible meaning?"

"That I don't know what was in his summary."

Daniel nodded.

"And what do you know?"

Lena looked at him.

"More about the oversight path than you need to know right now."

Jonas looked up after all.

Daniel said nothing.

Lena held his gaze.

"That wasn't a good answer," he said.

"Maybe not."

"Then give me a better one."

"No."

The room was small enough that the word sounded harder than Lena had said it.

Daniel stood.

"You've been demanding for days that I separate information properly."

"Yes."

"Then separate it."

"That's exactly what I'm doing."

"By refusing to tell me who can access the documentation?"

"By telling you what you can reliably use for this source question and what you can't."

"Why can't you tell me who?"

Lena took one breath.

"Because an independent oversight path stays independent only if the person whose decisions are being reviewed does not control every step of the review."

Daniel stayed standing.

There it was.

Not yet an explanation, but at least the shape of a secret.

"My decisions."

"Among other things."

"Since when?"

"That's exactly the part I'm not opening up here as an informal side process."

"We're not in the hallway."

"You know what I mean."

Daniel went to the window.

Outside, the yard lay gray under low clouds. A delivery van stood half on the sidewalk.

"You have a separate track running."

Lena did not answer.

That was not proof.

But it was new.

And it touched exactly the kind of information Daniel was looking for: a protected path she knew and he did not.

He felt the suspicion gain weight before a single new fact had been added.

That was the dangerous part.

Jonas cleared his throat.

"I've got something else."

Daniel turned.

Jonas had put the document versions side by side.

"The source can't have inferred the three from a later report. The first version with the number of hits and the limitation existed only in the protected case record. The shortened situation version afterward contains neither."

"Sure?"

"Yes."

He showed them both versions. The later one said only **cross-case vehicle lead**. Nothing about three hits. Nothing about limiting the check to the plate.

Daniel checked the modification time.

10:41 p.m.

The source message had arrived at 11:18.

"So someone could have read the early version," Jonas said.

"Or heard about it."

"Or seen the technical record."

Lena said, "Exactly."

"Or through a review office."

Lena looked at him.

"Yes."

Daniel registered the brief hesitation before her answer.

Again, no proof.

Again, something that weighed more heavily with her because she really was withholding something from him now.

"And Berg?" Jonas asked.

Daniel looked at the access list. "Knew the note."

"Heller?"

"Could have received detail through coordination. Not established."

"Technical staff?"

"Can see logs. Motive open."

Daniel did not write names. He wrote paths.

**Original note.**

**Access logging.**

**Separate review.**

**Verbal onward disclosure.**

**Coordination.**

That removed the convenient explanation that someone had simply seen a normal forwarded summary.

The information had come from a protected space.

It did not have to have come directly from the system.

Someone could have read it and told someone else. Someone could have heard it in a meeting. A technical role could have seen more than it should.

But it had not been public.

Daniel took a second notepad.

"From now on, not everyone gets the same information set."

Lena closed her eyes briefly.

"That will make the work worse."

"Maybe."

"No. It will. Two people will know different versions of the same situation. Questions will take longer. Someone will miss a connection because they don't know it exists."

Daniel wrote three workstreams.

**Operational situation. Source file. Internal provenance review.**

"Exactly why we separate by function."

"And when function doesn't separate cleanly?"

"Then we decide."

"Who?"

Daniel looked at her.

"For now, me."

Lena did not laugh. "Of course."

"Have a better proposal?"

"Yes. A documented distribution model with an independent check instead of your personal gut call."

Daniel stopped.

"Build one."

The answer caught her off guard briefly.

"What?"

"You're right. Not just my distribution list. You define the independent check. Jonas maintains the operational picture. Provenance review stays with the smallest group possible."

Lena studied him.

"And you accept that you don't decide everything alone?"

"If it reduces information leakage."

"We don't know that yet."

"Then we measure it."

She stepped away from the window.

"And that's exactly what I mean. Distrust creates work."

"So does trust without verification."

The sentence came out faster than Daniel intended.

Before Lena left, the first question from the operations team came in.

Jonas read it aloud. "Service-provider connection from Mika's handovers. They want to know whether the source ever mentioned anything about it."

Daniel looked at his three workstreams.

There was nothing about it in the operational picture.

The source file contained an old, vague reference that might help.

"Who's asking?"

Jonas named the office.

"They don't have the source file," Daniel said.

"Exactly."

Daniel hesitated.

The separation was working.

And making the answer slower.

He opened the source file himself, checked the old reference, determined that it added nothing defensible to the specific question, and sent back only that conclusion.

Almost four minutes.

Before, Jonas could have searched the shared file in seconds.

Lena looked at the time.

"That's what I mean."

"Four minutes."

"Four today."

"And if it stops us from showing the source what everyone knows?"

"Then it may be worth it. But don't pretend caution is free."

Daniel nodded.

He added a fourth item beside the three workstreams:

**Document delays.**

Then they tested the new distribution model on two more real questions.

The first became faster because the operational picture was enough. The second had to go through Daniel again because it touched source knowledge.

Six minutes.

Jonas wrote the number beside the four from the first round without comment.

Daniel looked at it.

"You're keeping score?"

"You wanted to measure."

"Right."

"Distrust has now cost ten minutes."

Lena said, "And perhaps prevented a leak. We don't know either way."

Daniel nodded.

That was exactly why he left the numbers there. The new caution was not a free security upgrade. It only shifted which risk became visible.

Lena gathered her papers.

"Then I hope you still trust yourself."

She left.

Jonas waited until the door closed.

"That was uncomfortable."

Daniel sat down again.

"Yes."

"Do you think it's her?"

Daniel looked at the path labeled **separate review**.

That would have been the easy answer.

Lena knew a protected channel. Lena was withholding details. Lena had access to the original note.

All true.

"I think she's not telling me something right now."

"Not the same thing."

"No."

Daniel did not put Lena's name into the working document.

He marked only the path she would not disclose.

Less satisfying.

Cleaner.
