## 43

Heller died the next morning at 5:18 a.m.

Daniel got the news in the corridor outside the conference room.

Berg said only, "He's dead."

Daniel looked at him.

"When?"

"Twenty minutes ago."

"Who knows?"

"Enough people."

Daniel put the phone away.

Nothing more came.

Nothing more came from Heller either. No message. No explanation.

Three folders lay on the table in the conference room.

The head of the internal review pointed to them.

"We separate this."

Daniel sat beside Lena.

Berg sat opposite them.

"First: Mr. Heller's information steering, accesses, project activity, and the preliminary review of Mr. Reuter."

Her hand rested on the first folder.

"Second: Mr. Reuter's own procedural violations and yesterday's shooting."

Second folder.

"Third: the operational impact of the working structure created during the case."

Third folder.

The review lead left all three closed for the moment.

"Before we mix the contents, I need a simple discipline from everyone in this room," she said. "If you use a point from folder one to answer a question from folder two, say so explicitly."

Berg frowned.

"That's artificial."

"No. Artificial would be pretending the operational success settles the question of the shooting. Or that Heller's manipulation settles the question of Mr. Reuter's decisions."

Daniel looked at the edge of the table.

He did not like the separation.

That was precisely why it was necessary.

In his own head, the pieces had long since begun explaining one another.

Heller had created pressure.

Daniel had made decisions under pressure.

People had been protected.

Someone had died.

It would have been easy to turn that into one story.

The three folders prevented it.

Berg folded his arms.

"You can't separate it completely."

"You don't have to," the woman said. "But no answer automatically closes the other two."

Daniel looked at the second folder.

His name was not written on the cover.

Only a case number.

"Let's start with Heller," Lena said.

They laid out the chain.

The early professional assessment and the improper preliminary check on Daniel.

The 8:11 p.m. trace and the anonymous messages.

The plate processed in Heller's working environment before the first real harm and later omitted from the warning.

The project records in which Daniel's successes were subsequently used institutionally.

None of it was complete by itself.

Together, it was difficult to construct another explanation requiring fewer additional assumptions.

"No direct technical attribution of the message channel?" the review lead asked.

"No," Daniel said.

"No confession?"

Daniel looked at her.

"No."

She nodded and wrote something down.

"Then that's how we phrase it."

Then came Daniel's folder.

The first protected cross-case data check.

The later broader review.

The decision not to scale back.

The shooting.

"Before yesterday's expanded review, you knew Mr. Heller had probably helped construct the pressure situation deliberately—and you used the structure anyway?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

Daniel looked at the question as though it were lying on the table.

"Because the final threat was independently confirmed, and in my assessment the narrower path carried a higher time risk."

"Did you have another option?"

"Yes."

"At the shooting too?"

Daniel was silent for a second.

"Yes."

Berg shifted.

Lena did not.

The review lead asked, "Which ones?"

"Close the distance. Stop him physically. Rely on operational command to hold my withdrawal against his message in time."

She wrote the three possibilities down separately.

"At the time, did you consider any of them certain to fail?"

"No."

"Any certain to succeed?"

Daniel looked at the list.

"No."

"Then you were not choosing between possible and impossible."

"No."

"You were choosing between risks under time pressure."

Daniel nodded.

Berg moved in his chair.

The review lead did not look at him.

"For the review, it matters that we do not manufacture certainty backward from the outcome. The fact that the message was not sent does not prove you could certainly have stopped Heller another way. But it also does not prove the shot was the only possible option."

"Yes."

Daniel did not like the sentence.

It was still precise.

"And the project structure?" she asked.

"Same question," Daniel said. "The narrower path existed. I chose not to use it because of the time risk."

"Even though you already knew about Heller's manipulation."

"Yes."

The two decisions were not in the same folder, but they shared the same uncomfortable core.

Daniel had seen alternatives.

He had weighted them.

And he could not reduce responsibility simply because some of the later outcomes favored him.

"Why didn't you use the non-shooting option?"

"Because at that moment I wasn't sure it would work in time."

"Not sure is not the same as ruled out."

"No."

The review lead let the answer stand.

"Let me ask it another way. Did you believe the non-shooting alternative was impossible?"

Daniel looked at the second folder.

"No."

"Practically too uncertain?"

"At that moment: yes."

"That's your assessment from then, not ours from today."

"Correct."

She wrote.

Daniel sensed Berg shift beside the third folder but say nothing.

There was no wording with which Daniel could make the moment smaller without also making it less honest.

He could say Heller had been about to transmit an outdated assessment.

He could say the operational effect would have been immediate.

He could say a physical alternative had existed and its timely success had been uncertain.

After that, the decision was still his.

She wrote again.

No one helped Daniel.

He did not want them to.

Then came the third folder.

Berg opened it.

"The structure delivered the decisive time savings in the final phase."

Lena looked at him. "And we had Nils Weber. Me. Several correlations weighted incorrectly. That belongs in the same balance."

"I'm not disputing that."

The review lead went through the documents.

"The question for the current case will be moot soon anyway. The project structure is time-limited."

Daniel looked up.

"And afterward?"

"That's no longer the same decision."

"What decision is it then?"

"Which elements were operationally useful. Which can be made legally sustainable. Which control thresholds are necessary. What feeds into the ongoing reform effort."

Daniel looked at Lena.

She had heard it too.

Berg said, "That's reasonable."

"Maybe," Lena said.

The review lead continued.

"When the temporary structure expires, its current legal and working basis expires too."

Berg nodded.

"But not the finding that parts of it worked."

"Correct."

Lena said, "And not the finding that parts of it wrongly implicated people."

"Also correct."

The review lead took a blank page from the third folder.

"So the next question will not be whether we extend this exact project. It will be which elements, if any, can be transferred into a regular framework."

Daniel looked up.

"That already answers whether."

"No."

"Partly."

She thought for a moment.

"For individual capabilities, perhaps. Not for every reach, every data source, or every threshold."

It was reasonable.

That was exactly what made the sentence harder.

No one in the room said Heller had been right.

No one said Daniel had been right.

And yet they were no longer discussing whether the working method created in the case should disappear entirely.

They were discussing limits, roles, and controls.

The vocabulary had shifted before a final decision had been made.

Daniel looked at her.

She returned the look.

The new question was already sitting in the room as though the old one had been settled.

Daniel pulled the first folder toward himself again.

"The preliminary check goes in in full."

The review lead nodded.

"Even if that later changes how my own access is evaluated."

"That's the point of the review."

Daniel also pushed forward the documents on the shooting.

"Then everything."

Daniel remained seated as the review lead gathered the documents.

"When will there be an assessment of my shooting?"

"Not today."

"And the other violations?"

"Not today either."

Berg looked at him.

Daniel ignored the look.

"Until then?"

"The usual interim measures apply. You'll be informed separately."

Daniel nodded.

No dramatic suspension.

No immediate exoneration.

Only a process that would last longer than the situation in which everything had happened.

Lena closed her folder.

"And Heller?" she asked.

The review lead looked at the first folder.

"There will be open points there too that his death can no longer close."

Daniel looked at the stack of files.

That was the most uncomfortable form of ending.

No final statement. No clean motive formula. No way to assign Heller everything in retrospect that Daniel himself had decided.

He stood.

The third folder remained on the table.

Operational impact.

It was the only one everyone already discussed as though there would inevitably be another version afterward.

Berg said nothing.

Neither did Lena.

Daniel did not place his hand on any of the three folders.

For the first time, he did not have to solve any of them immediately.

Heller's actions would be reviewed.

Daniel's own decisions would be reviewed too.

And independently, other people would decide what remained of the working structure.

The three processes could reach different conclusions.

That was not disorder.

It was probably the cleanest form still possible after this case.

Before Daniel left the room, the review lead asked, "Do you want to know whether the assessment of the structure affects your own proceeding?"

Daniel stopped.

Of course he wanted to know.

If the working structure was ultimately judged useful, controllable, and suitable for a future regular framework, every decision in the case would read differently in retrospect.

Maybe not legally.

But humanly.

"No," he said.

The review lead raised her eyebrows slightly.

"Why not?"

Daniel looked at the three folders.

"Because that would pull the questions back together again."

Heller could have manipulated him.

Daniel could have violated boundaries.

The working structure could still contain elements other people wanted to retain in the future.

None of those statements justified the next one.

Lena was already standing by the door.

"Good answer," she said.

Daniel looked at her.

"Wasn't a test."

"Those are the worst ones."

The review lead closed Daniel's folder.

"Then you'll receive the assessments separately."

"Good."

Daniel walked toward the door.

He knew separate proceedings did not mean the consequences would remain separate.

Only that no one was allowed to take a good outcome from one folder and use it to close another.

In the corridor, Daniel stopped briefly at the window.

Three folders. Three timelines.

Heller's possible manipulation began before Daniel was assigned the case.

Daniel's own rule violation began later.

The operational impact of the new structure later still.

The sequence alone prevented them from being turned into a single clean cause-and-effect sentence.

Daniel wrote nothing down.

For the first time, it was enough that the separation was visible in the actual chronology and therefore existed as more than a methodological demand.

Anyone who later wanted to make one story out of everything would have to actively ignore those differences.

He kept walking.

Outside the window, people crossed the yard carrying coffee cups.

The rain had stopped.
