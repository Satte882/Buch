## 14

At 7:24 p.m., Heller's update came in.

**Still no solid connection. Two offices outstanding.**

Daniel read it on the work phone.

The search field on his monitor was still empty.

It had been empty for thirteen minutes.

During that time he had done nothing new. Only waited, opened updates, tried not to look at the time, and looked anyway.

7:12 p.m.

7:17 p.m.

7:21 p.m.

Berg had not called back.

Lena had sent no new authorization.

And now this from Heller.

Two offices outstanding.

Outstanding was not the same as negative. Daniel knew that.

At the moment, it was equally unusable.

He could have kept waiting.

That was not a theoretical option. He did not need to pretend otherwise.

The regular confirmation could arrive at 7:40, at 8:05, or tomorrow. The plate might turn out to mean nothing.

A rental vehicle belonging to a courier company in Berlin was about as unusual as rain.

Daniel took his hands off the desk and stood.

He had nowhere to go. Even so, he walked to the door, opened it, and looked into the corridor.

At the far end, someone walked past carrying two binders. Jonas was three rooms away on the phone. Nothing in the building had changed simply because Daniel had spent thirteen minutes staring at a search field.

He closed the door again.

Jana's message was still on the private phone.

**Daniel?**

He did not open it.

Not because it was unimportant.

Because he knew what would happen if he connected it to the plate now.

Jana had been watched. Probably.

That did not make B-QV 4172 more dangerous.

Daniel said the sentence quietly, just to hear whether he still believed it.

"Jana doesn't make the plate more dangerous."

The room gave no answer.

He thought of Tobias Ahrens.

Photographs of the loading door. A question about delivery times. A van nearby.

They had waited then.

They had been right.

Ahrens had been harmless.

Daniel remembered Jonas standing in front of the closed case afterward. The man had never known about them. That had been the success.

Then he thought of the security guard from the night before.

Multiple fractures. No danger to life.

That was true too.

Daniel did not reopen the depot timeline. He no longer needed it.

They had found forty minutes of avoidable friction there.

Not five hours.

Not proof that the security guard would have remained unhurt.

Only forty minutes that could have been theirs if they had worked in parallel earlier.

That did not help him now.

Maybe the source had engineered this exact moment. The danger could still be real independently of that.

A real injured victim.

A new warning.

A narrow time window.

A proper review that might not finish before the window began.

And a technically trivial route sitting directly in front of him.

Daniel knew it.

He sat down again.

The permitted-use notice was still at the top of the system.

He did not dismiss it.

He read it.

Not word for word. He knew notices like this too well for that. The records belonged to another case. Access and use were logged. The fact that Daniel could technically view them was not permission to use every hit in a new matter.

He could have built himself a justification out of nothing but true statements: urgent situation, threat to a major public event, two precise source messages, an injured security guard.

None of those statements changed the fact that this specific cross-case use had not been properly authorized.

Daniel opened the current case and looked at the field for case nexus.

If he ran the search, that field would record why he had entered the legacy records.

This was not an invisible step and not a hack. The access would run under his name, his credentials, his timestamp. The visibility of it was almost reassuring.

He picked up the work phone again.

No new message from Berg.

None from Lena.

Heller's last update was four minutes old. Daniel opened it as if the words might have changed.

**Two offices outstanding.**

He could have called Heller again. The answer would have been the same: review in progress. He could have asked Jonas to start the keeper check over from the beginning. The clean routes had not vanished; they were simply exhausted or still open.

Daniel wrote three times on a sheet of paper:

**7:24 – now**

**8:20 – warning window begins**

**9:00 – warning window ends**

Underneath, he wrote:

**No certainty that the window is real.**

That mattered. He was not acting against a clock he knew to be running. He was acting against a deadline set by a manipulative source whose previous warning had been substantially correct.

That was the trap.

If he waited and nothing happened, waiting would be easy to defend afterward.

If he waited and something happened, every minute would suddenly look like a decision.

If he searched and found nothing, the rule violation would still exist.

If he searched and found something, success would not retroactively authorize it.

Daniel turned the paper over.

The calculation did not become cleaner the longer he looked at it.

He could only decide which risk he was going to carry now.

He clicked into the case-nexus field.

The cursor blinked.

Daniel entered the current case number.

Then he stopped.

He could also claim an authorization. A verbal consultation. Some exceptional urgency worded in a way that made it sound as though the problem had disappeared.

He did not.

He did not write that authorization existed.

He did not invent a new justification that made the situation larger than it was.

He linked the current case.

That did not make the use proper.

It only made it visible.

Daniel took his hands off the keyboard again.

Not because he had changed his mind.

Because he wanted to prevent the first boundary crossing from quietly becoming a second one.

He opened the current working note and recorded the status before the search.

**Regular plate review produced no solid connection. Two responses outstanding. Cross-case use currently not authorized.**

Beneath it, he added the time.

7:24 p.m.

The sentence was ugly. That was exactly why Daniel left it there.

If he later had to explain why he searched, no one should be able to think he had discovered the missing case nexus only afterward.

He returned to the search screen. The fields for person, address, and phone remained empty.

The observation of Jana stayed in another note and in his head.

Daniel looked at the empty fields.

Technically, he could have retrieved more.

Precisely for that reason, limiting the search was not some virtue he could use to make the decision feel better. It was merely the smallest form of the violation that could answer his specific question.

Nothing more.

Daniel opened the search form.

Beside the plate field were fields for person, address, phone number, and additional associations.

He looked at them.

Then he hid the expanded fields.

Not necessary.

Not now.

He wanted to know whether this one plate had already appeared in a relevant context.

He did not pull drivers, contacts, households, or movement histories. He did not search for Jana's name either, though the thought appeared briefly.

He pushed it away before it took shape.

Then he entered:

**B-QV 4172**

He limited the query to plate hits and existing vehicle associations.

A selector offered a broader time range. Daniel left it on the narrow default set relevant to the known prior case.

He used only the slice needed to answer his one question instead of opening everything the system could technically provide.

His finger rested on the Enter key for a moment.

He did not think about statutes.

He thought about the question he had asked Jonas in the first case.

What would we need to find if it's harmless?

A rental vehicle that connects to nothing.

Then Heller.

*Still no solid connection. Two offices outstanding.*

Those offices could call back in ten minutes.

Daniel knew that.

He also knew this was not his only option.

That was exactly why it was a decision.

Daniel pressed Enter.

The search took less than a second.

Three hits.

He felt no relief.

Only the immediate loosening in his body that came from suddenly having something in front of him he could test.

The first hit was worthless. Parking violation, more than a year old.

Daniel closed it immediately.

The second came from a protected case unrelated to the current matter. The plate had been captured in the edge of a surveillance photograph.

Daniel opened only the metadata.

Six weeks earlier.

Industrial area in Reinickendorf.

B-QV 4172 had been parked four meters behind a white delivery van.

In that earlier case, the white van had been associated with a person from an Islamist support network.

Daniel stopped.

Not a suspect in the current case.

No proof the drivers knew each other.

No proof the same drivers had even been inside the two vehicles.

Only two vehicles at the same place at the same time.

He did not click through to the person associated with the white van.

The option was there; he left it alone. Daniel opened the third hit. Two days later, on a different street, both vehicles again appeared within the same time window, this time nine minutes apart.

He leaned back.

Coincidence was possible.

The same coincidence twice was worse.

Daniel looked at the time.

7:26 p.m.

He could now have expanded into drivers, contacts, other courier-company vehicles, and people from the old case.

That would have been the logical next search if he treated his first decision as authorization for the second.

He did not.

He took no screenshot of the result list.

He exported nothing.

He wrote down the case references, timestamps, and the association of the white delivery van.

That was all he needed for a regular follow-up check.

Then he closed the legacy records.

A notice appeared in the upper right confirming that his access had been logged.

Of course it had.

Daniel looked at his note.

Three hits: one worthless, two open.

He had not crossed the line to obtain certainty.

Only to turn an open plate into two testable points.

That made the step smaller.

It did not make it proper.

Daniel looked at the time.

7:27 p.m.

Three minutes.

That was how long it had taken to cross a boundary they had been discussing for hours.

His phone rang.

Jonas.

Daniel answered.

"I have a lead," he said.

"From where?"

Daniel looked at the black monitor, where his face reflected faintly back at him.

For the first time since the search, he noticed how tightly he had been clenching his jaw.

He relaxed it.

"I'll explain," he said. "First we find out whether it's worth anything at all."
