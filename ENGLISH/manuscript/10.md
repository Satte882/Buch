## 10

Markus Heller arrived four minutes late and did not apologize.

He set a thin folder on the table, shook Berg's hand, and took the empty chair beside Lena.

"You're Reuter?"

"Yes."

"Heller."

Nothing more.

Daniel knew the name. Coordination, special analysis, interagency threat assessments. One of those people whose name appeared on distribution lists before you ever met them in person.

Before Heller arrived, Berg had asked the same question twice.

"What exactly do you want?"

Daniel had given the same answer both times.

"If the courier passes the item on, I don't want to wait until tomorrow to find out where it went."

Lena had folded her hands on the table.

"That's an objective. Not a measure."

"Access to the service provider's premises and vehicles if the case ends up there."

"On what basis?"

"The source predicted the pickup correctly. Billing party, wrong unit, time window."

"And the service provider?"

"That's where the route leads."

"That makes it the next location. Not the offender."

Berg had said nothing.

Now he slid the documents toward Heller.

"Daniel wants to move faster at the second location."

Heller skimmed the first page.

"What second location?"

"The courier from this afternoon," Daniel said. "The pickup from Warehouse C went to a technical service provider in the south of the city. They work several major events and have temporary access permissions for delivery areas."

"Legitimate permissions?"

"Yes."

"And the contents?"

"Unknown."

"Packaging?"

"Black hard-shell case."

"Size?"

Daniel indicated it roughly with his hands.

Heller did not look impressed.

Tools, measuring equipment, replacement parts—any of it was possible. So was something dangerous.

Heller turned the page.

"Direct link to a threat?"

Daniel pointed to the page with the anonymous message.

"The source predicted the pickup."

"That wasn't my question."

His tone was not sharp. Almost friendly.

Daniel leaned back.

"The pickup was booked under the wrong unit and paid for by the tenant in Unit 17."

"That explains the pickup. Not a planned attack."

"Correct."

Heller looked at Lena.

"What do you want exactly?"

Daniel answered himself.

"Search the service provider's premises and vehicles before anything moves on tonight."

Lena shook her head slightly.

"We don't have enough for that."

Berg looked at her. "Even with the time window?"

"Time pressure doesn't replace the missing link."

"If the tip turns out to be right tomorrow, that won't help us either."

"If it's wrong, we've just searched an ordinary service provider because an anonymous sender told us a story."

Berg said nothing.

Heller turned back to the delivery address.

"Who saw the case?"

"Colleagues on site. Black hard-shell case. Normal size."

"What was inside?"

"Unknown."

"Who accepted it?"

"An employee of the service provider."

"Did he do anything that doesn't fit his job?"

Daniel was silent for a moment.

"No. The case hasn't been opened or passed on."

"Did the source claim the case was dangerous?"

Daniel looked at the message.

"No."

Heller waited a second.

Daniel understood.

He had made the case the center of the story himself because it was the most visible movement out of the warehouse.

"The message only says the delivery is the beginning," he said.

"Exactly."

Daniel liked that Heller did not correct him. He simply left the gap in place.

Heller nodded.

"Then we have an odd booking, an anonymous tip containing genuine advance knowledge, and a legitimate service provider accepting a case."

"Plus the manipulated exterior door at the warehouse."

"Or a door someone propped open."

Daniel looked at him.

"Yes."

Heller put the folder down.

"What is the harmless version if everything you know for certain is still true?"

Daniel did not answer immediately.

Berg looked at him.

Lena did not. She knew the question.

"A retailer orders ordinary goods," Daniel said. "The courier job is assigned incorrectly or routed through the neighboring unit for convenience. Someone props an exterior door open because they don't want to use the access fob every time. A technical service provider accepts a hard-shell case because that's part of its business."

"And the source?" Heller asked.

"Knows internal or semi-internal details."

"Which is a separate problem."

"Yes."

"But not evidence for the story the source builds from them."

Daniel looked at him.

"No."

Heller let the harmless version sit for a moment.

Then he asked, "What would have to happen for you to discard it?"

This time Daniel answered faster.

"A second point that doesn't come from the same chain."

"More specific."

"A vehicle from the service provider's environment appearing somewhere ordinary business doesn't explain. Access that doesn't fit normal activity. A person or place that independently already matters in a relevant case."

"And if the case turns out to contain nothing but equipment?"

"Then the harmless version gets stronger."

"Even though the source knew about the pickup?"

Daniel looked at him.

"Yes."

Heller nodded.

"Good. Then you have a threshold, not just impatience."

Berg leaned back.

"The threshold costs time."

"Of course," Heller said. "Every threshold costs time. Otherwise it wouldn't be one."

He turned the folder back toward Daniel.

"The question isn't whether you want to move quickly. Everyone here does. The question is which false explanation you still have to take seriously before you interfere with someone's rights."

Daniel did not like the sentence.

It was not wrong. What bothered him was that it sounded like something Daniel himself would have said.

The alternative reading was not comfortable. But it held.

That bothered Daniel more than a formal refusal would have.

Heller set the folder down.

"I'm not trying to minimize this. Quite the opposite. If the source genuinely has advance access to details like these, that may be a larger problem than the individual tip."

Berg folded his arms.

"But?"

"No but." Heller looked at Daniel. "The gap is real. If we pretend it isn't, tomorrow we'll have two problems instead of one."

Daniel knew what he meant.

He just did not like that Heller was right.

"What can you run in parallel?" Heller asked.

Daniel named the open points. Company connections. Vehicles. Personnel, as far as legally and operationally justified. Comparison with existing threat information. Queries to the offices responsible for the service provider's event access.

Heller let him finish.

"Which of those requires the source as its basis?"

Daniel went through the list again.

"None of them. Once the service provider is established from the actual courier route."

"Good."

"It only makes things slower."

"Yes."

Heller said it without consolation.

Then he tapped the company connection.

"That can run in parallel. Vehicles too, as far as they follow from the real service-provider link. Event access through the responsible office. Don't wait for one question to be answered before you even ask the next."

Daniel looked at Lena.

She gave a slight nod.

It was not a special route.

Just better organization.

Heller continued.

"I can speed up the cross-state checks. No special measure. Just see whether the same company, address, or vehicle has already appeared somewhere else on a clean basis."

"How fast?" Berg asked.

"If the systems and the people cooperate: today."

"And if they don't?"

"Then tomorrow. As always."

For the first time Heller smiled briefly.

It was neither a likable nor an unlikable smile. Just tired.

Daniel took the folder back.

"If we go into those premises on the current facts," Heller said, "and all we find is ordinary event equipment, what do we explain afterward?"

"That we acted on a concrete threat tip."

"From whom?"

Daniel was silent.

"Exactly."

Heller did not stand up to make the point bigger. He remained seated.

"And if the tip is right and we don't go in tonight?" Berg asked.

Heller looked at him.

"Then later we'll ask ourselves whether we could have moved faster."

"That's not an answer."

"It is. Just not a comfortable one."

Daniel looked at the clock.

Heller noticed.

"Time is a risk," he said. "So is a thin basis. You can't pretend one of them can be argued away."

Daniel asked, "What if we simply keep the case under observation?"

Heller nodded. "Then you may gain a connection. Or lose it. Both belong to the decision."

"So your recommendation?"

"I'm not recommending that you turn my role into an authorization."

Daniel held his gaze.

Heller continued calmly.

"I would run everything that follows independently from the real route in parallel. I would not turn the case into evidence. And if a second solid anchor appears, reassess."

It was almost exactly the answer Daniel himself would have given a younger investigator.

He no longer liked it as much as he would have a week earlier.

Daniel stated the alternative reading again, more briefly this time.

"A retailer orders normal goods. A courier job is assigned incorrectly. An exterior door is propped open for convenience. A service provider accepts a hard-shell case because that's what the business does."

Berg looked at him.

"And a source knows all of it in advance."

"That's why we keep checking."

Heller stood.

"Exactly."

At the door, he stopped once more.

"Reuter."

Daniel looked up.

"If you get a second solid point, call me directly. Not through three distribution lists."

"Why?"

"Because we just spent this meeting talking about time being a problem."

Then he left.

Berg watched him go.

"Unpleasantly reasonable."

Lena gathered her documents.

"The worst kind."

Daniel did not answer.

He was not angry about the decision.

That almost bothered him more.

Heller had not denied him anything Daniel himself could have cleanly defended on the current facts.

He had only named the same boundary Daniel had understood as one of his own strengths a few days earlier.

This time it felt slower.

After Heller left, Daniel opened a new note.

Lena saw him.

"What are you writing?"

"What he actually offered."

"Why?"

Daniel entered three lines.

**Parallel regular checks. Direct coordination route. Reassessment on second anchor.**

Underneath he wrote:

**No additional authority. No authorization to search premises. No claim that the source is trustworthy.**

Lena read over his shoulder.

"You're protecting Heller against your own memory."

"I'm preserving the record."

"Of course."

Daniel let the objection stand.

He knew how quickly later harm could change the memory of an earlier conversation. A man who had named a boundary could later become someone who had forced it on them. A recommendation to parallelize the work could become a refusal to act faster.

Neither would be true.

Jonas came back into the room.

"What's the working rule now?"

Daniel turned the screen toward him.

"Everything supported by the real service-provider link runs in parallel. No measure based on the hard-shell case alone. If a second independent point appears, we decide again."

"And Heller?"

"Coordinates if we need him."

"You trust him?"

Daniel shook his head.

"I have no reason to distrust him."

Lena looked at him.

"Also not the same thing."

"No."

Daniel saved the note.

That did not remove Heller from the story. But it did not make him larger simply because Daniel was angry about lost time.

For Daniel, that mattered more at the moment than it did for Heller.

Daniel drew another line beneath the note.

"What would Heller have had to say for me to treat him as an obstacle?"

Jonas thought about it.

"That we shouldn't run anything in parallel at all."

"He didn't."

"Or that the source is worthless."

"He didn't say that either."

Lena said, "Or that the missing basis wouldn't matter as long as the risk sounded serious enough."

Daniel nodded.

Heller had not said that either.

He had placed two risks beside each other and left Daniel with the decision.

Daniel saved the record with a timestamp.

If his assessment changed later, at least it would remain clear what it had been based on at the time.
