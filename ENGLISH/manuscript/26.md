## 26

"You're holding back information and calling it source review."

Lena stood in the doorway.

Daniel did not look away from the screen.

"How do you know I'm holding anything back?"

"Because I know you."

"That's not a reassuring argument right now."

She came in and closed the door.

Daniel still had the note on the pre-case chronology open. Not in the wider source file. Only in his narrowest working view.

**Private reference predates Warehouse C assignment. Review possible selection point.**

Nothing more.

Lena did not sit down immediately.

"What did you find?"

Daniel looked at the sentence.

The easy reaction would have been to turn the screen toward her. Lena was the person who had challenged his decisions most aggressively over the past few days. She was also the person who still refused to show him part of her own oversight path.

Both were true.

"The private reference in the first message predates my case assignment."

Now she sat down.

"Sure?"

"Yes. The observation at my mother's building was in the evening. Berg assigned me the warehouse complex the following morning."

Lena looked at the monitor Daniel still had not turned toward her.

"And the time?"

"Cross-checked with Jana. No prior involvement in my records; handover list starts the next morning. Verbal pre-assignment isn't ruled out."

Lena nodded.

"So not: someone definitely knew you'd get the case."

"No."

"Someone had you in view beforehand."

"That's as far as I'll go."

Daniel turned the monitor slightly toward her after all.

Lena read the sentence once.

"And now?"

"Now I find out who could have assessed me professionally before the case."

"That wasn't my question."

Daniel looked at her.

"What was?"

"What do you do with the possibility that you were selected, and the information was only the means?"

Daniel leaned back.

"I control the information paths more tightly."

"You're already doing that."

"Then more tightly."

Lena ran a finger along the edge of the table.

"That's control. Not an answer."

"Right now, control is a pretty useful answer."

"For you."

"For the case."

"Maybe."

Daniel noticed his reaction to the word.

"Say it."

"What?"

"You've got something in mind."

Lena exhaled.

"If someone selected you beforehand, maybe it wasn't because you're reckless."

Daniel was silent.

"Because you're credible," she said. "Because you normally take boundaries seriously. Because your decisions carry weight precisely when you cross one anyway."

"That's a very large theory built on one timestamp in an apartment hallway."

"Yes."

"And you have no evidence for it."

"No."

Daniel stood.

It helped. Not much.

He went to the window. Outside, someone pushed a file cart across the yard. A completely ordinary movement. Daniel watched it longer than necessary and was annoyed with himself for doing so.

"We protected people," he said.

"Yes."

The security guard might not have been the last. The wider review group had exposed a real preparatory step. Daniel disputed neither point.

He turned back to her.

"Then don't talk as if every step is just political material."

"I'm not."

"Sounds like it."

"Daniel, the fact that the steps actually work is exactly what makes them institutionally useful."

He said nothing.

Lena took her tablet from her bag and placed it on the table.

"This has been circulating in the project group since yesterday."

Daniel went back and read it.

An internal overview of accelerated cross-case cooperation. His name was not in the heading. His problematic data cross-check was still in the document. Beside it was the later operational success.

The overview did not tell a hero story. It used the case as an example.

**Time lost through separated information environments. Operationally effective countermeasure. Structural action required.**

Daniel read the line twice.

"Berg?"

"Provided it. Heller was on the distribution."

"You?"

Lena looked at him.

"Me too."

Daniel pushed the tablet back.

"That proves nothing about the source."

"I didn't say it did."

"It doesn't prove anyone needed my decision either."

"No."

"Then what?"

Lena did not take the tablet back.

"That your decision didn't stay with you."

Daniel looked at the overview again.

He had known the case was being used internally as a reference. He had not known this form of it.

Berg had assessed the access. The project structure had grown out of it. Of course the case had become a reference point internally.

It still felt different now that Daniel knew someone had been observing him beforehand.

The mechanism might include not only his information, but his reasonableness.

Daniel sat down.

"So what?"

"Boundary."

"Which one?"

"One that doesn't depend on whether the next access works again."

Daniel looked at her.

"You want us to shut down the project structure."

"No."

"Then what?"

"I want you to define when it ends. Before the next situation arrives."

Daniel opened the project note.

The end date was already there.

"It's time-limited."

"A duration isn't the same as a return boundary."

"Yes, it is."

"Not if one sentence is enough on the final day: threat not fully resolved."

Daniel was silent.

Lena tapped the screen.

"Case-specific. Temporary. Independent review. And when the acute case basis is gone, no automatic transition into the next complex."

"When the threat is over."

"When the acute basis is over."

"Those aren't always the same."

"Exactly why I'm saying it."

Daniel stood again.

"And if we still have fragments at the end?"

"Then we justify it again."

"And lose time again."

"Maybe."

"You saw the security guard."

"Yes."

"You know what waiting can cost."

"Yes."

Lena stayed seated.

"And now you know what success can cost too."

Daniel looked at her.

"Weber."

"Among other things."

"The find was still real."

"Yes."

Again she denied him the easy opposing position.

Daniel returned to the desk, left his private note alone, and opened the formal project record.

He wrote:

**Use exclusively within the defined terrorism case complex.**

Underneath:

**No automatic continuation once the acute case-specific basis ends. Continuation requires renewed documented justification and authorization.**

Then:

**Exceptional decisions and expansions will be independently reviewed after the operation; full logging will be retained.**

Lena read along.

"Who's independent?"

Daniel stopped.

"Not me."

"That's a start."

"Not Berg alone."

"Also a start."

Daniel added the responsible oversight function from the existing project structure without entering a person's name.

"And the data?" Lena asked.

"Returned under the defined rules. No silent permanent pool."

"Write it."

Daniel did.

He did not like the tone she was using with him.

That was not an argument against the sentence.

When he finished, he did not send the change only to Lena. Berg received it. So did the defined oversight channel.

The delivery states changed one after another. Berg: read. Oversight function: delivered. Lena: in the room. The boundary was no longer merely Daniel's intention.

"What if I have good reasons tomorrow?" Daniel asked.

"Then you justify them against your own boundary. You don't pretend the boundary never existed."

Daniel nodded.

"Is that a commitment?" Lena asked.

"Yes."

"Even if the structure works again before then?"

Daniel looked at the note. "When the acute basis is gone, we justify continuation again."

Lena nodded once.

Daniel said, "You're acting like control is only my problem."

"No."

"You still haven't fully explained your own path to me."

Lena stopped at the door.

"True."

"And at the same time I'm supposed to accept that you're protecting my autonomy."

"No. You're supposed to accept that I want your decisions to remain reviewable. Whether you trust me while I do that is a different question."

Daniel felt anger.

"Very reassuring."

"That wasn't the goal."

She opened the door.

"Success is not an argument for why you need less oversight right now."

Daniel looked at her.

"I never said it was."

"Good."

This time she did not add *not yet*.

She left.

Daniel remained alone with the project note.

At that moment, he meant every word.
