## 44

Eleven weeks later, it was no longer called a project structure.

It was called an integrated review.

Daniel stopped on the term when he reached page two of the new guidance.

It did not sound threatening at all. Quite the opposite.

It sounded like something you might use to audit a travel-expense claim.

Lena sat two seats away and had already marked five passages.

Berg was turning pages faster.

"This is narrower than what we had in the case."

"In several places," Lena said.

"So that's good."

"I didn't say that."

The presenter put a comparison table on the screen.

It began with concrete changes rather than principles.

**Case nexus:** more narrowly defined.

**Group of persons:** must be justified in advance.

**Expansion:** second approval.

**Hits:** no automatic measure.

**Post-review:** sampling plus mandatory review where consequences burdened individuals.

Daniel read the lines twice.

Only when he turned the page did he notice how tightly he was holding the pen. He loosened his fingers and kept reading.

"Who gives the second approval?"

"Depending on the data category, professional oversight or the designated control office."

"Not the same operational chain?"

"No."

Lena put a check mark beside it.

Berg noticed.

"Another compliment?"

"Not yet."

The presenter continued.

Where hits arose only from old contacts or geographic proximity, a counter-review now had to be explicitly documented before any burdensome measure could follow.

Daniel thought of Nils Weber.

Then Lena.

He said nothing.

"And under time pressure?" Berg asked.

"The review can run in parallel. But any deviation is logged and must be reviewed afterward."

"That costs time. At the wrong moment, that can matter."

"Yes."

The presenter said it so matter-of-factly that Daniel looked up briefly.

No rule without a price.

Not even the new, cleaner version.

That made it more credible.

Berg exhaled.

Daniel kept reading.

Defined threat nexus.

Documented group of persons.

Two-stage approval for certain cross-case linkages.

Logging.

Time-limited use.

Control review.

Some things Daniel had once decided himself could no longer be decided by one person alone.

Other things that had once been imaginable only under heavy justification pressure now stood in the procedure as a regular option.

"The project access has been shut down completely?" Daniel asked.

A presenter at the other end of the table nodded.

"For three weeks. The data sets were returned or blocked according to the rules. This is separate from that."

Daniel nodded.

That mattered.

It did not make the rest any smaller.

"The case is one of several practical examples," the presenter said. "Not the sole basis."

Berg leaned back.

"But the clearest."

She did not answer.

She did not need to.

Daniel turned to the appendix.

There were examples.

Neither his name nor Heller's appeared. Instead, an abstracted case involving delayed linkage of existing information, later time gain through joint analysis, and prevention of major harm.

He recognized every line.

Lena said, "The independent control review is better than before."

Berg smiled. "I'm framing that sentence."

"Then read the next one."

He did.

"The threshold for bringing in an expanded group of persons is too open for me in two places."

"Of course."

"You wanted praise."

"Not from you."

Daniel listened to them.

Three months earlier, this conversation would have started differently.

Three months earlier, it would have begun with the basic question of whether a linkage like this belonged on the table at all, not with how far it could reach.

Now they were discussing who could authorize it.

How long it could run.

How hits were reviewed.

Those were real improvements.

Daniel knew that.

He had argued for some of them himself.

Later, they reached a passage Daniel had not marked.

**An integrated review may be proposed as a regular review option where a sufficient threat nexus exists.**

Not ordered.

Proposed.

Daniel held the pen over the sentence.

"Before the case, this would have said who may request an exception."

The presenter looked at the passage.

"Possibly."

"Now the review itself is the regular option. Only its reach needs additional approval."

"That's the consequence of the reform process."

"I'm not saying it's wrong."

"I didn't take it that way."

Berg asked, "Then what exactly bothers you?"

Daniel thought briefly.

"That the important shift isn't written in a spectacular sentence."

Lena looked at him.

The presenter was already turning the page.

That was exactly the answer.

No one had to celebrate the new capability.

It was enough that no one had to explain anymore why it was on the table at all.

The presenter slid another page toward him.

"We'd like you to remain in the operational advisory group."

Daniel looked up.

"Why?"

Berg laughed briefly.

"Seriously?"

Daniel ignored him.

The presenter said, "Because you know both sides of the instrument."

"That's a kind formulation."

"It's a factual one."

"And if the review of my shooting reaches a different conclusion?"

"Then your role will be reassessed. The proceedings remain separate."

Again the same separation: Heller's manipulation, Daniel's shooting, operational benefit. None canceled the others.

As the meeting was almost over, the presenter returned to the advisory group.

"This isn't an honorary role," she said. "You'll read cases, edge cases, and wrongful burdens. You do not give operational approval."

Daniel nodded.

"How long?"

"Six months initially. New decision after that."

He could have declined.

The shooting was still being reviewed. His own violations had not disappeared. No one needed him in order for the new structure to exist.

That was exactly why withdrawal suddenly seemed too easy.

If he left, he could later say that whatever the instrument became had nothing more to do with him.

That would not be true.

"Six months," Daniel said.

The presenter put a check mark beside his name.

That was all.

Daniel looked at Lena.

"You're staying?"

"For now."

"Why?"

"So Berg doesn't read everything alone."

"Very trust-building," Berg said.

Lena turned the page.

Daniel picked up the pen.

"Me too."

No one made a moment out of it.

The presenter only put a check beside his name.

Then she opened another anonymized edge case from the trial phase.

Not a terrorism case.

A suspicious access to a sensitive technical facility, two conflicting log records, and an employee with a plausible explanation.

An integrated review had been requested and then narrowed again after the first counter-check.

"Outcome?" Daniel asked.

"No substantiated threat nexus. Proceeding closed."

Berg looked at the two pages.

"And why are you showing us this one?"

"Because an instrument can't be evaluated only by its hits."

Lena leaned back.

"How many terminations like this have there been?"

The presenter gave a number.

The number was unspectacular but high enough that Daniel immediately understood how different the later statistics would look if presentations showed only successful linkages.

"Those belong in every evaluation," he said.

"They are included."

Daniel put down his first check mark of his own.

His check was for the requirement to keep even the **decision not to use it** measurable. It was not an endorsement of the integrated review itself.

Then they moved on to the next open formulation.
