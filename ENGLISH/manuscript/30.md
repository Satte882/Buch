## 30

Lena would explain the contacts neither in the corridor nor to Daniel alone.

"Formally," she said. "With a record and the responsible office."

Daniel stood opposite her.

"That takes time."

"Then it takes time."

"You know what's happening right now. You still accept being out until then?"

Lena looked at him.

"I accept that you applied the rule. I don't accept that the oversight path becomes worthless the moment it's inconvenient."

Daniel felt anger at the fact that she was using up his time. Her position was not the problem.

Or maybe the problem was that he experienced it that way.

Jonas came out of the analysis room.

"The delivery-role review is stuck."

Lena turned toward him.

"Why?"

"A question is sitting in your restricted area. I can only see that it's there. I started the formal reassignment."

"How long?"

Jonas lifted his shoulders.

"No idea."

Daniel looked at the time.

The exclusion was no longer an abstract marker. It was producing friction immediately.

"Can you tell me what the question is about?" he asked Lena.

"Not without opening the restricted material."

"You might remember it."

"Maybe."

"Lena."

"That's exactly the point."

She walked past him.

An hour later they were sitting in a small room Daniel normally used for quick coordination.

This time a formal record lay on the table. With it sat someone from the internal data-protection and oversight unit. No name Daniel needed to remember. The function was enough.

The door stayed closed.

The oversight officer did not begin with Lena's contacts.

They began with Daniel's first problematic data access.

Date.

Time.

Data environments used.

Rationale.

Documented follow-up.

Then a second matter.

Then a third.

Daniel recognized his own decisions, only written in language that contained neither justification nor accusation.

"Who prepared this compilation?" he asked.

"Ms. Vogt documented the individual decisions. The oversight assessment comes from our unit."

Daniel looked at Lena.

She said nothing.

The officer turned the page.

"After the first access, Ms. Vogt requested a subject-matter assessment. Not operational authorization. Independent documentation and later reviewability."

"How often?"

"Several times."

"When?"

The times were placed on the table.

Daniel knew them.

They were exactly the windows that had appeared unusual in the broad review.

After his first rule violation.

After an expansion.

After an operational decision that later became part of the project documentation.

The pattern had been real.

Only its meaning had been different.

"Why wasn't that explained in the project file?" Daniel asked.

This time Lena answered.

"Because a confidential oversight contact doesn't become more independent by being entered into the operational project it's supposed to review."

"You could have told me an oversight contact existed."

"I did."

Daniel remembered.

*I know more about the oversight path than you need to know right now.*

At the time, it had sounded evasive.

It had still been evasive.

Only with a reason.

"Did anything from this contact leak into the source channel?" Daniel asked the oversight officer.

"Based on everything we've checked: no."

"Access to the early source contents?"

"Not in a form that would explain your first messages."

"The number of hits from my legacy-record search?"

"This contact doesn't explain it."

"Heller's area?"

The officer shook their head.

"This review doesn't allow me to say anything about that."

Daniel nodded.

No new solution.

One fewer old hypothesis.

Lena was not the leak.

Daniel did not put that sentence into the normal source file. He corrected only what belonged there: the unusual contact path had been formally explained and provided no indication of information leakage.

Then he opened the security note that had triggered her temporary restriction.

The old entry remained in the history.

Beside it came the correction.

Not deleted. Refuted.

Lena stood behind him.

"Better than Weber," Daniel said.

"What?"

"We're correcting the origin everywhere we control."

"Yes."

"And you were still out for four hours."

"Also yes."

Daniel looked at the two timestamps.

Exclusion.

Restoration.

Between them were reassigned tasks, a delayed query, and an interim assessment that had needed correction because her independent check was missing.

"I can't reset that," he said.

Lena took her coffee.

"That's the point of temporary."

"That it isn't temporary?"

"The decision can be temporary. The effect can't."

Daniel nodded.

That did not settle the apology.

It only made the apology smaller than what he wanted to apologize for.

Her timing pattern had been real.

His interpretation had not.

Daniel looked at the list of contacts.

He noticed he felt relieved.

He did not like that.

With Weber, he had felt no relief. Only correction.

With Lena, he had been looking for a harmless explanation from the beginning.

And it had been right.

That did not make his reflex cleaner.

Daniel opened Weber's old hit assessment beside it.

He did not want to equate Lena with Weber in any way. Precisely that would have been wrong.

He was only looking at the places where he had documented alternative explanations.

With Weber, they had been there early:

**Work phone shared. Shift swap possible. Badge assignment open.**

With Lena, Daniel had produced almost the same kind of sentences verbally in the first few minutes, only faster and with more conviction.

**Oversight contact. Confidential path. Legitimate documentation.**

The second list had been right.

That did not make the difference in how he had weighted them objective.

Daniel placed the same sentence beneath both cases:

**Personal proximity does not change the evidentiary weight of a counterhypothesis.**

Lena saw it later.

"Nice sentence."

"Too nice?"

"No. Just harder than it looks."

Daniel nodded.

At least now he knew where personal knowledge could be relevant professional context and where it could become preference.

The boundary between the two remained messy.

But it was no longer invisible.

"Can the restriction be lifted?" Berg asked, having joined them for the final part.

"From the perspective of this oversight review, there's no reason it can't," the officer said.

"Then let's do that."

"Not immediately," Lena said.

Berg looked at her.

"Why not?"

"Because the formal restoration should be documented as carefully as the exclusion was."

"You want to stay out longer?"

"No. I don't want you to become less careful with the correction than you were with the suspicion."

Jonas, standing at the door, lowered his eyes.

Daniel could not tell whether he was hiding a smile.

Berg exhaled.

"Then carefully."

Restoring access took another forty minutes.

Forty minutes in which Lena could not reach the sensitive project material.

Jonas had to take over two of her tasks. One question was passed to another role. An interim assessment went into the operational picture without Lena's independent check and came back ten minutes later with a correction she probably would have caught immediately.

No drama.

Only cost.

When the authorization reappeared, Lena was sitting by the coffee machine.

She pressed the button and did not remove the cup.

Daniel stopped beside her.

"You're back in. The internal assessment against you is being corrected."

"I know."

"I'm sorry."

Lena looked at the machine.

"For what?"

"That you were out."

"That was the consequence of your rule."

"The anomaly was real."

"Yes."

"I couldn't exempt you because I know you."

"I didn't ask you to."

Daniel looked at her.

"Then what?"

Lena finally took the cup out.

"Don't pretend *temporary* is a neutral word for the person it hits."

Daniel was silent.

"I was out of a case I'd been working for days for four hours," she said. "Other people had to take over my work. My name was in a security note. That will be corrected. It still happened."

"I know."

"No. Now you know."

She walked toward the elevator.

Daniel did not follow.

He returned to the analysis room and opened the task history covering the four hours Lena had been out.

He called it follow-up, not guilt.

Three items had been reassigned. On one, Jonas had to ask the same question twice because an old oversight note was missing. A second sat open for ten minutes until Berg reassigned responsibility. On the third, the situation picture needed a correction after Lena regained access.

None of it had endangered anyone.

None of it was dramatic.

It was simply work created because, for a few hours, her name had sat in the same category as the other unexplained hits.

Daniel opened the security note.

Beside the original restriction was now the correction:

**Contact pattern explained by legitimate oversight activity. No confirmed information leakage. Restriction lifted.**

The old entry had not disappeared.

It remained above it, with time and author.

Daniel knew why. A corrected case could not look as though the first decision had never existed.

That was exactly what he had wanted with Weber: traceable correction instead of quiet deletion.

With Lena, the same cleanliness suddenly felt like a mark that would remain readable forever.

He closed the note again.

When Lena reappeared in the room, it went quieter for a moment than necessary.

Barely noticeable; only two conversations ended a second too early.

Jonas pushed the tasks he had taken over back toward her.

"This one's done. This one's open. And I probably sorted this one differently than you would have."

Lena sat down.

"Then I'll look at it."

No comment about being back.

No small welcome gesture.

Daniel was grateful for that and found even the gratitude uncomfortable.

The correction had restored her access. It could not restore the fact that everyone in the room had spent four hours seeing her name beside an unexplained contact pattern.

Jonas looked up from his screen.

"Everything okay?"

Daniel thought of the question Jonas now asked too often.

"No."

"Because of Lena?"

"Because of the rule."

Jonas waited.

Daniel corrected himself.

"No. Because of what the rule does when it lands on someone."

"Isn't that the same thing?"

"Not quite."

The rule had not lied. The contact pattern had been real.

It simply had not known what the pattern meant.

People had supplied that meaning.

Daniel included.

New hits from the same broad review were waiting on his phone.

He opened them.

Lena's objection had not been wrong. Her innocence said nothing about whether the other hits were wrong too.

That separation was beginning to feel dangerously reasonable.
