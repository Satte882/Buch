## 27

The next message came that afternoon.

No greeting.

No reference to what had happened before.

**The next stage runs through tomorrow's setup. A new delivery authorization was added today. Don't check only the name. Check the access around it.**

Underneath was an identifier from the event logistics system.

Then a blank line.

**6:32 p.m. Your sister left the practice through the rear exit.**

Daniel stood before he knew why.

The chair rolled into the cabinet behind him.

Jonas looked up from his desk.

"What?"

Daniel held the phone tightly.

6:32 p.m.

It was 6:47.

Fifteen minutes.

"Check the delivery authorization."

"Which one?"

Daniel gave him the identifier.

"Source?"

"Yes."

Jonas was already typing.

Daniel opened Jana's contact.

His thumb stayed over the call icon.

He did not call.

Not yet.

The fact that someone knew which exit Jana had used proved exactly one thing: someone knew it.

Daniel wrote the sentence on paper.

On a separate sheet, outside the source file and away from the delivery identifier.

Underneath:

**Private knowledge increases pressure, not threat evidence.**

Daniel drew another line beneath it. He assigned no urgency level beside Jana's name.

He could have used it as an argument without effort. Fifteen-minute-old observation. His sister. Unknown source. Everyone in the room would have understood why it made him want to move faster.

That was exactly the problem.

If the delivery authorization was dangerous, it had to be dangerous without Jana. If it was harmless, his fear could not replace the missing second anchor.

He slid the sheet where Jonas could see it.

The separation should not exist only in Daniel's head.

His handwriting remained neat. His body did not cooperate. His neck was rigid, his mouth dry, and while Jonas checked the delivery authorization Daniel had to stop himself twice from calling Jana before he even knew what he could tell her.

"You're faster right now," Jonas said.

Daniel looked up.

"What?"

"With the questions. Normally you let me finish one status before you start the next."

Daniel looked at the sheet.

Jonas was right.

"Then slower," he said.

"Because of Jana?"

"Yes."

Jonas simply nodded.

Daniel liked that he did not turn it into a psychological scene.

"Operationally, she doesn't count," Daniel said.

"Understood."

"If I pull her into a justification anyway, tell me."

"I will."

The pressure did not disappear.

It simply had a witness now, someone who could stop Daniel from quietly turning it into evidence.

"Authorization exists," Jonas said.

Daniel moved to his screen.

"Since when?"

"Created today at 4:08 p.m."

"For whom?"

"New delivery service for setup."

"Created only, or approved?"

Jonas opened the next view.

"Formally approved. 4:41 p.m."

"By whom?"

"Event logistics. Two approval steps."

"Why new?"

"The existing provider handed off part of tomorrow's route. It's in the logistics note."

Daniel read it himself.

Capacity bottleneck. Replacement service provider. Driver lists partly to follow.

A plausible explanation.

Not even unusual.

"Company connection?"

"Pulling it."

Jonas opened the existing project data. The delivery service had not appeared before. No direct connection to Mika. No known link to the storage unit they had most recently found.

"So all that's confirmed right now is that the identifier is real," Jonas said.

"And new."

"Yes."

"Nothing more."

"Nothing more."

Daniel showed him the message down to the blank line.

Jonas read it.

"'Access around it' is pretty convenient."

"How convenient?"

"Could mean drivers. Shift planners. Access badges. People who can see the driver lists. People who approve the delivery area. Or basically anyone working anywhere near it."

"So not a criterion."

"Not yet."

Jonas marked the identifier **confirmed** in the working view and its significance **open**.

Daniel now showed him the final line too.

Jonas read it and said nothing at first.

"That's current?"

"Fifteen minutes old."

"Shit."

"Yes."

"You calling her?"

"In a minute."

"Why in a minute?"

Daniel looked at him.

"Because I want to know what I can actually tell her first."

"And if she's on the road right now?"

The objection was good enough that Daniel disliked it.

He still did not call yet.

"First check whether the delivery authorization appears in any other current strand."

Jonas turned back to the screen.

Lena came in carrying a printout.

"What is it?"

Daniel gave her only the factual part first.

She read the message down to the blank line.

"Identifier confirmed?"

"Yes. Significance open. Company unremarkable so far. Time window only says tomorrow's setup, no exact time."

Lena looked at him.

"What are you leaving out?"

"Why does something always have to be missing?"

"Because you're standing again."

He was still standing. Only then did he notice.

He showed her the final line.

Lena read it once.

Her face barely changed.

"Jana is not evidence for the setup."

"I know."

"Then don't treat her like a hit in the same column."

Daniel pointed to his note.

**Private knowledge does not confirm the threat hypothesis.**

"I'm not."

"Good."

"Does that make it feel better now?"

"No."

Daniel finally sat down.

Jonas said, "I have a second factual point."

They turned toward him.

"The driver lists still aren't complete. Two names are missing even though the access authorizations are supposed to become active tomorrow morning."

"Unusual?" Lena asked.

"Bad practice. But logistics set a deadline for late submission by tonight."

"So a process gap, not yet a threat connection."

"Exactly."

Daniel looked at the time.

6:54 p.m.

The sentence about Jana was now twenty-two minutes old.

Berg arrived with his jacket still over one arm.

"What do we have?"

Daniel explained the delivery authorization first. Identifier genuine. Replacement provider real. Driver lists incomplete. No confirmed offense connection.

Then the line about Jana.

Berg stopped at the door.

"Can you reach her?"

"Haven't called yet."

Berg looked at him.

"Why?"

Daniel felt irritation because two people had now asked the same good question.

"Because I don't want to enter the call with an instruction before I know what we actually have."

Berg nodded once.

"Then what can we properly check before tomorrow morning?"

Lena answered first.

"Delivery service within the existing project context. Existing access roles. Submitted drivers. Known contact overlaps. No new broad collection just because of the source wording."

Jonas said, "And we can break 'access around it' into concrete testable categories. Otherwise we end up checking everything."

Berg looked at Daniel.

"How much time?"

"Setup starts early. We don't have a precise threat time."

"The operational question is what we can rule out tonight without being blind tomorrow," Berg said. "The source's credibility comes second."

Daniel nodded.

That was Berg at his best. No theory about manipulation. No demand for maximum reach. Only the question of which options needed to remain open.

Lena tapped Daniel's project note, the one he had changed that morning.

"And your boundary?"

"Still stands."

"Then it applies now."

"Yes."

"Even with Jana in the message."

Daniel looked at her.

"Jana isn't evidence."

"That wasn't the question."

Daniel was silent briefly.

"Yes. Even now."

Jonas created three columns.

**Delivery authorization. Access context. Private knowledge.**

Under the third he wrote only: **handle separately**.

Daniel looked at it and left it there.

Then they began the narrow review.

The replacement provider was real. Contract genuine. Contact reachable. Two of the already submitted drivers had ordinary prior work at other events. Nothing unusual.

One access role in the logistics system had been expanded that afternoon. The reason was documented: the responsible employee was covering for a sick colleague.

Another hit initially looked like an unusual overnight permission. It was a standard authorization for setup phases.

Everything explainable.

Daniel took a blank sheet and drew a line down the middle.

On the left he wrote:

**Real threat + pressure tactic.**

On the right:

**Real fragments + deliberate steering.**

Jonas looked at it.

"Those are almost the same thing."

"For the first review steps, yes."

"And later?"

"Later, what holds independently decides."

Lena stood beside the desk.

"And what about the version where the delivery authorization is completely harmless and the Jana line exists only to make you stop treating it as harmless?"

Daniel added a third line beneath the right side:

**Harmless factual point + psychological pressure.**

He did not like the line.

It stayed anyway.

Jonas pointed to the first column. "What would make that bigger?"

"A second current anchor. Not from the source."

"And what would make it smaller?"

"A clean explanation for the open access roles and drivers."

Lena said, "Then at least we have criteria before we start inventing them."

Daniel looked at the message again.

The source had named no specific person. No vehicle. No time for an offense. Only a genuine new identifier and wording that could widen the view almost indefinitely.

**Check the access around it.**

The longer Daniel looked at the sentence, the less information it contained.

And the more action could be derived from it.

That was new enough to bother him.

In the working view, he marked both what had been confirmed and what the source had **not** supplied: no concrete threat time, no named person, no independent indication that the delivery authorization had been manipulated.

Berg read the addition and nodded.

"Good. Then investigate the gap, not the tone of the message."

Then came the first small irregularity.

One of the two missing driver names had been added, but the listed mobile number, according to the available records, had not belonged to him for months.

"Could be sloppy admin," Jonas said.

"Yes."

"Check?"

"Narrowly."

The query to the service provider went out.

Berg looked at the time.

"And if we have nothing by midnight?"

No one answered immediately.

Daniel thought of the four paragraphs in his self-imposed return boundary.

No automatic expansion.

New justification.

Documented review.

Then he thought of Jana at the rear exit.

His mind tried to pull both into the same calculation.

He forced them apart again.

"Then we decide with what we have," he said.

"What does that mean concretely?" Berg asked.

"If the narrow checks leave a real gap and an independent anchor appears, we put the broader option back on the table. Not before."

Lena looked at him.

"That's the boundary."

"Yes."

Jonas said, "And Jana?"

Daniel picked up his phone.

"That's a different decision."

He stood.

This time at least he knew he was standing.

He went into the empty corridor and called his sister.
