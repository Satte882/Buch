## 12

"If you stare at the clock long enough, it won't slow down."

Jonas stood in Daniel's doorway.

It was after midnight.

Daniel had opened the timeline again.

This time he was using a different view than earlier that evening. On the left were the facts they had actually had at each point in time.

On the right, separated from them, everything they had learned only later.

"I'm not looking at the clock."

"You're looking at times."

"That's different."

Jonas came in and set two cups on the desk.

Daniel took one.

The coffee was too hot and too weak.

"What do we have?" Jonas asked.

Daniel turned the monitor toward him.

"Two places where we lost time without the legal situation forcing us to."

Jonas leaned forward.

Daniel pointed to the first.

"We waited for the landlord's response before checking the service provider in parallel."

"Because we didn't know whether the tip was real."

"We could still have checked the public registry and contract information."

"Would that have given us the blue van?"

"Probably not."

Jonas looked at him.

"Then why mark it?"

"Because it was still avoidable delay."

Daniel pointed to the second.

"The query on event access went through two offices one after the other."

"Because the first office was responsible."

"And the second had the data."

"Would parallel requests have prevented what happened at the depot?"

Daniel looked at the timestamps.

"I don't know."

"So maybe nothing would have changed."

"Correct."

Jonas took a sip and grimaced.

"This coffee should be illegal."

"Almost."

Daniel marked both points with a different symbol from the actual legal limits.

Not *mistake*.

**Friction.**

Beside **Friction**, Daniel added a second column.

**Visible at the time?**

Jonas read the heading.

"Now we're really having fun."

Daniel ignored him.

For the first point, he wrote **yes**. The public company check could have run in parallel with the landlord inquiry without requiring any new assumption.

For the second, he also wrote **yes**. They had already known the event data sat with another office; they had simply followed the chain sequentially.

Then Daniel came to a point he had marked in red earlier that evening: their failure to focus sooner on the technical service provider.

He stopped.

"What?" Jonas asked.

"We didn't know that then."

"We knew the courier route."

"We knew there was a legitimate recipient and a case."

Daniel removed the red marker.

"Knowing what happened at the depot makes it look more inevitable in hindsight than it was."

Jonas nodded slowly.

Daniel wrote **no** in the second column.

That made the review less satisfying.

Two organizational delays remained. Other supposed failures disappeared as soon as he took away the knowledge they had gained afterward.

That was exactly how Daniel wanted it.

If he took a bad outcome and built a past in which the right decision had supposedly been obvious all along, he would learn nothing.

At the bottom of the page, he wrote:

**Change only what could already have been organized differently with the information available at the time.**

"What's the difference?" Jonas asked.

"A mistake means we made the wrong decision then."

"And friction?"

"That we could have organized the same defensible path faster."

Jonas nodded.

Daniel opened a new list.

Event-coordination contact.

Direct landlord contact.

Readiness for rapid vehicle and company checks within the permitted scope.

No special authority. No shortcuts that required a different legal basis.

Only routes they would not have to rediscover every time.

Beside each item, he added an owner.

None of them belonged to Daniel alone. That mattered to him.

Then he took the previous day's sequence and ran it again with the new order.

Pickup confirmed.

In parallel: check service provider through open sources.

In parallel: contact event coordination.

In parallel: check known vehicles arising from the real service-provider connection.

"How much do we gain?" Jonas asked.

Daniel shifted the timestamps.

"If everyone responds the way they did yesterday? Maybe forty minutes."

"Not five hours."

"No."

"Forty minutes might not have protected the security guard either."

"Correct."

Daniel did not delete the simulation.

"Then the result is still forty minutes."

Jonas looked at him.

"You're not looking for blame."

"No."

"Then what?"

"Time we don't have to waste again."

"And the next tip?"

"What about it?"

"How certain does it have to be?"

Daniel held the cup in both hands.

A week earlier, he would have had an answer.

Not a percentage. Not a formula. But at least a standard.

Now he thought of Ahrens from the first case. Event technician. Photographs at the loading door. Harmless.

Then of the security guard in the hospital.

Both stories were true.

"As certain as it needs to be," he said.

Jonas looked at him.

"That's not an answer."

"I know."

"Yesterday you would've given me one."

Daniel looked at the screen.

"Yesterday I would've said: first enough basis, then act."

"And today?"

"Today I say we need to start building that basis earlier."

"That's still not the same question."

Daniel knew.

"No."

Jonas sat down across from him.

"What if the next source gets one thing right and another thing wrong again?"

Daniel pulled up the three-column structure from Lena's sheet as a digital template.

Confirmed.

Open.

Refuted.

"Then we split it apart immediately."

"We do that now too."

"Too late. First we built a story, then separated the pieces."

Jonas looked at the line about the gray van.

"True."

Daniel added a fourth field beside it.

**What can we verify without already believing the claim?**

Jonas read it.

"That's going to get long."

"It shouldn't. One sentence."

"And who fills it in?"

"Whoever takes the tip."

"So usually me."

"Then write clearly."

Jonas shook his head, but he smiled.

Lena was suddenly standing behind him.

"Good."

Jonas turned around.

"How long have you been standing there?"

"Long enough to smell the coffee."

She came in and set a printout on the desk.

"The blue van was rented properly. False contact details, genuine payment. Still no solid link to Unit 17."

Daniel took the sheet.

"Security guard?"

"Staying overnight. Prognosis is good."

Daniel nodded.

Lena looked at the timeline.

"You're already building the next case."

"I'm removing avoidable delay."

"That's not the same thing."

"I didn't say it was."

She pointed to the two columns separating what had been known then from what had become known later.

"You know what hindsight does."

"Yes."

"Then tell me what you would have decided differently this afternoon if all you had was what you actually knew then."

Daniel looked at the left-hand column.

Pickup confirmed.

Service provider real.

Case contents unknown.

Source informed, motivation open.

No direct link to the claimed attack scenario.

He did not need a long answer.

"I still wouldn't have supported entering the premises."

Jonas looked at him.

Lena nodded.

"Good."

"But I would've started the parallel checks earlier."

"That's legitimate."

"And with a new comparable tip, I'd prepare faster even while its significance was still open."

Lena looked at him for a moment longer.

"Preparing isn't intervening."

"I didn't say it was."

"Not yet."

Daniel leaned back.

"The decision this afternoon was defensible on the information we had then."

"Yes," Lena said.

"The outcome doesn't make it retroactively bad. And I still change the process."

"I don't object to that."

"You sound like there's more."

"There is."

Lena tapped the new three-column template.

"If next time you start weighting uncertainty toward action more quickly, don't call it process improvement."

Daniel looked at her.

"What should I call it?"

"A decision."

She said it without accusation.

That was exactly why it stayed with him.

Jonas picked up his cup.

"Reassuring."

"I wasn't talking to you."

"Even more reassuring."

Lena left.

Jonas remained in the doorway.

"And if the next source is only half right again?"

Daniel looked at him.

"Then we need to find out which half faster."

Jonas nodded and left too.

Daniel stayed alone.

He moved two review paths into parallel.

Then he created a simple rule for new source tips:

**Immediately separate operational claims into: confirmed / open / refuted.**

Under it, he wrote:

**Do not confuse the source with the claim.**

He read both lines.

They looked like order.

In reality, they also meant he wanted to become capable of acting earlier next time.

Daniel put the cursor beneath them again.

Capable of acting earlier could not mean convinced earlier.

He added three limits.

**No intervention based solely on a source claim.**

**No expansion of data access merely because time is short.**

**Refuted parts remain refuted even if another part later proves true.**

He read the sentences.

This was not new authority. Not even a new threshold. It was an attempt to shorten the time between receiving a tip and testing it properly without prejudging the result.

Daniel turned the three fields into a small template. Beside every open point, there now had to be one sentence stating what could already be checked and what explicitly must not follow from it yet.

The template would detain no one, authorize no access, and shift no purpose limitation.

It would only prevent a testable question from sitting idle until some other question had been answered.

Daniel looked at the depot timeline.

That was exactly the kind of lost time he did not want to give away again.

For that night, he had allowed himself no more than that.

He was about to lock the computer when the private phone beside him vibrated.

This time he looked at it immediately.
