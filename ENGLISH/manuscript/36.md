## 36

Berg dropped out of the simple story first.

The suspicion remained; only Berg dropped out of the simple story.

"He sold your case upward," Lena said. "We know that."

Daniel scrolled through the records.

"He kept me on the case."

"We know that too."

"He knew about the new project structure before I knew how widely my case was already being used as an example."

"Yes."

"But?"

Lena looked at him.

"You hate that word right now."

"A little."

"Berg doesn't explain the apartment hallway."

Daniel said nothing.

That was true.

Berg had assigned the warehouse complex only the morning after Daniel's visit to his mother. The early observation did not fit his later institutional use of the case.

Lena did not explain it either.

Her confidential oversight contacts began only after Daniel's first problematic data access.

Months too late for selection before the case.

That left several technical and coordinating paths.

And Markus Heller.

Daniel liked how ordinary the name looked on the list.

No red marker.

No warning.

Just the same typeface as everyone else.

Jonas came in with two printouts.

"I've got something old."

"How old?" Daniel asked.

"Eleven months."

He put the first printout on the table.

An internal staffing list for a cross-state scenario exercise. Daniel had participated in two sessions and then forgotten about it.

Several names had brief professional assessments beside them.

Beside Daniel:

**reliable in ambiguous situations; high operational/legal acceptance; does not tend toward premature escalation.**

Daniel read the sentence twice.

"Who wrote that?"

"Don't know. Consolidated version."

"Then it doesn't help."

Jonas slid the second printout over.

A forwarded copy of the consolidated version.

From Heller's project area.

Below it, a short note: **Keep Reuter in mind for future complex situation formats.**

No reason.

No secret assessment.

A sentence that could have appeared in a hundred internal emails.

Daniel pushed the page back.

"That proves only that he knew me."

"Yes."

"Which we basically knew already."

"Not that early."

Lena picked up the printout.

"And not with that exact assessment."

Daniel looked at her.

"Which assessment?"

"That you don't escalate prematurely."

"That was positive then."

"Still is."

He leaned back.

That was exactly why he disliked the direction.

An obvious hardliner did not need real harm to argue for broader authority.

Daniel did.

Or someone who understood how Daniel thought under real pressure did.

"No," he said.

Jonas frowned. "No what?"

"We're not turning an old project email into motive."

"I'm not."

"Good."

Lena put the page aside.

"Then the other thing."

Daniel looked at her.

"What other thing?"

Jonas opened a file.

"The preliminary check."

Daniel felt something tighten in his back.

There was no person's name in the heading.

No file about him.

It was a small support request from a project context. Broadly worded. A check of reachability and external availability of possible roles for a later operational phase.

Several people were named.

Daniel among them.

"Is this normal?" he asked.

Lena answered first.

"Not like this."

"Why?"

"Because an organizational preliminary check is not a license for covert everyday-life checking."

Jonas scrolled down.

The execution was documented briefly.

No prolonged surveillance.

Two external contact points. Workplace. Residential environment derived from a previously known private contact address.

Then a time.

8:11 p.m.

Daniel did not move.

Jonas stopped scrolling.

"That's the time from the first message," he said quietly.

Daniel knew.

He did not need the sentence.

The short note stated that Daniel had entered the building and later left it again. His name had been confirmed at the entrance through the mailbox label.

Nothing more.

Daniel saw the apartment hallway again.

Jana with the binder.

The plastic strip sitting crooked.

His thumb pressing it back into place.

At the time, he had checked, found nothing solid, and let it go.

Correct, he thought.

Still correct now.

The significance had not been visible then.

"Who initiated the request?"

Jonas pointed to the origin.

A project account from Heller's working area.

Heller's personal name was not attached.

Daniel stared at it.

"Who could use the account?"

"Several people."

Daniel mentally removed the point from the evidence column.

"Who provided the list of people?"

Jonas clicked back one level.

That narrowed it.

A project template had gone through Heller's office. Daniel was already on it.

Daniel was neither alone nor highlighted, but he was there early.

Very early.

Lena said, "Now put together everything that actually holds."

Daniel looked at her.

"I thought that's exactly what I'm not supposed to do."

"Not everything you like. Everything that carries weight."

He stood and walked toward the door.

He did not want to leave. He simply did not want to sit anymore.

Heller had early access to fragmented situation information.

His working area had processed the plate before the first real harm.

He had known Daniel professionally beforehand.

His project environment had initiated an improper short external check whose timestamp later appeared in the first anonymous message.

And Heller sat exactly where Daniel's later successes were now becoming arguments for new working methods.

Lena had been close to Daniel's exceptional decisions.

Berg had carried them upward.

Heller connected both to what came before.

Daniel took a sheet and drew three vertical lines.

"Early information access," he said.

He wrote it over the first column.

"Knowledge of me before the case."

Second column.

"Proximity to what my decisions later became."

Third column.

Jonas looked at the sheet.

"That's suspiciously tidy now."

"Then break it."

They started with Lena.

Late knowledge of exceptional decisions: yes.

Oversight access: yes.

Pre-case observation: no.

Early access to the first fragments: only partial.

Daniel did not cross out her name. He wrote only: **does not explain before**.

Berg.

Institutional use: clear.

Knowledge of Daniel's working style: yes.

Apartment hallway before case assignment: did not fit in time.

Daniel wrote: **explains effect, not selection**.

Then Heller.

Jonas placed nothing new on the table. They used only what already carried weight.

Special analysis.

Old professional assessment.

Project environment of the preliminary check.

Proximity to coordination and later institutional use.

Daniel did not place the points faster just because they fit.

"That's still a role description," he said.

"Yes," Lena said.

"Someone in that role inevitably appears in many places."

"Yes."

"That's the harmless explanation."

"For every individual point."

Daniel looked at the three columns.

The combination was still not secured.

He did not write Heller's name in as the answer.

Not yet.

Instead he placed the preliminary-check note beside it.

8:11 p.m.

Beside that, the later first message.

The same detail.

Jonas said, "The project account isn't a personal login."

"I know."

"And the template going through Heller's office doesn't mean he initiated every operational step himself."

"I know."

Daniel heard how sharp the second *I know* sounded.

He forced his shoulders to loosen.

"Keep going."

Jonas did.

Several people could have carried out individual pieces. An organizational-sounding preliminary check did not require the people executing it to know any larger motive. The improper purpose did not have to be written in any file.

That did not make Heller safer.

It made the trace less cinematic.

And therefore more plausible.

Daniel stayed by the door.

"Why you?" Jonas asked.

Daniel turned.

Jonas immediately raised his hands.

"Sorry. Why you?"

Daniel looked at the old assessment.

**does not tend toward premature escalation.**

He thought of Ahrens.

The first man his restraint had protected.

The conversation with Heller when there had not been enough basis.

Heller had not pressured him then.

He had affirmed Daniel's boundary.

Unpleasantly reasonable, Berg had called him.

Daniel went back to the table.

"Because it would be worth less with someone else."

Lena did not answer.

"What?" Jonas asked.

Daniel picked up the project document containing his later successes.

"If Berg wants everything broader from the start, that's Berg."

"Thanks," Jonas said dryly.

Daniel barely noticed.

"If someone who already thinks every boundary is an obstacle asks for an exception, that convinces no one who opposed it beforehand."

Lena looked at him.

"And if you ask for it?"

Daniel pushed the project document away.

"Then it looks reasonable."

Daniel looked again at the old assessment: no premature escalation, high operational and legal acceptance.

He had spent years building exactly that kind of trust without ever calling it that.

Ahrens.

A suspicion that had not been enough.

Daniel had waited.

The man had been innocent.

At the time, the rule had simply been work to him. He had not seen cowardice in it.

Later, the same Daniel had crossed a boundary.

Not because he despised rules. Because real harm had changed his weighting.

And precisely because of that, his move meant something different outwardly from the demand of a man who had wanted broader access all along.

Daniel felt the thought physically before he could phrase it cleanly.

His credibility had not only been protection.

It was usable.

"If that's true," Jonas said carefully, "then you weren't interesting despite working that way."

Daniel looked at him.

Jonas stopped.

"Never mind."

"No. Say it."

"Then you were interesting because of it."

Daniel put the project document with his later successes beside the old assessment.

Two documents that were not sinister on their own.

That was precisely what bothered him.

He did not need a master plan with his name on it.

It was enough if someone had understood how valuable an exception became once it came from a man who normally did not want one.

Daniel pushed the two pages apart.

"My decisions remain mine."

Lena nodded.

"Yes."

"Even if he counted on them."

"Yes."

That was not exoneration.

It was worse.

If Heller had merely built conditions under which Daniel himself made reasonable decisions, without steering him like a puppet, then Daniel could hand none of those decisions back to him.

That was true for Lena, Weber, and the refusal to scale back alike.

The decisions remained Daniel's.

Heller only needed them to become useful.

The sentence stayed between them.

Daniel did not like it.

Because it was true without absolving him.

Daniel himself had checked B-QV 4172 against the protected legacy records. He had accepted the broader project logic. He had removed Lena from the information flow.

No one had forced him to do any of it.

And it had worked.

That was exactly why the decisions were useful.

"Where is Heller?" Daniel asked.

Jonas reached for the phone.

Lena said, "Slow down."

"No."

"Daniel."

"I'm not confronting him."

She studied him.

"Then what?"

"Locate him. Preserve his current access. Don't touch anything that warns him before we know what we can formally support."

"And if he's the source?"

Daniel looked at the time 8:11 p.m.

"Then he probably knows before we do if we start searching for him openly."

Jonas already had the phone to his ear.

After a few seconds he shook his head.

"Not in the office."

"Where?"

"Situation coordination. At least according to the calendar."

Daniel gathered the documents from the table.

"Small circle. Berg only gets what he needs to preserve the situation. No big group."

Lena stood.

"Daniel."

He looked at her.

"Highly likely isn't proved."

"I know."

"Don't just say it."

Daniel put the old project email into the folder.

"That's why I'm not going to him."

He added the documents in which his later successes were now being used as reference points.

The documents did not prove Heller was the source. Daniel took them because he did not want the case to become too small again.
