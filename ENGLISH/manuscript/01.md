# 1

"If we're wrong, he'll never know."

Jonas Keller said it as if that were the best part of his proposal.

Daniel kept looking at the screen.

A narrow-faced man. Thirty-three years old. Event technician. Seven years with the same company. No criminal record. No current proceedings. In the second photo he wore a black work jacket and held a cell phone up in front of a roll-up gate.

The image had been cropped from a surveillance camera. Bad angle, too much contrast. Enlarged, Ahrens's face looked harder than it did in the ID photo beside it.

Daniel returned the image to normal size.

"You don't know that," Daniel said.

"We wouldn't arrest him."

"I didn't say we would."

Jonas pulled his chair closer to the table. He had been on the team for eight months and still hadn't broken the habit of leaning forward whenever he thought something was finally becoming concrete.

"If a patrol happens to see him anyway, it's a routine stop."

"If a patrol happens to see him."

"Or we flag the van for a check."

"Then it isn't routine anymore."

Jonas edged the chair closer again. "For Ahrens it makes no difference if there's nothing there."

Daniel looked at him now.

Jonas immediately raised one hand. "Okay. For us it does."

Four points were listed on the large monitor on the wall.

Tobias Ahrens had photographed the delivery area of a federal government building on two separate days.

He had asked about access times.

A security guard remembered Ahrens asking whether vehicles were in the courtyard at night.

And that morning a tip had come in that his van had been seen near the building again later the same evening.

Four points. No proof.

But enough that Jonas wasn't the only one who wanted certainty faster.

Daniel moved the first report onto the second screen. The tip was brief. No name for the person who had made it, only an internal forwarding note. The word **photographed** appeared three times.

"When was he there the first time?"

Jonas checked his notes. "Tuesday. Just after eleven."

"And the second?"

"Thursday, three-thirty. We don't know how long he was there either time. There's video only from Thursday—entrance camera, four minutes in frame."

Daniel opened the sequence.

Ahrens entered from the left. Work jacket. Phone in hand. He stopped by the roll-up gate, raised the phone, moved two steps to the side, took another photo. Then he disappeared from view.

"Looks like he's casing the place," Jonas said.

"Also looks like someone taking pictures of a roll-up gate."

"Technically, that's the same thing."

Daniel stopped the video at the moment Ahrens lowered the phone.

A company logo was visible on the back of his jacket. Blurred, but large.

"If you're secretly casing a federal government building, do you wear your employer's jacket?"

Jonas shrugged. "Maybe he's bad at it."

"Possible."

"Or he knows that's exactly what makes him look harmless."

Daniel let the video continue.

"Also possible."

Jonas waited for more.

Daniel gave him nothing.

"Patrol could pull him over on his way home," Jonas said. "Traffic stop. If something gives them grounds, they look in the van. If not, he drives on."

Daniel took the mouse and reopened the security report.

"What exactly did he photograph?"

"The gate. The bollard. The access road."

"That's what the tip says."

Jonas was quiet for a moment.

"We don't have the pictures."

"Right."

Daniel scrolled farther.

The security guard had documented what he'd seen the next morning. Not immediately. Daniel clicked the timestamp.

"Why not until the next day?"

"Shift change. He spoke to a colleague who said they should report it."

Daniel went through the next points himself. Ahrens had neither tried to get inside nor tried to obscure the surveillance camera. The report also contained no question about security measures.

"Who said he asked about nighttime operations?"

"The guard."

"Exact wording?"

Jonas flipped through his notes. "Whether deliveries still take place after eight p.m."

"That's different."

"Not by much."

"Enough."

Jonas exhaled through his nose. Not annoyed. Not yet.

Daniel opened the tip about the van. A white panel van with company lettering had been seen two streets away on Thursday evening. Time: 7:12 p.m. No photo. The person reporting it had not written down the full license plate.

"Are we sure it was his van?"

"Company name matches."

"How many vans does the company have?"

Jonas typed. "Still checking."

"Then the company name matches."

"Daniel, we're not talking about some van outside some building."

"No."

"If the innocent explanation doesn't hold, we've got someone who photographed the access twice, asked about evening operations, then showed up in the area again later."

Daniel nodded.

"Yes."

The agreement knocked Jonas off rhythm for a second.

"And that's not enough for you?"

"Enough to check. Not enough to stop him."

Jonas tapped one finger on the table.

"I don't want to make him a suspect. I want to keep us from realizing at five o'clock that we've been looking for the same white van since this morning."

Daniel looked at him.

That was the stronger argument. Not curiosity. Time.

"If you're right," Daniel said.

"Yes."

"And if you're wrong, you still caused the stop."

"Then he drives on."

"Maybe someone asks why. Maybe one stop becomes a second because an officer sees the note. Or maybe nothing happens."

Jonas nodded slowly.

"And if I'm right?"

Daniel leaned back.

"Then I don't want us wasting two hours."

"Exactly."

"So we check faster now. Not broader."

Jonas had nothing to say to that.

Daniel pulled a blank sheet of paper toward him.

"What would we expect to find if Ahrens is completely innocent?" Daniel asked.

Jonas looked at him.

"A reason for the photos."

"More specific."

"A work order. A contact at the building. Something that explains the questions."

Daniel wrote **JOB / CONTACT / ACCESS**.

"And the van?"

Jonas thought. "A job in the area."

Daniel wrote **VEHICLE ASSIGNMENT** underneath.

"What else?"

"Maybe it wasn't his van."

Daniel added that too.

"That doesn't automatically make him innocent."

"It isn't supposed to."

Jonas looked at the sheet. "And if we don't find any of it?"

"Then the innocent explanation gets weaker."

"While he keeps driving around."

"Yes."

Daniel pushed the paper toward him.

"Then we check it."

Jonas twisted his mouth. "That takes time. How long?"

"The company is open. Security is staffed. Vehicle dispatch shouldn't be a state secret."

Jonas looked at the clock. "An hour if everyone answers."

"Then start."

"And if it isn't an innocent job?"

Daniel turned to face him now.

Jonas held his gaze. Daniel liked that about him. He didn't withdraw an objection just because someone older in the room had made a decision.

"Then in two hours we'll know more than we do now," Daniel said.

"Or we'll have two hours less."

Daniel let the sentence stand.

Someone passed in the hallway and laughed too loudly. A door slammed shut. The work phone on Daniel's desk vibrated, nothing but a calendar reminder.

He selected the standard inquiry option and gave Jonas two checks to run. The client behind the work order. The van's assignment.

Then he added a third.

"Call security. Not the guard. The shift supervisor. I want to know whether Ahrens was registered or whether his company has any kind of job there."

Jonas got up.

"The guard should have known that."

"Maybe."

"You think he didn't check."

"I don't think anything. Not yet."

Jonas took the paper.

"No traffic stop," Daniel said.

Jonas nodded.

"Understood."

He was on his feet but stayed beside the table for another moment.

"You don't think it's at least a little strange?"

Daniel looked at the photo of the roll-up gate.

"I do."

"And?"

"Strange isn't enough."

Jonas stopped at the door.

"What would be enough for you?"

Daniel looked from the photograph to him.

"For the stop?"

"Yes."

Daniel thought for a moment.

"One fact that actually weakens the innocent work explanation. Not another detail that only looks like the first one."

"For example?"

"No work order. Wrong contact. Van not assigned where the company says it was. Something independent that doesn't fit."

Jonas nodded.

"And if we find exactly that in an hour?"

"Then we talk again."

"So this isn't a no."

"It's a no to what we have right now."

Jonas held the sheet a little tighter between two fingers.

"That's a distinction no one outside this room will ever see."

Daniel looked back at Ahrens.

"That's why we have to make it in here."

Jonas's mouth shifted briefly. Almost a smile, almost not.

Then he left.

Daniel remained alone with the photograph.

He opened the stills again. In none of them was Ahrens looking toward the camera. He really was photographing the lower part of the access area, not the windows above it. In one image he had even crouched beside the bollard.

Daniel didn't zoom in any farther.

The temptation was there to read intent into every movement once you knew what you were looking for.

Ahrens had taken the picture from an angle that really did show the access route, not the building itself.

Daniel had seen worse reasons to get nervous.

It still wasn't enough.

He didn't close the file. He only moved it out of the center of the screen.
