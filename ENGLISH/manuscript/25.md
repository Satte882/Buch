## 25

8:11 p.m.

Daniel had seen the time dozens of times.

It was in the source's first message.

**8:11 p.m. Hallway outside your mother's apartment. You pressed the mailbox nameplate back into place.**

At the time, the line had been personal above all else.

Now something else bothered him.

Because of Weber, he had gone back to the beginning.

Not systematically.

No large timeline on the wall monitor.

Just the old messages, his notes, and the places where he had later seen meaning that had not existed yet at the time.

The first warning.

The yellow strip.

The pickup.

The mailbox.

Daniel stopped at the time.

8:11 p.m.

He opened his calendar.

The appointment with Jana was there as a private block. No address.

He opened Jana's message from that week.

**I'm already upstairs. Realtor may come by again later.**

Then the documents for the warehouse complex.

Then the internal assignment of the case.

The sequence had been in front of him for days.

He had simply never put the points beside one another.

On the evening with Jana, Warehouse C was not yet his case.

Berg had put the matter on Daniel's desk only afterward, not hours before.

Daniel sat still.

He checked the times again because the first reaction had come too quickly.

Maybe the matter had already been sitting somewhere in his work area.

No.

It had arrived the following morning.

Maybe Berg had mentioned it verbally beforehand.

Daniel opened his appointment records and his messages with Berg.

Nothing.

Maybe another task had already linked him to the warehouse address.

He searched his earlier cases for Warehouse C.

No hit.

Unit 17 either.

His only earlier contact with the same complex was the closed fraud matter he had found only later through the address search.

That was not enough to predict that this case would land with him.

Daniel also opened the team's handover list from the following morning.

The warehouse complex first appeared there assigned to him at 8:34 a.m.

He searched the version from the night before.

No line.

No advance marker.

No indication Berg had already reserved the matter for Daniel.

That did not rule out Berg or someone else having discussed it earlier.

It only ruled out Daniel's normal work records as the explanation for the observation in the apartment hallway.

Daniel pushed his chair back. The wheels sounded suddenly too loud against the floor.

He stood for a moment.

His right hand rested on the edge of the desk. His fingers were cold.

Then he sat down again.

Someone had captured the scene in the hallway to the minute before the case was even his.

That could be coincidence.

Someone could have been watching Daniel for another reason.

Someone could have been watching many people and later pulled out the one detail that fit.

The source could have received the detail later from whoever had observed it.

Daniel wrote down the three possibilities.

Then a fourth.

**Time reconstructed later.**

Possible. Jana had received a call from the realtor. Emails carried timestamps. Someone could have reconstructed 8:11 later from other data.

Daniel opened the original first source message.

It had arrived before he had widely shared the connection to Warehouse C.

The private reference was already there.

So any later reconstruction would still have had to happen before that message.

Still possible.

Just more complicated.

He called Jana.

She answered on the fourth ring.

"If you're calling to ask whether the mailbox is still straight, I'm hanging up."

Daniel looked at the time.

"What time did the realtor call that night?"

Silence.

"Which call?"

"When we came out of the apartment. You were already on the street."

"Daniel, that was weeks ago."

"You got an email afterward."

"Of course I got an email afterward. That man sends more email than a newsletter."

"Can you check?"

"Why?"

Daniel did not answer immediately.

"Because I need the sequence."

Jana sighed.

He heard her typing.

"Here. 8:13 p.m. Subject: purchase price. He called two minutes before that. Is that enough?"

Daniel looked at the anonymous message.

8:11 p.m.

"Were you already outside then?"

"I was in front of the building. You were heading toward the subway."

"Sure?"

"Because I almost walked into that broken planter while I was on the phone. Yes."

Daniel remembered the planter. Gray concrete, chipped corner beside the entrance.

"And the nameplate?"

"What about it?"

"When did I press it back in?"

"Before we went outside."

"Approximately?"

"Two minutes before? Three? Why?"

So the time did not merely fit an email visible afterward.

It fit a brief event in the hallway the realtor had not seen and that had never been described in any message.

"Did the realtor see me at the mailbox that night?"

"No. He'd already left."

Daniel said nothing.

"What's going on?" Jana asked.

"Nothing I can explain cleanly yet."

"That's one of your worst answers."

"I know."

"Then try a better one."

Daniel looked at the four explanations on his sheet.

"Someone knew something about that evening before I was assigned a case in which the same source later appeared again."

Jana was silent.

"Person?"

"Source."

"And you think someone was already watching you before that."

Daniel did not answer immediately.

"I'm checking exactly that."

"Which means yes when you say it."

"No. It means the sequence no longer fits my previous explanation."

Jana sighed.

"Fine. That's all I know. The call was at 8:11, the email at 8:13, and you pressed that stupid label in shortly before."

"Thanks."

"And?"

"That's enough."

"Daniel."

He almost said again that he would call later.

The sentence was already there.

He did not say it.

"I don't know what it means yet."

"Okay."

"But it's important."

"Also okay."

"I'll call you when I have more."

"Then don't say later this time."

Despite everything, Daniel smiled briefly.

"I won't."

After the call, he put the times side by side.

Evening with Jana.

8:11 p.m. mailbox / realtor call.

Following morning, 8:34 a.m.: first documented assignment of the warehouse complex to Daniel.

Later: minor incident.

After that: first anonymous message containing the private detail.

Later: source knows about his problematic data cross-check.

The old explanation no longer fit. The source had not begun reacting to Daniel only after he became important to the case. The private reference predated the documented assignment.

That did not prove selection. Someone could have been watching him for another reason, reconstructed the time, or kept many people under observation. But Daniel had to allow a second possibility: someone had already had a reason to find his behavior interesting.

He wrote down two questions.

**Who could have assessed me professionally beforehand?**

**Who could have expected me to become relevant before the case was assigned?**

Then only one note:

**Private reference predates Warehouse C assignment. Review possible selection point.**

He opened no personnel files and no lists of names. Later access could not retroactively turn someone into a pre-case candidate.

The new chronology changed no operational fact. Mika remained involved. The wider review group had genuinely worked. Weber had been wrongly burdened. The source had delivered real information.

Only Daniel's place in the sequence had shifted.

Not: who knew the case?

Who knew him?

And why had that mattered before Daniel himself knew there was a case?
