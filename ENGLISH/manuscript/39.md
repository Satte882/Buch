## 39

There were two paths.

Berg did not like the sentence.

"There are always twelve paths if you talk long enough."

Lena slid two sheets across the table.

"Today, two are enough."

Daniel pulled the narrower path closer.

"Forty-five minutes best case," he said. "What does that consist of?"

Berg picked up a pen.

"Access office separately. Vehicle keeper separately. People through the responsible offices. Then combine the responses. After that, targeted measures."

"What runs in parallel?"

"Not all of it."

"Why not?"

"Because not every office has the same basis, and some parts can't be passed on until the case connection is clearly established."

Lena added, "And because the narrow path depends on each access being justified more narrowly. That takes coordination."

Daniel wrote the earliest realistic response times beside the steps.

The times were limits, not an exact plan.

Twelve minutes here.

Maybe twenty there.

A callback if someone was reachable.

Another if not.

After three lines, the first theoretical measure was already beyond the moved-up access window.

"Best case," Berg repeated.

Daniel nodded.

The narrower path was real.

So was its risk.

Lena slid the second sheet back across.

The project structure saved time not through secret access.

It saved time because existing case-relevant data became visible simultaneously inside an approved group and several reviews could start in parallel.

Daniel had learned to value exactly that.

"What scope do we actually need?" Lena asked.

Daniel marked the two confirmed service areas.

"These."

"People?"

"Only current access or current operational function there."

Berg pointed to the intermediary node.

"And indirect contacts?"

Daniel shook his head.

"Not without their own anchor."

"That can cost us exactly the intermediary who isn't on the list himself."

"Yes."

Daniel said it before Berg could continue.

The price belonged to the decision.

It belonged to it now, not only to the later review.

Daniel read the first sheet.

The narrow path.

Check the affected companies and people individually through the responsible offices, have existing access rights blocked selectively, verify current vehicles one after another, build the cleanest available basis for every further measure.

Feasible.

Just not fast.

"How long?" Daniel asked.

Berg did not answer immediately.

"Best case, forty-five minutes."

Jonas looked at the clock.

No one commented.

"And realistically?"

"Longer."

Daniel took the second sheet.

The project structure they had built over the previous few days.

Joint analysis of existing case-relevant information. Review contact, timing, and access relationships in the defined group in parallel. Have people assess hits immediately. Measures only where their own basis supported them.

Faster.

Broader.

And exactly the logic whose emergence had begun on Heller's desk before Daniel even knew he was part of the calculation.

Lena tapped one line.

"If we use this, I'd set the group narrower than last time."

"How narrow?"

"Only people and vehicles with current access to the two confirmed service areas. No second layer just because of contact relationships."

Berg said, "Then we can miss exactly the intermediary who isn't on the list himself."

"Yes."

"And if tomorrow he is the reason we were too late?"

Lena looked at him.

"Then the limitation may have been wrong."

Berg shook his head.

"That's too academic for me."

"No. That's just the honest form of risk."

Daniel looked at the page.

Weeks earlier, he probably would have said the same thing himself.

That did not help now.

"No private contacts," he said.

Lena nodded.

"No legacy data that becomes relevant only through Heller's unconfirmed claims."

"Yes."

"Every hit gets an alternative explanation before it becomes a measure."

Jonas wrote it down.

Berg asked, "And the time advantage?"

"Remains."

Daniel looked at him.

"Because we review in parallel."

"So we do it."

Daniel did not answer.

Berg raised his hands.

"Sorry. Your decision."

It did not sound mocking.

Almost worse.

Daniel thought of the security guard in the hospital.

The image appeared briefly and disappeared.

Then of the plate Heller's area had possessed earlier.

That did not go away either.

This was exactly the form of pressure Heller had wanted.

Real danger.

Too little time.

A clean path that was probably slower.

Daniel knew it now.

That was the difference.

Not that the situation had become less real.

"We use the project structure," he said.

Lena looked at him.

Daniel continued.

"Limited to the confirmed access group. No expansion from the source. Every access logged. Every operational step needs its own confirmed anchor."

Berg nodded once.

"Good."

Lena asked, "And if the wider review wrongly hits someone again?"

Daniel looked at the timestamp of the service authorization with the moved-up window.

"Then we'll have to answer for that later."

She said nothing.

He noticed the sentence only after it was out.

Later.

As though later were a place where every debt could be paid.

Daniel turned to Jonas.

"Also: preserve everything on Heller. Accesses, project versions, preliminary check, old special analysis. Separate from the operation."

"Now?"

"Now."

"That costs people."

"Then it costs people."

Berg looked at him.

Daniel said, "I don't want to hear tomorrow that the success was more important than the record again."

Jonas nodded.

The work began.

No sirens.

No grand announcement.

On three screens appeared the same categories Daniel would not have wanted to see together in one room a few weeks earlier.

Access.

Vehicles.

Time windows.

People.

A hit came after four minutes.

The first was wrong.

A technician had two badges because his replacement after a malfunction had not yet been checked out of the system.

Jonas clarified it and crossed him off.

The second was ordinary.

A driver was registered at two service points because he had moved goods between them.

Gone.

The third remained.

A vehicle covered by the current authorization was registered to a subcontractor whose actual use that evening could not be confirmed by the organizer. At the same time, it had been seen that afternoon at the same address as the vehicle from the support strand.

Lena read the counterhypothesis.

"Dispatch error. Short-notice swap. Unauthorized third-party use."

"Check it," Daniel said.

They did.

The subcontractor had officially listed the vehicle as unavailable since noon.

No proof of the final plan.

But a current independent basis to stop the vehicle and establish who was behind it.

Daniel held Berg back a moment longer.

"Before anyone turns this hit into a whole group: what's the measure exactly?"

Berg put the phone down again.

"Secure the vehicle. Establish the people inside. Clarify the connection."

"Not the whole subcontractor group. Not everyone who's had contact over the last few days."

"No."

Lena said, "And if nothing in the vehicle supports the concern, the hit goes back to zero."

Daniel nodded.

That was exactly the point where faster work otherwise expanded almost invisibly.

A solid current anchor opened a review.

It did not automatically legitimize every relationship around it.

Jonas pointed to the fourth hit they had left open.

"Then this contact stays without action?"

"Yes."

"Even though the timing fits?"

"Timing alone isn't enough."

Berg said, "If that man matters later, we lost minutes."

Daniel looked at him.

"If he doesn't matter and we treat him now as part of the plan, we lose something else."

Berg wanted to answer but did not.

The first units reported ready for the vehicle.

Daniel looked one more time at the narrow path, still on the table.

He could still choose it now.

Just not without the cost they had already calculated.

"We stay with the project structure," he said. "But only inside the limits we just set."

Lena asked, "And when the next hit makes those limits inconvenient?"

"They're still limits."

Daniel knew he had not chosen a safe outcome.

He had only decided which risk of error he was more willing to accept.

The difference from earlier was not that he controlled uncertainty.

He simply could no longer let it pretend it belonged to someone else.

Berg picked up his phone.

"Then the intervention can be prepared."

Daniel looked at the two paths still on the table.

The first had not been wrong.

Only slower.

Daniel picked up the narrower path again and placed the estimated responses beside the moved-up time window.

He used the realistic values rather than the optimistic ones.

First callback: twelve to twenty minutes.

Second jurisdiction: open.

Person review: only partly parallel.

Consolidation afterward.

"If everything goes well?" Berg asked.

"Then we're close to the window."

"And if one office takes ten minutes?"

Daniel moved the final step beyond the marker.

Lena said, "That's still not proof the narrow path fails."

"No."

"Only its time risk."

"Yes."

Daniel wrote exactly that on the page.

**Alternative available. Expected to be slower. Success inside the confirmed window uncertain.**

Not *too slow*.

Not *impractical*.

He did not want to write an actual alternative out of existence before choosing against it.

Then he placed the faster structure beside it.

More simultaneous scope.

More false hits.

More need for oversight.

And a time advantage.

That did not make the decision clean.

Only finally comparable.

Daniel asked Jonas one last question.

"If we choose the narrow path and the first response comes early enough, can we still switch to the structure later?"

Jonas checked the sequence.

"Partly. But then we lose exactly the parallelism that makes it faster."

"So it isn't a choice between safe and risky."

"No."

"Between two different risks."

Lena said, "And both remain real options."

Daniel nodded.

That mattered.

He was not choosing the only workable route.

He was choosing the one whose failure he was more willing to own in that moment.

The first had not been wrong.

Only slower.

Daniel wrote the two risks one beneath the other.

**Narrow path:** fewer people in the review group, higher probability of later connection, confirmed time window continues to run.

**Project structure:** faster connection of existing data, more benign hits and misattribution risk, additional reach despite Heller's history.

No line said *no alternative*.

Daniel did not underline **faster** in the second sentence. He underlined **more benign hits** instead.

"Safeguards again," he said.

Jonas listed them.

Defined current access group.

No expansion to contacts of contacts.

Hits without an independent anchor trigger no measure.

Every incriminating hit gets a counterhypothesis.

Discarded hits remain visible in the after-action report.

Lena added, "And if the basis for the current group disappears, we don't silently expand to rescue it."

Daniel nodded.

Heller's manipulation had not disappeared from the instrument. It remained a reason Daniel had to justify every step more narrowly.

But it was also not a reason to pretend the structure had never produced real successes.

Daniel looked at the two paths.

He was not choosing between clean and dirty.

He was choosing between two real risk profiles.

And this time he could never later say he had not known the other side.

He did not fold up the narrow path.

He left it on the table.

Daniel picked it up one more time.

"I want the operational record to state that we had this option."

Berg looked at him.

"Why now?"

"Because tomorrow otherwise it will look as though there was only one reasonable path."

Lena nodded.

Daniel dictated briefly: narrower review path available, expected slower, higher risk to confirmed time window; expanded structure consciously chosen and limited.

No justification.

Only the alternative that had existed.

He put the sheet back.

That did not make his decision better.

Only more clearly his.

Daniel looked once more at the limits they had set for the faster review.

They had not remained theoretical.

Two hits had been discarded, one had supported a targeted measure, another remained open despite time pressure.

That was the difference between seeing broadly and acting broadly.

Lena stood beside him.

"If this works, tomorrow everyone will point at the hit that worked."

Daniel nodded.

"Then the others go into the report too."

"Even the ones where nothing happened?"

"Especially those."

Because a system always looked cleaner in retrospect once the discarded names disappeared.

Daniel did not want success to make the costs invisible again.
