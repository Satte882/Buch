## 11

At 7:06 p.m., Daniel still did not have a second solid point.

Instead, six unanswered requests were sitting on his screen.

One company connection was clean and ordinary.

A vehicle check had produced nothing.

The query concerning a former employee of the service provider was still open.

At the event coordination office, the responsible person had called back only to say that she did not manage the access permissions herself.

A second office had taken over.

Answer: pending.

Daniel looked at the time and hated that he did.

6:42 p.m.

6:51 p.m.

7:02 p.m.

The same open list every time.

Jonas had checked two more publicly available company connections. Nothing.

The black hard-shell case had arrived at the technical service provider and, according to the report, remained there.

No unusual contact.

No rushed handoff.

No person suddenly disappearing.

It almost looked as if Heller's harmless version intended to win.

Daniel called the office managing event access once more. An employee searched for the service provider in the system and found it immediately.

"Registered normally," she said. "Several vehicles, several people. Nothing blocked."

"Any changes today?"

"I'd have to check. I'll get back to you tonight."

Daniel thanked her. He knew explaining his impatience would not make her work faster.

At 7:12 p.m., Jonas called.

"We have a problem."

Daniel heard voices in the background.

"Where are you?"

"Operations room. Report from Neukölln. The technical service provider."

Daniel stood.

"What happened?"

"A vehicle drove through the entrance to one of their depots. The security guard tried to stop it."

"And?"

Jonas was silent one second too long.

"He's in the hospital."

Daniel was already moving down the corridor.

"Driver?"

"Gone. Van abandoned on the grounds."

"Plate?"

"Not the gray van from the warehouse."

Daniel stopped for a moment.

"Then what?"

"Blue van. Rental vehicle."

That did not fit.

The sentence changed everything and nothing at the same time.

If the gray van was not involved, Daniel's most visible connection had been wrong.

If the depot still belonged to the service provider's environment, the warning had not simply disappeared.

Five people stood in front of two monitors in the operations room. One colleague was on the phone with the depot. Someone else was pulling vehicle records. A fragmented follow-up to the initial report came over a speaker.

A colleague read out the first note: barrier damaged, security employee injured, vehicle abandoned, driver fled.

Daniel did not let anyone summarize the initial report for him.

"Who is on the phone with the depot right now?"

The colleague pointed at her handset. "Shift supervisor."

"Speaker."

She switched it over.

A man's voice came through, too fast while still trying to sound factual.

He had not seen the impact. Only heard it. When he came out of the building, the van was standing at an angle behind the damaged barrier. The security guard was on the ground. The driver was already gone.

"Did anyone see whether he was hit or fell while trying to get out of the way?" Daniel asked.

"No. Not clearly."

"Did the driver speak to him first?"

"I don't know."

"Did the security guard say anything afterward?"

A short pause.

"Not to me."

Daniel stopped the questions there.

More pressure would not turn the absence of an eyewitness into a better one.

A second phone brought the first update from the hospital. Multiple injuries, including fractures. At present, no life-threatening condition.

Daniel did not copy the sentence down.

He did not need a detailed medical file to understand that the until-now abstract cost of waiting had become a specific person.

At the same time, he still did not know whether faster action would have prevented this exact harm.

Both had to remain true at once.

Jonas pulled the first still image of the abandoned vehicle onto the monitor.

Blue.

Daniel looked at the front first, then the plate, then the color again.

Not gray.

The story he had built around the hard-shell case and the gray van did not collapse because of some major piece of evidence.

Only because the vehicle was different.

That was enough.

"Take the gray van out of the operational chain," Daniel said.

Jonas looked at him. "Completely?"

"As the main lead. If it comes back independently later, it comes back."

Daniel said the sentence and felt how much easier it would have been to keep the gray van somewhere in the picture anyway.

Just in case.

That was exactly what he did not do.

Daniel had them show him the origin of the new report.

"The depot itself?"

"Yes. Initial report from the shift supervisor. Patrol officers are there."

"Not the source?"

"Not the source."

He did not really need the sentence. It still mattered that the new anchor had emerged outside the anonymous message.

Jonas immediately pushed a printout toward him.

"The van first appeared at the service provider today at 4:41 p.m. From everything we have so far, it was never at Warehouse C."

"Certain?"

"Front cameras, known access points, the yard footage. Can't prove a complete negative, but no hit."

"The black case?"

"Still there."

Daniel looked at him.

"Where?"

"In the service provider's office. Unopened, according to an employee. The officers are securing it."

"Who confirmed that?"

"Employee on site and the first patrol."

That broke apart the simplest story.

Case from the warehouse. Service provider. Vehicle. Threat.

Too clean.

And wrong.

Lena came in, still holding her phone.

"The security guard is responsive. Multiple fractures, not life-threatening."

Daniel looked at her.

"How did it happen?"

"He approached the driver's side when the van stopped at the barrier. The van suddenly accelerated through. He was hit from the side and pushed against the entrance structure."

No dramatic description.

Only enough to give Daniel an image he did not need.

"Intentional?"

"Unclear. Driver's gone."

"Name?"

Lena gave it to him.

Daniel did not write it down. It was already in the case file.

"Family notified?"

"As far as I know, yes."

He asked nothing more.

The man was not there to illustrate Daniel's decision morally. He was injured. That was enough.

Berg followed Lena in.

"What do we have in the van?"

"No complete search result yet," Jonas said. "But two access badges that don't belong to the vehicle. One has been reported lost. The second appears to be forged."

"Appears?" Daniel asked.

"Number doesn't match the issue sequence. Final confirmation is running."

Daniel looked at the screen.

A photo of the blue van. Front-end damage. The barrier bent beside it.

The vehicle looked ordinary. Blue paint, light scratches, nothing matching the weight it had acquired over the last three minutes.

"Event?"

Lena nodded. "Both badges belong to the service-provider environment for a major event this weekend."

"Which one?"

She named it.

Daniel knew the name. Everyone in Berlin did.

He felt his mind start filling in the missing pieces immediately.

Warehouse. Courier. Service provider. Badges. Van. Major event.

A line.

Maybe.

He moved to the second monitor.

"What connects the driver to Unit 17?" he asked.

Jonas shook his head. "Nothing yet. Nothing to the courier either. The van was at the service provider for just under twenty minutes today. Rental agreement under false contact details, identity of the renter still open."

"So not the employee who accepted the case."

"Not on what we know now."

Daniel looked at the two vehicle images.

Gray from the warehouse environment.

Blue from the depot.

He had made the gray vehicle larger because it was the first thing he could see.

The source had never claimed it was the decisive vehicle.

The reply from event coordination appeared on Daniel's screen.

**No unusual changes to regular service-provider access permissions.**

Ten minutes earlier, that sentence would have reassured him.

Now he knew that two badges from exactly that environment had been found in an abandoned vehicle.

Clean data could be reassuring in the wrong context too.

Berg looked at Daniel.

"The warning?"

Daniel took out his phone and opened the message.

**The target is not at the warehouse. This is about the logistics of a public event in Berlin.**

That was true.

Or true enough.

The part Daniel had focused on was not.

The hard-shell case apparently was not the decisive object. The gray van was not the vehicle involved in the incident. The company in Unit 17 was still only a connection, not an established offender.

"We read the source wrong," he said.

"Or the source was imprecise," Lena said.

"Both are possible."

"Or deliberately broad," Berg said.

Daniel looked at him.

"Also possible."

Jonas looked up from the screen.

"Does that matter right now?"

"Yes."

"A man is in the hospital."

"That's why it matters."

Jonas started to say something, then stopped.

Daniel stepped closer to him.

"If we now start treating every part of the warning as confirmed because one part was real, tomorrow we'll be worse than we are today."

Jonas looked at the photo of the van.

"And if we separate things for too long again?"

Daniel did not answer immediately.

That was the new sentence in the room.

A colleague at the other end called for Berg.

Confirmation on the second badge came in.

Forged.

It was not enough for the whole chain. It was enough for more than a traffic accident.

Daniel sat at an open workstation and pulled up the timeline.

2:20 p.m. Pickup.

4:41 p.m. Blue van at service provider.

6:53 p.m. Van enters depot.

7:01 p.m. Security guard injured.

Between them he added their inquiries.

3:03 p.m. Request to event-access office.

3:26 p.m. Forwarded to second office.

4:12 p.m. First partial response.

4:41 p.m. Blue van at service provider.

Part of the lost time had not been a legal problem.

Only sequence.

They had had almost five hours between the pickup and the incident.

Five hours with indications, not proof.

That was a difference.

The difference was now lying in a hospital with multiple fractures.

Daniel looked at the 3:03 line.

That did not suddenly make the decision from the meeting with Heller wrong.

On the facts they had then, Daniel could still have defended it.

That did not help the security guard.

For a moment he imagined the man arriving for work that afternoon without being part of any threat situation. No longer than that. Just long enough to understand what the word consequence meant here.

Then Daniel pushed the image away.

He did not need guilt larger than the facts.

He needed a better process.

Daniel closed the timeline again.

"Everything we can legally pull on the blue van, run it in parallel now," he told Jonas. "Not one after another."

"Registered owner, rental, contacts?"

"Everything the current vehicle itself supports. No expansion just because of the message."

Jonas nodded.

"On it."

Daniel reached for his phone.

Heller had given him a direct number.

He called.

While it rang, Daniel looked once more at the front-end damage on the van.

The warning had not been entirely right.

Only right enough that someone was now in the hospital.
