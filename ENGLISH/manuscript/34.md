## 34

"Again," Daniel said. "Without Heller."

Lena looked at him over the top edge of her laptop.

"That's the third time you've said that now."

"Maybe it'll be true the third time."

The room was two floors below the operations area and normally used for confidential personnel meetings. Four chairs, one table, no windows. Jonas had run an extension cord under the door because only one outlet worked.

There was no evidence on the table.

Only printouts.

Warnings. Timelines. Access notes. Responses that had arrived too late at the time or had seemed unimportant.

Daniel took the first message.

The warehouse pickup.

Wrong unit. Right billing party. Exact time.

"What's missing?" Lena asked.

"Too much."

"That's not an answer."

Daniel put the sheet aside.

"The event."

"Came later."

"Yes."

"Would the source have had to know it already at that point?"

Daniel looked at the timelines.

"Can't prove that."

Lena made a line on her notepad.

No hit.

That was how they had been working for almost two hours.

Daniel reframed the review question: what survived the theory?

In one message, Daniel thought he had found an omitted location. Jonas showed that the location became reliable internally only seven minutes after the warning arrived.

Discard it.

In another, a company connection that later became important was missing. Lena found the first clean association only the following morning.

Discard it.

Daniel liked those two failures more than he should have.

They made it easier to tell himself the rest would fall apart too.

Jonas sat on the floor against the wall with the notebook on his knees.

"I've got yesterday's delivery authorization."

Daniel looked at him.

"Which version?"

"All of them."

"Helpful."

"Doing my best."

Jonas turned the screen.

The new authorization that had led Daniel to approve the wider review. Exactly the identifier that had appeared in the anonymous message.

**Don't check only the name. Check the access around it.**

The internal status where the identifier first appeared in exactly that form contained a second note directly below it.

**Formal review completed. Regular addition to event setup.**

Daniel knew the line by now.

He read it again anyway.

"Could the identifier have leaked through an earlier route?"

Jonas shrugged.

"Anything's possible. But we haven't found an older status containing that exact identifier."

"Verbally?"

"Not provable."

Lena said, "Then it remains a strong gap. Still not proof of intent."

Daniel nodded.

The point was true. Only the line beneath it was missing—the one that made it look less threatening.

"Next."

Jonas opened the first boundary crossing.

The plate Daniel had checked against the protected legacy records. Three hits. A real lead. Later the access that had helped protect people.

The source had known how Daniel searched.

"Nothing action-guiding is missing here," Daniel said.

Lena raised her eyebrows slightly.

"Sure?"

He read the old messages.

Brief. Precise. Always only one step farther.

But he could not identify the kind of specific omission they were looking for.

"No," he said. "Not sure. But I can't claim anything."

"Then don't."

Discard it.

Daniel took the printout back.

"Show me the seven minutes."

Jonas opened the version history.

Then the raw status instead of the summary.

An internal message had arrived at 3:06 p.m. The location Daniel later wanted to accuse the source of omitting did not become reliably assigned until 3:13.

"Before that?"

"A free-text field without attribution."

"So theoretically known."

"Theoretically, a lot of things are known."

Daniel did not like the answer.

"Who had the free-text field?"

Jonas opened the recipient list.

Too many offices.

No clean route to the source. No evidence anyone had recognized the location as relevant before 3:13.

Lena asked, "Would the location at 3:06 have changed your decision?"

Daniel looked at what they had known then.

At the time, they were missing more than the location. The connection to the service provider was still open too.

"No," he said.

"Then it isn't the gap we're looking for."

Daniel crossed out the point himself.

The next sheet took longer.

A company connection the source had mentioned only later. Daniel had been sure it existed in the system earlier.

It had.

But incorrectly.

Two companies with similar names. An automated link that was not confirmed by a human until the next morning.

"If I count this, I'm building my own pattern," Daniel said.

"Yes."

"And if I don't count it, I could still be missing something."

"Also yes."

Jonas leaned back.

"This is a terrible investigative method."

Daniel looked at him.

"Welcome."

They put the company connection on the pile of discarded points.

That pile was now higher than the other one.

That helped.

It did not clear Heller. It forced Daniel to earn every gap individually.

Daniel stood.

The back of his chair hit the wall.

"We're building a method out of four suspicious places."

"Three," Jonas said.

Daniel looked at him.

"Thanks."

"Anytime."

Lena pushed a printout toward him.

"That's why we're not looking for suspicious places."

"What are we looking for?"

"Places where the omitted information would have changed the decision."

Daniel pulled a blank sheet toward him and wrote three words one beneath the other.

**Unavailable.**

**Neutral.**

**Decision-changing.**

Lena read along.

"Too clean."

"That's why it's on paper."

He pointed to the first category.

"Unavailable: information wasn't reliable yet or wasn't accessible to the source."

Then the second.

"Neutral: it was missing but wouldn't have changed our decision at the time."

Then the third.

"Decision-changing: it was available in time and would have opened a narrower, faster, or procedurally cleaner path."

Jonas said, "And if we don't know whether it was available?"

Daniel wrote **open** beside it.

"Then it stays open."

Lena took the pen and drew a line beneath the last word.

"And intent?"

Daniel looked at the warnings.

"Doesn't go on any of these sheets."

"Good."

They went through the remaining points again.

In one, the omission was real but neutral. Daniel still could not have acted on the missing context.

In another, the information was probably accessible but the access chain could not be closed cleanly.

Open.

Then the delivery authorization remained.

The identifier was genuine. The reassuring status was in the same working context. The warning delivered one and not the other.

Daniel took both lines and covered one at a time with his hand.

With only the identifier, the situation narrowed. With both, it remained worth checking. Not harmless, but different.

"Decision-changing," he said.

Lena nodded.

"That's one."

"One isn't a pattern."

"Correct."

Daniel looked at the stacks.

Discarded.

Open.

One decision-changing omission.

Not enough for the theory he wanted to test.

And better than forcing one.

He pulled the first warnings back onto the screen.

Warehouse complex.

Pickup.

Service provider.

Public event.

The security guard at the depot.

Multiple fractures.

No danger to life.

At the time, Daniel had seen five hours in which they had had leads but not enough basis.

Afterward, he had worked differently.

Faster.

In parallel.

Less patient with gaps.

Lena said nothing.

She knew where he had landed.

Jonas got up from the floor and put the notebook on the table.

"I've got an old processing path from that special analysis."

Daniel looked at him.

"From whom?"

"Not who yet. Area."

"Which one?"

Jonas named the coordination and special-analysis area from which Heller had accelerated the cross-state fragments at the time.

Daniel felt himself wanting to build a story immediately.

He resisted it.

"Heller works there. That proves nothing."

"I didn't say it did."

"What do you have?"

"Several old cases. I still need to separate the timelines."

Lena turned the notebook toward herself.

"Do only the depot."

Jonas looked at her.

"Why only that?"

"Because otherwise we try to prove everything at once again."

Daniel took the timeline from that evening.

2:20 p.m. pickup.

4:41 p.m. blue van at the service provider.

7:01 p.m. security guard on the ground.

There had not been five empty hours between those points.

There had been systems, people, fragments.

And maybe something Daniel had never seen at the time.

"The depot," he said.

Jonas sat down again.

Lena took the next printout from the pile and pushed all the others aside.

Only the old incident remained on the sheet.

This time the gap was testable.

Daniel held the depot printout for another moment.

He did not see more in it than before. What mattered was that, for the first time, a case remained in which the review criterion did not collapse immediately.

No pattern built from mood.

A concrete next test.
