## 22

"Tuesday?" Daniel asked.

Mara said nothing.

He stood in the corridor outside a conference room, holding the phone between his shoulder and ear. In four minutes, the first joint review under the new project structure would begin behind the door.

On the small table beside it lay a printout of the new role model. Daniel's name appeared in three places.

"Tuesday what?" Mara asked finally.

"Our conversation."

"The one that was supposed to be Sunday?"

"Yes."

"And yesterday?"

Daniel closed his eyes briefly.

Sunday, six p.m.

**Hamburg. Not sometime.**

The appointment was still in his calendar. Not moved. Not deleted. Simply overrun by a situation briefing that had started at 5:20 p.m. and was still going at 9:40.

Mara had texted at 6:07.

**I assume tonight isn't happening.**

Daniel had not seen the message until after nine.

"Yesterday wasn't possible."

"I know."

She did not sound angry.

That made it harder.

"Tuesday evening," he said. "I'll try—"

"No."

He heard voices in the room behind him.

"No what?"

"Don't tell me Tuesday if you don't know Tuesday."

Daniel switched the phone to his other hand.

"I can't guarantee you a time right now."

"Okay."

"Okay?"

"What else am I supposed to say?"

"That we'll find another one."

"Daniel, I'm not looking for a free table at a restaurant."

He looked at the time.

Three minutes.

"The situation has become bigger. My role too."

"I understand that."

"Then you know why—"

"Yes."

She did not interrupt loudly. Only early.

Daniel fell silent.

Mara said, "I still have to decide."

"Then ask for another extension."

"I did."

He straightened.

"When?"

"Yesterday."

"What time yesterday?"

"Morning."

"And?"

"Two more days. Not three weeks."

Daniel was annoyed that she had already done it.

The annoyance arrived immediately and was immediately unfair.

"You could have told me you were talking to them."

"When?"

He had no answer that did not sound stupid.

Mara did not help him.

"I also talked to HR about the start date," she said.

"Why?"

"Because they need to know when I can resign."

"That doesn't mean you're accepting."

"No."

"And housing?"

A short pause.

"I asked for two viewings."

Daniel said nothing.

"Haven't signed anything," Mara added. "Just asked."

"That sounds pretty far along."

"Yes."

"So you're already planning the move."

"I'm checking what my life looks like if I take the job."

"Without me."

Mara was quiet briefly.

"No," she said. "Without your decision. That's not the same thing."

Daniel rested his free hand on the small table.

The printout shifted beneath his fingers.

"I wanted us to do this together."

"So did I."

"Then give me a little more time."

"The time isn't mine to give."

"You got two more days."

"Yes. And I'm using them."

Daniel looked through the glass door into the conference room. Berg was already at the screen. Lena was going through documents. Jonas was pulling a cable from his bag.

Inside, there really was a situation waiting for him.

On the phone, Mara was no longer waiting.

Both were reasonable.

"What else have you checked?" Daniel asked.

Mara answered as if the list were already in her head.

Handover at her current office. Notice period. Two possible apartments for the first few months. Commuting time. Whether she could work partly from Berlin during the first weeks. Which client appointments were already fixed for November.

"You did all that since yesterday?"

"Not all since yesterday."

Daniel needed a second.

"Since when, then?"

"Since I knew the offer was serious."

"Why didn't you tell me?"

Mara did not laugh. "I did. Not every spreadsheet row."

He remembered dinner. Team. Reporting line. Start date. Hamburg.

He had asked about salary and work from home.

Mara had already been talking about her life.

Daniel noticed he was once again trying to turn every preparatory step into a decision that had already been made.

"Okay."

"I haven't resigned either."

"I know."

"Doesn't sound like it."

Daniel looked through the glass door again.

"I'm just trying to understand how far along you are."

"Farther than a week ago."

"Because of me?"

"Because of the deadline."

Mara did not say it sharply.

"And because I don't want to lose another five days while we try to find a time to make a joint decision."

Daniel could have objected. The five days had not been only his calendar. There had been a real terrorism case, injuries, an active operation.

All true.

None of it extended Mara's deadline.

The conference-room door opened. Jonas put his head out.

Daniel raised one finger.

"Two minutes," Jonas said quietly.

Mara probably heard the movement.

"You have to go in."

"Not yet."

"Yes."

"Mara."

"What?"

He wanted to say this was not how he wanted it.

That was true and helped no one.

"I don't want you thinking I don't care."

"I don't."

"Then why does it sound like you do?"

"Because that's not the problem."

Daniel waited.

"You just don't have a place for it right now," she said. "That's different from not caring."

The sentence was less accusatory than Daniel had expected.

That made it more accurate.

"I could tonight—"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because you'll be exhausted tonight and I don't want to start talking about the next three years at half past midnight just so we can formally say we talked."

Daniel was silent.

Mara said, "I want a real answer from you when you have one. Until then, I make the decisions that belong only to me."

"And accepting the job?"

"That doesn't belong only to me. But the deadline does."

Behind the door, someone began the meeting even though Daniel was still outside. Muted voices. A screen tone.

Daniel looked at his calendar. The Sunday appointment was still shaded gray. He opened it briefly.

No note explaining why it had failed.

Only **Hamburg. Not sometime.** and a red bar because the time had passed.

He thought back to Sunday evening. At 6:07 Mara had texted. At 6:12 he had seen the message in the operations area and told himself he would answer after the next update. Then a new vehicle hit came in.

At 6:40 he had stopped thinking about it. He had not consciously canceled the appointment; something else had simply outweighed it.

"I didn't even cancel Sunday," Daniel said.

Mara was quiet for a moment.

"No."

"That was shitty."

"Yes."

No apology loop. Just the word that fit.

"I'm sorry."

"Okay."

"Just okay?"

"Daniel, I'm not trying to punish you. I just don't want to pretend the appointment didn't happen simply because we didn't have it."

He closed the calendar entry.

"Understood."

"Good."

"I'll call you as soon as I have something solid to say."

Mara exhaled quietly.

"Good."

"That doesn't sound good."

"It's just the answer."

"And if I don't have anything solid tomorrow?"

"Then I decide with what I have."

Daniel looked at the project-structure printout under his hand.

The same words were standing there in another form. Incomplete situation. Available information. Decision despite open points.

He would rather not have seen the comparison.

Mara had not developed a new personality over the previous few days. She had simply done what a deadline required.

Called HR.

Clarified start options.

Requested two viewings.

Spoken to her deputy about a possible handover.

Every single step was provisional.

Together they still meant her life was moving forward while Daniel waited for the joint decision point he himself had missed.

"If you accept," he said, "I don't want to find out in a message."

Mara was quiet briefly.

"Then answer your phone."

The sentence was not cruel.

Daniel closed his eyes for a second.

"Fair."

"I'll tell you before I decide."

"Thank you."

"But I'm not asking again whether the timing works for you."

Daniel looked through the glass door at Berg, Lena, and Jonas.

"Understood."

This time he meant it.

No threat.

No *then it's over.*

Only the same logic Daniel had been working with for days.

Incomplete information did not remove a deadline.

A voice called Jonas back into the room.

Mara said, "I'm not putting my things on hold anymore until your calendar decides when we can talk about ours."

Daniel grimaced.

"That's not how it is."

"Then tell me when it isn't."

"I will."

"Good."

They ended the call without saying goodbye.

Daniel stayed in the corridor for another moment.

The screen showed the duration of the call. Ten minutes.

The Sunday appointment had been supposed to get two hours.

He put the phone away.

Jonas was back at the door.

"Everything okay?"

Daniel looked at him.

"No."

Jonas nodded as if that were a sufficiently precise professional answer.

Daniel followed him inside.

Berg was pointing at the new project structure.

"We start with the open access points."

Daniel sat down.

His phone stayed in his pocket.
