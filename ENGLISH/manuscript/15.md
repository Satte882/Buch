## 15

The first hit was bad.

Jonas put it on the table anyway.

"The courier company made a delivery to the same industrial area eight months ago."

Daniel shook his head.

"Eight months. Industrial area. Courier company."

"I know."

"Then why bring it?"

"Because you said everything."

"Everything doesn't mean we make something out of everything."

Jonas pushed the printout aside.

"Next point."

They were working in a small room beside the operations area now. On the table were Daniel's handwritten notes from the protected legacy records, not the legacy records themselves.

That was deliberate.

Daniel did not want his one problematic access to turn into a shared workspace where everyone simply continued using the same data as though it were normal.

Lena sat at the far end of the table on the phone with a Berlin office. Daniel had told her what the problematic cross-check had produced, not exactly how he had obtained it.

Not yet.

He did not like that either.

Jonas pointed to Daniel's note.

"Again. B-QV 4172 twice in temporal proximity to a white delivery van from the old case."

"The old van was associated with a man from a support network—associated, not automatically a perpetrator."

"And we can't just start pulling the person's entire network now."

"Correct."

Jonas looked at the time.

"Then we search publicly."

"And in everything the current case legitimately gives us."

"Garage?"

"Where did you get a garage from?"

Jonas pushed aside the first registry printout.

"The white van from the old case," he said. "It's been off the road for three weeks."

"So no current movement."

"No."

"What remains?"

"The person it was associated with back then."

Daniel looked up.

"No people spiral."

"Didn't do one." Jonas tapped a line. "The man hasn't worked for the old company for two months. But a company his brother has an interest in rents a garage."

Daniel waited.

"Source?"

"Commercial-register link. Company address. Then a publicly listed rental property through the business entry."

"Where?"

Jonas gave him the address.

Four hundred meters from the parking garage named in the anonymous message.

Daniel felt the impulse to make the point larger immediately.

Two vehicles in the legacy records.

Brother's company.

Garage.

Four hundred meters.

He could tell the whole line in one sentence.

That was exactly why he said nothing.

"What's the harmless version?"

Jonas rolled his eyes, but only briefly.

"A company rents a garage. Someone's brother isn't that someone. Four hundred meters in Berlin isn't a conspiracy."

"Better."

"And the rental van could still have been there by coincidence."

"It hasn't even been there yet."

Jonas nodded.

"Right."

Daniel pointed to the address.

"Publicly traceable?"

"Register and rental property through the company address. Yes."

"That's better."

"Still no vehicle at the garage."

"Correct."

Lena ended her call.

"Colleagues can keep the public approach under discreet observation. No technical tracking. No search. First we see whether the vehicle appears at all."

Daniel looked at her.

"On what basis?"

"Current plate from the source warning plus a publicly confirmed garage in the vicinity of the current event-logistics area. Narrow observation of a public area. Nothing more."

Daniel nodded.

"How fast?"

"They're on the way."

"Time?"

7:46 p.m.

Thirty-four minutes until the warning window began.

Jonas stood.

"If the van shows up, is that enough?"

Lena looked at Daniel.

"Depends what it does."

"That seems to be the answer to everything today."

"Don't get used to it."

Daniel looked at the garage again.

The information from his rule violation had led them there.

But from this point on, every new step had to stand on something else.

He said it aloud.

"From here on, nothing solely because of my hit."

Daniel stayed with the sentence.

**From here on, nothing solely because of my hit.**

He wrote it across the top of Jonas's working sheet.

Jonas looked at it.

"So we forget where the garage came from?"

"So we don't forget."

"Difference?"

"If the current connection can't support itself, then the garage is only a lead produced by a problematic access."

Daniel drew a line beneath the legacy-record note.

Below it, he started a new section.

**Independently confirmed in the current case.**

There was nothing beneath it yet.

Deliberately.

"And if nothing happens in twenty minutes?" Jonas asked.

"Then we don't have a solid new connection."

"Despite the two old hits?"

"Despite them."

Jonas looked at him briefly, then at the time.

Daniel realized how strongly he hoped B-QV 4172 would appear in front of the garage.

Not only because of the situation. If it did, his hit would no longer stand quite so alone in retrospect.

He recognized the desire and disliked it.

"If I start needing the outcome, tell me," he said.

Jonas raised an eyebrow.

"How am I supposed to know?"

"If I turn a maybe into a therefore."

Jonas nodded.

"I can do that."

Then he looked at him.

"Your hit?"

Daniel knew the moment had come.

"Later. Situation first."

Lena looked over.

She had heard the tone.

Daniel did not avoid her eyes.

At 8:07 p.m. the first report came in.

**B-QV 4172 on site.**

Daniel read it aloud.

Jonas sat down again.

"At the parking garage?"

"No. At the garage."

That was better than the source.

Or worse.

Because the source was no longer the only reason the location mattered.

Daniel asked where the report had come from.

Observation from a public area.

Plate visible.

No technique he could not support.

"Driver?"

"Still inside the vehicle."

"Contact?"

"None."

"Then wait."

Jonas looked at the time.

"Warning window's running."

"It's not an order."

Twelve minutes later, the second report came in.

The driver got out. No visible contact. Went into the garage.

Daniel looked at Lena.

"Not yet."

"I didn't say anything."

At 8:26 p.m. the garage door opened.

A second vehicle drove out.

A dark minibus, not the white delivery van from the old case.

Jonas leaned over the phone receiving the field updates.

"Plate's coming."

It came.

Lena wrote it down and passed it on.

The response took four minutes.

Four minutes in which Daniel noticed how easily he was already turning the minibus into proof.

Garage from the legacy records.

Rental van from the source.

Second vehicle.

He pushed back against it.

"If the plate gives us nothing, it's still just a vehicle leaving a garage."

Jonas nodded.

"Yes."

The response came.

The minibus was registered to a subcontractor of the technical service provider linked to the two access badges recovered the night before.

Now there was a current connection.

Without the anonymous source.

Without Daniel's legacy-record hit as the sole basis.

Daniel had the association confirmed a second time through a separate current system. Registered keeper, company address, subcontractor relationship. Same subcontractor. No stale record, no echo from his own access.

"Now," he said.

Jonas understood. "Now the garage isn't only yours anymore."

Daniel disliked the wording, but it was accurate.

The problematic lead had produced a location. The current minibus gave that location its first independently supportable significance.

Lena stood.

"Now we can move forward."

Jonas looked at Daniel.

"That's it?"

"No," Daniel said. "Now the defensible part begins."

The words sounded almost like relief.

Daniel disliked that.

He was relieved because his rule violation had produced a place that actually mattered.

The danger itself had not become any smaller.

Lena stopped at the door.

"Daniel."

He knew what was coming.

"Later."

"No."

Jonas looked from one to the other.

Daniel checked the time.

8:31 p.m.

The minibus had not moved yet. Operational command was reviewing the new basis.

There were a few seconds now in which telling the truth would not delay anything operationally.

Daniel said, "I checked the plate from the warning against protected legacy records. Different case. No proper cross-case basis."

Jonas did not move.

Neither did Lena.

"When?"

"7:24 to 7:27."

"Who authorized it?"

"No one."

"What exactly did you pull?"

"Plate hits and existing vehicle associations. Three hits. One parking violation. Two shared time-and-location appearances with a vehicle from an old case."

"People, contacts, movement data?"

"Didn't expand. No contacts, no movement data."

Lena looked at him for a long time.

"And you're telling me now?"

Daniel could have said: because the lead has now been independently confirmed.

That was exactly the truth.

And exactly why it sounded like a justification.

"Yes."

"Why not immediately?"

"Because I wanted to know first whether the hit was worth anything."

"So you tested the usefulness first and disclosed the rule violation afterward."

Daniel was silent.

Jonas looked down at his notes.

The answer was there.

"Yes," Daniel said.

For a few seconds, the ventilation was the only sound in the room.

Lena picked up her phone.

"We move the current situation forward on a clean basis. After that, we talk about your access."

Daniel nodded.

"Document it now," she said.

"I will."

"Not later."

Daniel was already opening the note.

Time. Data set. Purpose. Scope. Result.

No softened language.

Lena left.

Jonas remained seated.

"You just did it?"

Daniel kept typing.

"Yes."

"Even though Lena said the basis wasn't enough?"

"Yes."

"And Heller was still checking?"

"Yes."

Jonas looked at the new vehicle connection.

"And now there really is something."

Daniel stopped with his hands over the keyboard.

"That doesn't make the access proper."

"I didn't say it did."

Daniel looked at him.

Jonas had learned that sentence from them.

Suddenly that was not reassuring.

The minibus began to move.

Daniel saved the note.

Then he picked up the new vehicle association.

"Now we work with what we have independently."

Jonas nodded.

He said nothing more.
