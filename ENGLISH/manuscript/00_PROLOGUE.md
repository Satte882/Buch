**Normalfall (German noun):**

A case handled according to the usual rules.

*A rule withstands everything,*

*except proof*

*that things work better without it.*

## Prologue

The rain ran over the slide of the pistol and pooled at the front sight.

A hand held the weapon steady enough.

A few meters away, a man stood in the rain. Dark clothes, shoulders slightly hunched forward. Behind him, a pale strip of glass and concrete blurred through the water streaming down the panes.

Under the canopy it was barely any drier. The wind drove the rain all the way to the wall. Water ran across the ground, past the shooter's shoes, and disappeared into the dark.

The man stood beyond the narrow strip of cover. His jacket was dark with rain. One hand hung at his side. The other couldn't be seen.

He moved only a little, and the pistol tracked him just enough to keep the muzzle centered on his chest.

Then he went still again.

Somewhere behind the glass front, a light came on and went out again. From somewhere on the grounds came a dull clang, metal on metal, directionless in the rain.

The man raised his right hand. Whether he said anything was lost in the rain.

The hand was empty.

His fingers were spread, not high enough for a clear gesture and not low enough to mean nothing.

The shooter did not move. Water ran over his knuckles and dripped from the base of the magazine.

The man's head was turned slightly to one side. Then he looked forward again.

The distance stayed the same.

Three meters. Maybe four.

Close enough to run. Far enough that the first step would not have been the last.

Then something changed.

The man's right arm dropped slightly. His weight shifted sideways. Not a step toward the shooter.

The pistol didn't have to follow the movement. It was already there.

The finger on the trigger tightened.

The man did not attack the shooter.

The shot cracked once, short and hard.

The slide snapped back. Water sprayed from the metal, and the empty casing vanished into the dark.

For a moment, the rain was the loudest thing again.

The man jerked and stepped backward as if someone had yanked him by the jacket. Then he fell. One knee struck the concrete first, then his shoulder. His body turned halfway onto its side and stayed there.

The gun stayed up.

The shooter took one breath through his mouth.

On the ground, the man barely lifted his head. One hand lay open on the concrete. The other had disappeared beneath his body.

The shooter moved his right foot half a step forward and stopped.

There was no second shot.

Only then did he step out of the shadow beneath the canopy.

Daniel Reuter looked down at the man on the ground. Water ran over his forehead and into his collar.

He did not lower the pistol.
