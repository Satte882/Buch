## 24

"The work phone wasn't with Weber."

Jonas said it as he walked past.

Daniel needed a second to understand.

"When?"

"Both evenings."

"How do you know?"

Jonas set his tablet on Daniel's desk.

An internal handover list from the subcontractor. Old-fashioned. Names, dates, signatures.

The phone was registered to Nils Weber but had been handed off as the on-call phone during night shifts.

Both times to other employees.

Daniel took the tablet.

"Signatures genuine?"

"HR confirmed the shifts. Two employees confirmed them too."

"Could have been filled in afterward."

"Could have. That's why I pulled the device records the employer keeps for internal assignment."

Jonas opened the next view.

At the start of each shift, the device had been signed in under the account of the employee on call. Not proof of every minute. But consistent with the handover list.

"Who had it when the call went to Mika's number?"

Jonas gave him a name. It was not Weber.

"Why did he call?"

"Still open."

Daniel did not want to close the point immediately.

"The 1:14 a.m. access?"

"Not Weber either."

Jonas swiped on.

The access badge was personalized. But the reader at the side door had been running in emergency mode that night. Security had issued several shift badges in a batch.

"Who got Weber's badge?"

"So far all we know is: not Weber."

"Video?"

Jonas opened the clip.

The camera showed the side door at an angle. A man in a cap held the badge to the reader. His face was only partly visible. The man was not Weber.

Daniel now knew Weber from the staff photo.

Height, build, gait did not match.

"Security?"

"Confirmed the batch issue. Documentation's bad. Three badges got mixed up within the same hour."

Daniel looked at the two images.

A man in a cap. Again, not Weber.

"Why was it recorded under him?"

"Because his access was stored as shift supervisor and the badge was issued in his name."

"And the second phone connection?"

"Same on-call phone. Different shift employee."

Daniel leaned back.

The three strongest points against Weber did not collapse all at once.

They became ordinary one by one.

Work phone: shared.

Night access: wrong user.

Phone contacts: on-call duty.

The harmless explanation had not merely existed.

It had been right.

Daniel opened Weber's shift schedule himself.

On one of the evenings, Weber was demonstrably at another branch. Access there at 8:04 p.m., meeting record until 10:15. Reaching the suspicious location afterward would barely have fit the timing.

"Why didn't we have this yesterday?"

Jonas looked at him. "Because we had the hits first."

"That's not an answer."

"Because the other branch wasn't in our first project scope. The HR response only came today."

Daniel nodded.

The new route had been faster, but not complete.

"Where's Weber?"

Jonas hesitated.

"At home."

"Why?"

"His employer removed him from the event project yesterday."

Daniel looked at him.

"Based on whose information?"

"Security review. Our case was part of it."

"Only from the project?"

"Temporarily no night shifts either. Employer decision."

"Paid?"

"Don't know."

Lena was standing in the doorway.

She had apparently heard enough.

Daniel looked at the successful operation from the night before, still open on his second monitor.

"The success still stands," he said.

"Yes."

"The other hit was real."

"Yes."

"The wider review group led us to the storage unit."

"Yes."

He liked that she did not help him.

"And Weber was wrong."

"Yes."

Daniel picked up his phone.

"I want to talk to him."

Lena raised an eyebrow. "Why?"

"Because our assessment got him removed from the project."

"Then talk to him. But he doesn't owe you a useful scene."

Daniel looked at her.

"What's that supposed to mean?"

"That he doesn't have to react calmly, reasonably, and gratefully just so you can categorize the damage more comfortably."

Before calling Weber, Daniel spoke to his employer.

The division manager was polite and visibly careful not to say the wrong thing.

"The security assessment has been corrected," Daniel said.

"We received it."

"Then Mr. Weber can return to the project?"

A short pause.

"That's an internal personnel decision."

"Based on what current information?"

"We've already reassigned the shifts."

"That doesn't answer the question."

"Mr. Reuter, I'm not putting someone back into a sensitive area today after a security office told me yesterday to remove him, even if a correction arrives today."

Daniel heard the logic.

It was not absurd.

That was exactly what made it so uncomfortable.

"For how long?"

"Until we've reviewed it internally."

"What are you reviewing if the specific association has fallen away?"

"That we haven't missed anything."

Daniel closed his eyes briefly.

The correction had triggered the same sentence he himself had been working with for days.

Better to check once more.

"And the night shifts?"

"Already assigned for next week."

"Can that be reversed?"

"It can. Whether we do is another question."

Daniel thanked him and ended the call.

The damage was not spectacular.

No one had publicly branded Weber.

It was enough that a correct correction moved through one organization more slowly than the earlier security concern had.

Weber did not come in. They spoke by phone.

His voice was higher than Daniel had expected.

"You told my boss I was a security risk."

"We reported an anomaly that did not hold up."

"Great."

"The correction was sent as well."

"Even better."

"It's already been sent."

"Then I can just go back tomorrow, right?"

Daniel knew the answer now.

"Your employer is still reviewing it internally."

Weber laughed once.

"Of course."

"I spoke to him."

"That makes it much better."

"No."

The answer came out faster than Daniel expected.

Weber was silent for a moment.

Then he became angrier.

Not controlled anger. He jumped between accusations, money, and his work schedule.

"I'm not getting the shifts back. They've already put someone else on next week."

"I can't decide that."

"But you could decide the other thing."

Daniel said nothing.

"You know what my boss said? Until this is cleared internally, he doesn't want me on the site. It's cleared with you now. Not with him."

"The correction says the grounds for suspicion against you were not substantiated."

"Grounds for suspicion." Weber laughed shortly. "Great phrase."

Daniel let him talk.

"My wife thinks I did some kind of shit because my boss isn't allowed to tell me what's going on. So I get to explain that everything's fine while I'm sitting at home."

"Mr. Weber—"

"Don't."

"I only wanted—"

"What? To apologize?"

Daniel was silent.

Weber waited.

"I can tell you that the specific association was wrong," Daniel said. "And that we passed on the correction."

"I know that now too."

"I can't promise you anything more at the moment."

"That's exactly the problem."

The call did not end immediately.

Weber breathed audibly.

Then he said, "My son asked yesterday why I'm not allowed to work. I told him because adults make mistakes sometimes. He thought I meant me."

Daniel closed his eyes briefly.

Weber added at once, "And no, you don't have to feel bad now. That won't get me my shifts back either."

Then he hung up.

Daniel kept the phone at his ear for another moment.

After the call, Daniel opened the correction's distribution chain.

Everything on their side was correct.

**Association with Weber not substantiated. Security assessment corrected.**

Sent.

Received.

Acknowledged.

Three green status markers.

Daniel opened the employer's shift overview to the extent it was available in the case.

Weber was no longer assigned to the event project for the next few days.

No contradiction in the system.

Only two different realities, both correctly documented.

The security office had withdrawn its assessment.

The employer had reacted to the earlier assessment and changed its staffing plan.

Nothing required the second organization to reverse its decision at the same speed.

Daniel looked at the three green checks.

That was what a successful correction looked like technically.

And Weber was still at home.

In the after-action report, Daniel did not write only **misattribution corrected**.

Underneath, he added:

**Downstream effects outside the originating case are not automatically reversible.**

Then he had the time confirmed when the correction reached the employer.

Timestamp: 10:14 a.m.

The new shift plan had been approved at 9:52.

Twenty-two minutes.

No bad intent. No system failure.

Only a decision that had taken effect earlier in another process than the later correction did.

Daniel added both times to the after-action report.

The twenty-two minutes did not explain the harm. But they showed how quickly a provisional assessment could develop a life of its own once another organization acted on it.

After speaking with Weber, Daniel did not open the next case immediately.

He walked through the correction path himself.

Security assessment changed.

Employer informed.

Internal project access: restriction removed.

Shift schedule: outside their control.

Personnel decision: outside their control.

Family: not a system field at all.

On the screen, the correction looked almost complete.

In Weber's life, it was not.

Daniel did not call the employer again. He would only have created the same pressure in the opposite direction that he had criticized before.

Instead, he added an explicit note to the project record that the person-specific grounds for suspicion were **not substantiated** and must not be carried forward as a residual flag in later reviews.

Jonas saw the sentence.

"So he disappears from our group."

"From this suspicion."

"And if something new comes later?"

"Then it's new."

After the call, Daniel pulled the timeline back. Work phone. Night access. Shift overview. At every point, there had been a harmless counter-reading. He had seen it. He had simply weighted it less heavily as the danger of not acting became more real.

The wider review group had genuinely worked. Weber had still been wrongly burdened. Both remained true.

The problem had not been the absence of a counterhypothesis. It had been what that counter-reading was still worth in Daniel's calculation.

Beside two early **open** markers, he wrote:

**Counter-reading existed; its weight declined over time. Why?**

Lena read over his shoulder.

"That's the better question."

Daniel nodded.
