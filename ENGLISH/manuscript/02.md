# 2

The first thing in Ahrens's favor came at 2:37 p.m. By then, two calls back to security had gone unanswered; the vehicle assignment was still pending.

Daniel was on his way to another meeting when Jonas intercepted him in the hall.

"Ahrens had a job there."

Daniel stopped.

Jonas held up a tablet. "His company is handling deliveries for an event next week. He was on-site to document measurements and access routes."

"Confirmed by whom?"

"The company."

Daniel started walking again. Jonas went with him.

"That's one source."

"I knew you'd say that."

"Then spare me the victory lap."

"What victory lap?"

Jonas was openly grinning now.

They turned into a small conference room. Daniel took the tablet from him.

Work order. Date. Contact. Scope of work. Nothing suggested it had been fabricated after the fact. Daniel read the page twice anyway.

The order was twelve days old. Not from today. The internal project number appeared in two older company emails Jonas had also obtained. Ahrens was named in both as the technical contact.

Daniel tapped the messages.

"The company sent you these?"

"Yes. Forwarded from their system. Full headers, original timestamps visible."

Jonas sat on the edge of the table.

"You can just say it looks good."

"It looks good."

"Thank you."

"It's just not finished yet."

Jonas rolled his eyes.

"Security?"

"They found the appointment too. Ahrens was registered. Just not with the guard who wrote the report."

Daniel set the tablet on the table.

"Who had the registration?"

"Event coordination. Next week's list hadn't been rolled into the regular daily roster yet."

Daniel nodded. The guard could have missed it—or might not have been able to see it at all.

Daniel took out his phone and called the number listed for event coordination. A woman answered, asked first for the project number and then for Ahrens's name.

"Mr. Ahrens was here on Thursday," she said. "Measuring the access route, turning radius, gate height, bollard spacing."

Daniel looked at Jonas.

"And Tuesday?"

"That was the first site walk with the construction manager. Why?"

"Did he take pictures?"

"I'm sure he did. That's what he was there for."

"Of the bollard too?"

A short pause.

"If he's doing his job, I hope so."

Daniel thanked her and hung up.

Jonas wasn't smiling anymore. He looked at Ahrens's photograph.

"And the question about after eight?"

"Delivery window. His crew sets up the night before."

"Who confirms that?"

Jonas opened the schedule. "Organizer. Setup runs until eleven p.m. The large truck is supposed to come after eight because the yard is full before then."

"The van?"

Jonas tapped a second file.

The company's vehicle assignment records placed Ahrens at another setup that evening, less than two streets away. Driving time between the locations: four minutes.

Daniel looked at the license plate.

"Was it his van?"

"One of nine identical company vans. The one two streets away was his."

"Proven?"

Jonas slid the digital trip log across. Key checkout, return, project address. Plus a gas receipt from the same area at 7:26 p.m.

Daniel laid the tablet flat on the table. With every document, the case got more boring, and Daniel liked boring explanations.

"So close it," Jonas said.

"Not yet."

Jonas raised both hands. "Of course not."

Daniel called the building contact himself. No answer the first time. Ten minutes later, the man called back. Ahrens was due the following Monday to test the access route with a larger vehicle. The photographs were part of his preparation.

The contact even remembered Ahrens complaining about the retractable bollard.

"He said if we don't lower it in time, his truck stays outside," the man said. "That's why he took the pictures. He wanted to show his driver."

Daniel also asked about the evening setup.

That checked out too.

Daniel ended the call.

"Now it's closed."

Jonas leaned against the table. "If we'd pulled him over yesterday, probably nothing would've happened."

Daniel looked at him.

"Probably."

"I just mean—"

"I know what you mean."

Jonas rubbed his thumb along the edge of his ID card.

"Patrol asks him why he's there. He shows the work order. End of story."

"Maybe."

"You think they'd have made more of it?"

"I think we don't know."

Daniel reopened the case and reset its status. No further action. No extension of the review. No sentence like *keep under observation as a precaution*.

Things like that had a way of sticking around. Residual suspicion without a reason, because no one wanted to be the person who closed something for good.

The form offered him a follow-up flag. Four weeks. No new intervention, just another look if more information came in by then.

Daniel left the box unchecked.

Jonas noticed.

"Why not at least set a follow-up?"

"For what?"

"In case something else comes in."

"If something else comes in, something else comes in."

"And if it doesn't?"

"Then I have the same closed case back on my desk in four weeks."

Jonas looked at the empty box.

"It's harmless."

"To whom?"

"To him. He never knows."

Daniel looked at him.

Jonas made a face. "Yeah. All right."

"No. That's exactly where it starts. If we have nothing left, we don't need an administrative tail just to make closing it feel less final."

He clicked on.

He opened the field for the closing rationale.

The standard form offered several neutral phrases. **Tip not substantiated. No further review warranted at this time.**

Daniel changed the second sentence.

**The original grounds for suspicion are accounted for by the confirmed work order, event-related purpose, and vehicle assignment. No further case-specific review.**

Jonas read over his shoulder.

"More explicit."

"It's supposed to be."

"If somebody sends in the same tip again tomorrow?"

"They'll see what we already know."

"And if something new comes in tomorrow?"

"Then it's new."

He wasn't erasing Ahrens from the world. He was only taking him out of this case.

Daniel reopened his own first note from the day before.

**Photographs. Question about evening operations. Vehicle seen in the area again.**

Three lines that still looked unsettling together if you didn't know the explanations beside them.

He didn't add a fourth. Under each one he entered the confirmed explanation: work order, delivery window, another setup in the same area.

Jonas watched.

"You could just delete the old note."

"No."

"Why?"

"Because the first assessment existed."

Daniel saved both versions.

"If we keep only the clean conclusion, later it looks like this was obvious from the beginning."

Jonas nodded slowly.

The mistake wasn't in checking Ahrens.

It would have been in turning the fact that they'd checked him into a reason to keep hold of him.

"The guy might have been pulled over today," Jonas said.

Daniel didn't answer.

"Officers asking why he's taking pictures outside a federal government building. Maybe searching the van if something doesn't fit. His company hears about it. Security too."

"Yes."

"All because a guard understood his question differently."

Daniel pushed the tablet back to him.

"Not because of the guard."

Jonas waited.

"Because of us, if we'd made more of it than we had."

Jonas nodded slowly.

He looked at Ahrens's photograph again.

"He knows nothing about any of this."

"If everything went right, no."

"Strange feeling."

"Why?"

"We're sitting here for an hour deciding whether to make his life more complicated, and he's probably somewhere unloading cables."

Daniel picked up the papers for his next meeting.

"It's not a bad feeling."

Jonas looked at him, questioning.

"Just one you shouldn't get too used to."

That was all he said.

Daniel went to his next meeting.
