## 6

Mara said it before dinner was on the table.

"I got an offer from Hamburg."

Daniel stopped with the pan in his hand.

"An offer?"

"The job."

"What job?"

She looked at him.

"The one I told you about in June."

Daniel put the pan back on the stove.

"The leadership role?"

"Yes."

"I thought the interview was just exploratory."

"At first it was."

Mara took two plates from the cupboard. She did not look like someone waiting for a particular reaction. More like someone who had already said the sentence to herself a few times.

She set the plates on the table, picked them up again, and switched places because Daniel still had papers lying on her side.

"Can this go?"

Daniel looked at the folder. "Yes."

"How long has it been there?"

"No idea."

"Since Monday."

He picked it up and put it on a chair.

"And?" Daniel asked.

"And they want me."

Now she smiled.

So did he.

"That's good."

"Yes."

"Really good, actually."

Mara nodded. This time the pleasure showed more clearly. "It is."

Daniel picked up the pan again and served the food.

"What are they offering?"

Mara sat down.

"I'd build the team myself. Eight people, my own clients. Not coming into projects only after they're already half stuck."

Daniel asked about reporting line, budget, and hiring. Mara answered without hesitation: directly to the division head, partial budget responsibility, hiring with HR—the professional decision would be hers.

Daniel looked at her. "You want this."

"Yes."

"Not just because of the title."

"No."

"Good."

Mara took a bite. "That was almost a normal reaction."

"What's missing?"

"Nothing."

"That sounds suspicious."

"Eat."

They ate for a moment. Daniel asked about the team that did not exist yet. Mara told him about two people she would like to bring in from earlier projects and a client who had explicitly said the new unit should not once again be limited to fixing projects that were already going wrong.

She spoke faster than usual when she talked about work.

Daniel noticed.

"You've thought this through pretty far already."

"Of course."

"Since when has it been this concrete?"

"Since the second interview."

"When was that?"

Mara put down her fork for a moment. "Ten days ago."

Daniel said nothing.

"I told you it went well."

"Yes."

"You were on duty that night."

"Right."

"And I mentioned it again the next day."

Daniel remembered a conversation in the car. Or thought he did.

"Okay."

"That wasn't an accusation."

"Sounded a little like one."

"Then take ten percent accusation."

He smiled briefly.

"And the terms?"

"Of course you want the terms."

"What's wrong with that?"

"Nothing."

She said it too quickly.

Daniel sat down across from her.

Mara had worked for years at a larger planning firm. Projects, teams, clients, too many meetings. She rarely complained about it. When she wanted to change something, she usually changed it before other people had even realized she was dissatisfied.

"Salary?" Daniel asked.

She gave him a number.

He raised his eyebrows. "Not bad."

"No."

"Work from home?"

"Two days. Maybe three, but not at the beginning."

"Start date?"

"November first."

Daniel calculated automatically. Apartment. Train connection. Probationary period. Her current notice period.

"Berlin to Hamburg is manageable."

Mara put down her fork.

"Daniel."

He looked up.

"What?"

"I just told you I might be moving to Hamburg."

"Yes."

"And you're turning it into a train connection."

"What do you want me to do? Panic?"

"No."

She picked up her fork again but did not eat.

"I have to give them an answer by the eighteenth."

Daniel looked toward the calendar on the wall even though it held only a picture of the sea, no appointments.

"That's three weeks."

"Barely."

"For a decision like that."

"I didn't set the deadline."

"You don't have to say yes."

Mara looked at him.

He heard his own sentence again, this time with Mara's ears.

"That's not what I meant."

"Then what did you mean?"

"If they really want you, the deadline can be negotiated."

"I did. Two more days."

Daniel put down his fork.

"You've already negotiated?"

"Of course."

"And that's as far as they'll go?"

"They want to plan the build-out. The position's been open for months. I'm not the only person in the world who can do it."

"If they've sent you an offer, they've made their decision."

"Yes. And now I have to make mine."

Daniel nodded slowly.

"Okay."

"Okay what?"

He leaned back.

"We need to see how this would work for us."

Mara smiled briefly, without pleasure.

"Exactly."

"Long distance would be possible at first. I'm away often enough anyway. You'd be here two days, I could—"

"Stop."

Daniel fell silent.

"Not because any of that is wrong," she said. "I just don't want to build a weekly schedule in five minutes."

"You're saying you have to decide in three weeks."

"I have to decide about my job. And I need to know whether the two of us have any idea what comes after that."

"Nobody can give you a serious answer to that today."

"I'm not asking for one."

Daniel looked at her.

Mara pushed her plate a little farther away.

"I'm not asking whether you're going to follow me to Hamburg with a moving truck on November first."

"Good."

"I'm not asking you to quit your job either."

"Even better."

"Daniel."

"That was a joke."

"Make better ones."

Mara noticed it. "What?"

"Nothing."

"At some point I need an answer to whether we're treating this as a change in our life together, or as your normal life plus my new address."

Daniel looked at his plate.

"What's the concrete difference?"

"For example, whether we're talking about one year of long distance or three. Whether either of us is fundamentally willing to move our center of life later. Whether we even have the same plan."

"I can't tell you today whether I can move to Hamburg in a year."

"Neither can I."

"Then we're at the same point."

"No."

She did not say it sharply.

"Why not?"

"Because I still have to decide whether to take the job."

Daniel leaned back.

"Then take it if you want it."

Mara looked at him long enough for him to hear the sentence again.

"Your permission isn't what I'm missing."

"It wasn't meant as permission."

"I know."

She picked up her glass and drank.

"I'm not asking for a guarantee."

Daniel started to answer.

"But?"

He shook his head. "Nothing."

Mara set the glass down.

"I can't tell them I'll get back to them when your calendar eventually becomes more convenient."

"I'm not asking you to."

"No. You usually don't ask for anything. You leave things open until they sort themselves out."

"That's not true."

"Not always."

"Not now either."

"Good."

Daniel put down his fork.

"We'll talk about it."

Mara let out a quiet breath through her nose.

"Not sometime."

"I didn't say sometime."

"You usually say it without the word."

Daniel looked at her.

She held his gaze.

His mind was already running through the next week: two appointments Saturday, Sunday morning free, Monday full again. When he reached Monday, he stopped the calculation.

"Sunday," he said.

"Which one?"

"This one."

"Time."

Now he had to smile.

"You're serious?"

"Very."

He took out his phone and opened the calendar.

"Six p.m."

"Not eight after some appointment."

"Six p.m."

"And we're not just talking about how often the ICE runs."

"It runs pretty often."

Mara looked at him.

"All right."

He put the phone on the table.

"We'll talk about Hamburg. Berlin. One year, three years, whatever lies in between. Without either of us having to sign a guarantee today."

Mara nodded.

"I can live with that."

"That's all I wanted."

"I actually believe you."

Mara picked up her phone again, then paused.

"One more thing."

"What?"

"What if something comes in Sunday?"

Daniel looked at her.

"What would come in?"

"Work."

"Then work comes in."

"That wasn't an answer."

Daniel put down his phone too.

"If something genuinely urgent happens, I can't promise you today that I'll stay sitting here."

Mara nodded immediately. "I'm not asking you to."

"And if it isn't urgent, the appointment stays."

"Even if Berg messages you?"

"Even then."

"Even if you think you could solve it faster than someone else?"

Daniel had to think for a moment.

Mara noticed, of course.

"That's exactly the question," she said.

"I know."

"So?"

Daniel looked at the still-empty calendar entry.

"Sunday, six p.m. If nothing urgent happens, I don't move it."

Mara nodded.

"Good."

Mara picked up her glass.

"Sunday is enough for me, as long as you sit down with me before my deadline makes the decision for us."

Daniel nodded.

He liked that sentence less than he should have. Not because he wanted Mara to turn the offer down. Because he understood the appointment was not there to give him more time to decide.

The conflict was not gone. But that did not mean the evening had to end.

Afterward they talked about a colleague from Mara's office who had advised her to buy an apartment in Hamburg immediately even though she had not even accepted the offer yet. Daniel thought that was absurd. Mara did too. Later they cleaned up the kitchen together.

When Daniel took out the trash, his phone stayed on the table.

Mara did not look at it.

When he came back, the slot in his calendar was still open.

Mara picked up her own phone. A moment later, the appointment appeared on his.

**Hamburg. Not sometime.**

Daniel looked at the words on the screen.

"The title is aggressive."

"Deliberately."

This time she really smiled.
