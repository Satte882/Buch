## 40

Markus Heller lay on the wet concrete.

Beside him, the display of the small radio terminal was still lit.

Less than a minute earlier, Daniel had withdrawn the existing situation confirmation.

Not the entire operation.

One part of it.

A vehicle from the confirmed service chain had already been stopped. Two people were being checked. The next tactical step had been prepared.

Then the new report had come in.

From an office that had nothing to do with Heller.

The service area they had until then treated as largely clear of uninvolved people was not clear.

A last-minute personnel change. Eight employees from another service provider were still working there. Legitimately. Not in the old situation picture.

At first the new report had sounded like one of the corrections that came constantly during an active operation.

One service provider finishing later.

A handover postponed.

Personnel still on site.

Then the number.

Eight.

Daniel did not have the names read out. First he needed only the status.

"Confirmed legitimate?"

"Four clearly. Two through the shift schedule. Two still being clarified."

"Where exactly?"

The answer turned a side detail into an operational change.

The change sat precisely in the area whose investigative attribution from Daniel's strand was about to feed into the next step.

He opened the map and overlaid the new personnel trace.

The old picture had not suddenly become false.

It had become incomplete.

That was enough.

"Withdraw the confirmation," Daniel said.

Operational command asked whether he meant only the person attribution or the entire area.

Daniel forced himself not to answer faster than the question.

"The area. The offender picture remains confirmed. Clearance for this access route does not."

"Alternative route?"

"Being reviewed."

"The window is running."

"I know."

He heard several voices on the channel become shorter at once. No panic. Only the kind of concentration that appeared when a prepared sequence suddenly no longer rested cleanly on its latest information.

Daniel asked for twenty seconds.

Twenty seconds were not safe enough. Ten were too few and thirty might already be too many.

For the first time that night, he felt the rain at the back of his neck.

Daniel had looked at the screen and said immediately, "Confirmation withdrawn. Area not confirmed."

Operational command had asked again.

Daniel had repeated it.

"Area not confirmed. Twenty seconds. We pull the people out or change the access route."

Heller had been standing beside him.

Not as operational commander.

Not armed.

Only with the same secured access to situation communications he had had for days as coordinator.

"We don't have that time," he had said.

Daniel had looked at him.

"That's not your decision."

"No."

Heller had glanced at his terminal.

"But the previous assessment is solid."

"It was."

"The new information doesn't change the core threat."

"It changes who we may encounter there."

Heller had said nothing to that.

In Daniel's earpiece, operational command asked again for status.

Daniel answered, "Not confirmed. Hold."

Heller raised the terminal in his left hand.

Daniel saw the prepared message on the display.

**Target attribution from investigative strand confirmed. Tactical plan unchanged.**

Not an order.

But Daniel knew what the sentence would mean inside a sequence already running on the old confirmation.

Operational command would receive two contradictory professional assessments. In an operation already aligned to the earlier one.

"Leave it," Daniel had said.

Heller had looked at him.

Calm.

Almost tired.

"If we stop now, we lose them."

"Don't send it."

"Reuter."

"Don't."

Heller's thumb rested over the send field.

Daniel saw more than Heller's hand.

He saw the distance to him.

Three meters of wet concrete.

One step beneath the canopy, two out into the rain.

If Heller stayed still, the distance was ridiculously small.

If he completed the action with one movement, it was too large.

Daniel could move.

He could grab Heller by the forearm.

He could knock the terminal away.

At the same time he could tell the channel that any message from Heller on this matter was not part of the current investigative assessment.

None of those options was absurd.

That was exactly why they would matter later.

Daniel took one step.

"Markus. Keep the device down."

Heller looked at him.

"You're stopping the operation because of a personnel change."

"I'm stopping an old assessment."

"Same result."

"No."

"For the people outside, yes."

Daniel heard a query from operational command in his ear. One of the eight people had been located. Not all of them yet.

He pressed the talk button.

"The withdrawal stands."

Heller waited until Daniel released it.

"And if the vehicle is gone by then?"

Daniel did not answer immediately.

The question was legitimate.

That made it more dangerous.

"Then that's the cost of correcting the picture."

"Eight possibly uninvolved people against confirmed preparation."

"You're turning them into an equation."

"So are you."

Daniel felt the sentence land.

Not as revelation.

As fact.

Every decision that night had involved weighting something.

The only difference was which uncertainty a person was willing to carry.

Heller lowered his eyes to the terminal again.

Daniel took the second step.

Now he could have lunged.

The rain made the concrete slick. Not so slick that a lunge was impossible. Only enough that Daniel could not treat the first step as a guaranteed closing of distance.

Heller was not in a fighting stance.

He was not attacking Daniel.

His body was almost still.

Only the thumb was positioned where one movement would be enough.

Daniel saw the edge of the terminal in Heller's hand, the distance between them, and the open space in between.

Three meters.

Maybe less.

A fast step, a hand on the forearm, a strike against the device.

All possible.

None certain to be fast enough.

It was equally possible that Daniel's withdrawal on the channel would override Heller's prepared message even if Heller sent it.

Daniel did not know how operational command would handle two contradictory professional messages in exactly those seconds.

He knew only that his withdrawal was already out there.

Which meant the option of doing nothing physical also existed.

Heller glanced at the display.

Daniel heard his own breathing louder than the radio traffic.

Heller's thumb moved.

Daniel drew his weapon.

The shot.

Now Heller was on the ground in front of him.

Rain struck Daniel's face.

The terminal had slipped from Heller's hand and lay a meter away.

Daniel walked toward it without lowering the pistol.

The prepared message was still open on the display.

It had not been sent.

The open field on the terminal stayed in Daniel's vision.

Not sent.

He needed a moment to understand that those two words now mattered more than anything else about the device.

Operational command asked for status on the channel.

Daniel forced his voice into the same tone it had had before the shot.

"The previous assessment remains withdrawn. The new personnel information is sufficiently confirmed to require the correction. Other offender anchors unchanged."

"Understood."

"Heller?"

Daniel looked at him.

"Not part of the situation assessment."

He said nothing more.

The question of Heller now belonged to someone else.

Daniel took two steps back to give the teams coming from the building room.

Only then did he notice how tightly his right hand was clenched.

He opened it.

The channel continued in his ear.

Another uninvolved person located.

New route under review.

Vehicle status unchanged.

The case had not cared that something irreversible had just happened.

Daniel could not afford to either. Not yet.

Heller made a sound.

Daniel looked at him.

Blood ran dark through the water beneath his jacket.

Daniel did not holster the pistol.

"Medical to West Coordination," he said into the channel. "Gunshot wound. One person."

Heller turned his head slightly.

His mouth moved.

Daniel did not move closer to hear whether he was saying anything.

A door behind the strip of glass flew open. Two officers came outside.

Daniel stepped back.

Now he lowered the pistol.

He took another step back.

The world was full of ordinary procedures again.

Someone spoke with operational command.

Someone secured the terminal.

Someone asked Daniel's name even though everyone here knew who he was.

He answered automatically.

His eyes stayed on the wet concrete between himself and Heller.

Three meters.

Maybe less.

The distance had not become greater after the shot. It had only become measurable.

Daniel knew that later he would talk about exactly that.

Whether he could have run at Heller.

Whether he could have reached the terminal in time.

Whether his withdrawal on the channel would have been enough.

None of those possibilities had disappeared because of the shot.

They had only become impossible to test.

The next operational report came through his earpiece.

Daniel forced himself to listen.

The area with the eight employees was being reorganized.

Several people had now been located.

The alternative route had not yet been established.

The operation had not been won.

Nothing about Heller lying on the ground had solved the situation outside.

Daniel looked up.

"Who's consolidating the new situation assessment now?"

A voice named the responsible lead.

"My last confirmed basis is there in full—including the withdrawal?"

"Yes."

Daniel had the answer repeated.

He had heard it perfectly the first time. He needed the repetition as a clean point at which responsibility for the ongoing operation was back where it belonged.

One of the officers knelt beside Heller.

The other looked at Daniel.

"Your weapon."

Daniel knew what normally had to happen next.

Secure.

Hand over.

Separate.

Document.

Daniel knew the procedure after a shooting from regulations, exercises, and other people's reports.

Now it did not feel like a procedure.

The officer in front of him waited for the weapon.

At the same time, a situation was still running in Daniel's ear for which he held information no one else knew in exactly the same sequence.

He could not mix the two.

"My weapon goes to you," he said. "I'll give the final investigative assessment on the channel first. Then I'm out as soon as operational command takes over."

The officer gave a short nod.

Daniel realized he needed to explain the sentence to himself.

The shot gave him no additional authority.

If anything, it took authority away.

What was still needed from him was the last clean state of his investigative strand. He did not command the teams outside.

Daniel passed on the situation one sentence at a time.

He noticed he was speaking more slowly than usual.

Not less clearly. More slowly because every word now had two functions: the live operation had to be able to work with it, and later it had to remain reconstructable what Daniel actually knew at this moment.

"Confirmed offender anchors unchanged," he said. "Area assessment withdrawn because of new uninvolved-personnel information. Heller's counter-message is not part of my assessment."

Operational command confirmed each point separately.

Daniel waited for the final "understood."

Only then did he know the shooting would not compound itself through communication chaos.

He repeated the confirmed offender anchors and the withdrawn area assessment once more.

No interpretation of Heller.

No justification.

Only the status.

In his ear, Jonas said, "Daniel, we have the second location."

Daniel looked at the officer in front of him.

"Take the weapon."

He held it out by the grip with two fingers.

Then he pressed the earpiece more firmly into place.

"Go."
