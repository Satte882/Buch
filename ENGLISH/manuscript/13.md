## 13

The second message was shorter.

**B-QV 4172. Today, 8:20 to 9:00 p.m. Parking level 3, south. The vehicle belongs to the next step.**

**Don't check only the registered keeper.**

Then a blank line.

**5:46 p.m. Your sister left your mother's apartment alone.**

Daniel looked at the time.

6:38 p.m.

Less than an hour until the start of the window.

The first message had given him almost a day.

This one had not.

He did not call Jana.

He texted her.

**Were you at the apartment today?**

The reply came two minutes later.

**Yes. Realtor. Why?**

Daniel typed nothing.

Then:

**What time did you leave?**

Three dots appeared.

**No idea. Just before six. Daniel, what is this?**

He put the phone down.

5:46 p.m. fit.

Not exactly provable. Jana normally did not check the time as she left the apartment. But *just before six* was close enough to take the line seriously.

Daniel opened the realtor's appointment history Jana had sent him. Today's viewing was listed there. End: 5:30 p.m. Jana could easily have spent another few minutes gathering papers afterward.

That made 5:46 plausible.

It did not explain who had seen her.

The message could still have been constructed. Someone could have watched Jana. Someone could have watched Daniel. Both were bad. Neither confirmed the operational threat claim.

Daniel wrote the sentence down before his mind could merge the two.

**Observation of Jana does not confirm the operational claim.**

He picked up the work phone.

"Jonas."

"I'm not asleep, if that's what you're calling to ask."

"Plate B-QV 4172. Regular channels only. Registered keeper, current assignment, anything we can properly check under the current case."

"From where?"

"Source."

A short pause.

"The source?"

"Yes."

"On it."

Daniel opened the map.

Parking level 3, south.

Several garages could fit that description. The message also named an address Daniel had not read aloud. A few streets from a logistics area used for the event.

No proof.

But not random either.

He put three open routes side by side.

Check the plate through regular channels.

Check the courier company through open sources.

Cross-check the event connection through the contacts they had already established.

All legal.

All immediate.

Exactly what he had spent the past few hours preparing for.

At 6:46 p.m. the first vehicle response came back.

Plate valid.

Rental vehicle.

No current wanted hit.

At 6:51 p.m. Jonas called back.

"Plate exists. Rental vehicle. Rented yesterday to a small courier company."

Jonas went straight through the company data as well. Real company, seven years in the commercial register, three drivers, eight vehicles, ordinary website. Contract and payment ran through the business account; the driver was not clearly assigned.

"Current movement?"

"Not without more."

The proper routes gave him a real vehicle and a real company, but no significance.

"What about the old records?" Jonas asked.

Daniel did not answer immediately.

"Which old records?"

"Earlier you said that with the blue van we should check whether the same vehicles appear in other threat streams. Heller pulled contacts for that."

"Through the relevant offices, properly."

"Yes."

"Then that's how it stays."

Jonas heard the tone.

"Okay."

Daniel ended the call.

He knew there was a faster route.

The faster route would not give him a live location or some secret movement map. It would answer only one question inside protected data from a different case: had this plate already appeared there together with a relevant vehicle or at a relevant location?

Technically, the search would take seconds.

The cross-case basis was the problem.

Daniel did not open the system.

Not yet.

He called Lena.

"I need the fastest clean route for a cross-case plate check."

"What's the current basis?"

He gave her only the operational chain.

New source warning. Specific plate. Rental vehicle. Real courier company. Near the event-logistics area. No independent link to a threat.

Lena was silent for a moment.

"And the private line?"

Daniel looked at Jana's message.

"It has another accurate detail."

"Relevant to the plate?"

"Not demonstrably."

"Then it stays out. I'll check the authorization side."

Daniel hung up.

6:57 p.m.

He did not look at the clock. He looked at the line with the time window.

8:20 p.m.

Eighty-three minutes left.

He called the contact they already knew from event coordination.

The plate was not registered there. Neither was the courier company listed as a direct service provider.

No exoneration.

No incriminating point.

"The garage at that address?" Daniel asked.

"Not part of our site. Several companies in the area use it."

Another ordinary fact.

The source had chosen an address that could fit.

Still no proof why.

Daniel called the rental company through its public switchboard. The contract existed, the payment was unremarkable, and the vehicle had been picked up the day before. The employee could not tell him more without additional authority.

"Was there a return time?"

"Tomorrow morning. No planned mileage. I can't give you any additional drivers without further authorization."

Daniel thanked her.

Another proper route taken all the way to its limit.

Still no answer to the question the source had put in front of him.

At 7:03 p.m. Lena sent back the result on the last clean option.

**The current basis is not enough for cross-case use of the protected legacy records. Urgency is understood. We won't get a defensible authorization tonight.**

Daniel read the sentence.

Then again.

He called Berg.

No answer.

Again.

Voicemail.

He texted only: **Urgent authorization question. Call me.**

Then Heller.

Heller answered.

Daniel gave him only the new operational information.

"Can you check through your side whether the plate connects anywhere through regular channels?"

"I can ask."

"By when?"

"Not before eight. Maybe later."

Daniel looked at the time.

7:08 p.m.

"Window starts at 8:20."

"I know."

"The last warning was essentially right."

"I know that too."

Heller was quiet for a moment.

"Daniel, if I tell you this is enough now, I'd be saying something I can't substantiate."

It was the first time Daniel had heard Heller use his first name.

"Understood. Can you prioritize it?"

"Already have. I'm pulling what I can pull. Anything more would mean pretending we have something we don't."

Daniel said nothing.

Neither did Heller.

That was almost worse than being told to slow down.

"Call me," Daniel said.

"I will."

The call ended.

Daniel sat for a moment with both phones in front of him.

Private: Jana.

Work: open plate.

The source had put them in the same message.

Daniel separated them again.

He texted Jana:

**Everything okay with you?**

The reply came immediately.

**Yes. What's going on?**

He typed:

**I'll explain later. Please stay alert tonight. No specific indication of danger.**

He read it twice before sending.

No instruction to leave the apartment.

No claim that she was a target.

Only what he could honestly support.

7:10 p.m.

Daniel opened the list of regular review paths again.

Plate: exhausted.

Courier company: exhausted.

Event connection: exhausted.

Legacy-record authorization: denied on the current basis.

Heller: regular inquiry running.

Berg: unreachable.

There were still things he could do. He could wait. He could call again. He could hope one of the open offices responded before 8:20.

Those were real alternatives.

Just not alternatives whose outcome he controlled.

Daniel wrote the remaining options on a sheet of paper even though he knew them by heart.

**Heller – regular inquiry running.**

**Berg – callback pending.**

**Lena – authorization checked; current basis insufficient.**

**Jonas – regular vehicle/company checks exhausted.**

He left one line blank beneath them.

The blank line was for waiting, not for the protected records.

Waiting was no longer a non-decision. It was an option with its own consequence: the warning window could begin before any of the open offices responded.

Daniel wrote beside it:

**No new harm established.**

Then:

**No independent confirmation that the plate has threat significance.**

And underneath:

**Observation of Jana remains separate.**

He looked at the last sentence longer than the others.

It would have been easy to read it the other way. Someone was watching his sister, therefore the warning mattered more. Emotionally, that was instantly true.

Professionally, it was not.

Daniel did not cross the line out.

He simply drew a line between it and the operational list.

Then he picked up the work phone and called Berg a third time.

No answer.

Heller had no new update either.

Daniel did not make Jonas check the same public registers for a fourth time. Repetition was no longer diligence.

He stayed in front of the empty search field.

The clean alternatives still existed.

They were simply no longer in his hands.

That was exactly the point at which the faster, improperly authorized route began to feel like action and waiting like omission.

Daniel knew that feeling was not, by itself, a legal basis.

He also knew it would not disappear this time merely because he named it correctly.

Then he placed the private phone face down.

Daniel opened the internal system, passed the normal case view, and entered the protected legacy records he knew because he had worked another matter there months earlier.

The search field was empty.

At the top was the notice explaining permitted use.

Daniel put his hands on the desk.

He knew why this route was not clean.

He also knew why he had opened it at all.

Two days earlier, he would have closed it at this point and waited.

Now he thought of the security guard.

This time he thought of him as a fact, not an image.

Multiple fractures. No danger to life. A real cost in a situation where everyone had had defensible reasons not to move earlier.

That did not make the legacy records permissible.

It only meant waiting no longer felt neutral.

On the private phone, Jana's next message was waiting.

**Daniel?**

The work phone showed 7:11 p.m.

He did not type in the plate.

Not yet.
