## 18

The new message came at 11:18 p.m.

Daniel was alone again.

He had already locked the computer and was about to leave when the private phone vibrated.

Two lines.

**B-QV 4172. Three hits. You limited the search to the plate.**

**You decided before it was too late.**

Daniel stopped.

His jacket was already over his arm.

He put it back on the chair and unlocked the computer.

The important part was the limitation, not the plate.

Three hits.

Not two relevant hits.

Not *connection confirmed*.

Three.

The number Daniel had seen immediately after the search.

And *limited the search to the plate*.

The source now seemed as interested in how Daniel had restrained himself as in the outcome.

Daniel read both lines again.

The fact that he had searched only for plate hits and existing associations was in his note. Berg knew it. Lena too.

Jonas now knew the limitation from Daniel's disclosure.

After the operation, the situation summary had gone to a small coordination group that included Heller's area. Daniel did not know how much detail about his access had been included there.

Then there were technical roles that could see the access logs.

So there were no longer three possible paths.

More like five.

Maybe more.

Daniel still did not click on names.

He opened the timeline first.

When had his note been saved?

8:33 p.m.

When had Lena's separate documentation been created?

He could not see that.

When had the operational summary been sent?

10:41 p.m.

When had the message arrived?

11:18 p.m.

Enough time for an ordinary internal information path.

That mattered.

The message did not prove someone had been watching his screen live.

It proved only that someone had obtained a very specific detail of his actions before 11:18.

Daniel opened the access history for his own note.

Berg.

Lena.

Himself.

Two technical roles.

Later, an export into the protected internal review.

Heller's name did not appear.

The relief came too quickly. Daniel stopped it before it became a conclusion.

Heller's area could have learned different details through the operational summary. A name did not need to appear in this one list for someone to know the content.

Nor did Lena's name make her the source.

She had a legitimate reason to read the note.

So did Berg.

So did the technical roles.

The small circle was uncomfortable.

It still was not conclusive.

Daniel opened the distributed version of the operational summary.

His problematic access was not described there in full.

It said only that a cross-case vehicle lead had contributed to further review.

No number of hits.

No limitation.

That removed one convenient path.

Others remained open.

He searched for the internal-review version.

Not visible to him yet.

Lena had said: separate.

Daniel thought about her screen shortly before she left.

A separate workspace.

Not secrecy without purpose.

But an information path he did not control.

He closed the file.

The next easy hypothesis would have been Berg.

Berg had held the note in his hand.

Berg had an interest in making Daniel's success visible.

But interest was not leakage.

Then Heller.

Cross-agency coordinator. Access to parts of the operation. Direct number. He had pushed for faster review without removing boundaries.

Still not a lead.

Daniel did not create a suspect list.

Instead, he opened a new working document and created four fields.

**Operational information from the source.**

**What has been independently confirmed.**

**What the source knows about internal processes.**

**Who could legitimately have known that internal information.**

Better.

No person became the source automatically.

Only information paths.

Under the four fields, Daniel added a working rule.

**A person is not a source hypothesis merely because they sit on a possible information path.**

Then a second.

**Test source knowledge against access opportunity and timing.**

Jonas could not see the rules over the phone, but Daniel gave him the core of it.

"If the next message contains internal knowledge again, first we establish when that detail became available at all."

"And who could see it."

"After that."

"Why after?"

"Because a detail that existed only after the operational summary has a different audience from something known during my access."

Jonas was quiet for a moment.

"You don't want a suspect list."

"Not yet."

Daniel looked at Berg, Lena, the technical roles, Heller's area.

Names were seductive. They turned a messy information path into people with motives immediately.

He did not delete any names.

He simply placed them behind the timing question.

From now on, two reviews would run in parallel: what the source knew about the case would be tested like any other tip. What the source knew about Daniel and internal processes would be tested separately against possible information paths.

Neither track could automatically confirm the other.

He entered the new message.

Operational information: none new.

Internal knowledge: three hits; plate-only limitation.

Possible legitimate knowledge: Daniel, Lena, Berg, technical documentation; later onward-distribution paths open.

Then he stopped at the second line.

**You decided before it was too late.**

The message did not say *you were right* or *you saved people*. Not even *the access worked*.

Decided.

As if that had been the relevant part.

Daniel leaned back.

The source was not commenting on the find.

It was commenting on him.

His reaction to the situation.

That was different.

He opened the first message.

North door.

Pickup.

Event.

Mailbox.

Then the second.

Plate.

Jana.

Now the third.

Three hits.

Limitation.

Decision.

The source had never told him to break a rule.

It had only placed information so that eventually the question was sitting in front of him.

Daniel felt the muscles in his neck tighten.

He stood and went to the window.

Outside, the yard was almost empty. A single car drove through the gate. The guard raised the barrier.

Daniel thought about 7:24 p.m.

He could have waited.

The source did not have to know he would press Enter.

Maybe it had only counted on him doing it.

That was worse.

Predictable was not the same as controlled.

Daniel put the phone down again. First he wanted the information paths separated before drawing Jonas further into the operational work.

He opened two separate working documents.

The first contained only what was operationally needed: no new situation information, no new location, no new person.

The second contained what the source knew about Daniel's conduct.

**Three hits. Plate-only limitation. Timing after internal disclosure.**

He set different access permissions.

Not perfect. Not final. Only enough to keep convenience from pulling both layers back into the same view.

Then he checked what the separation cost him.

The operational document now lacked the sentence that made the source psychologically interesting. The provenance document lacked the live case context that made every internal access path immediately look suspicious.

Both views were worse for building a quick story.

That was deliberate.

Daniel wrote beside them:

**Separation creates follow-up questions. Follow-up questions are a cost, not a mistake.**

He read the sentence and changed **not a mistake** to **a deliberate cost**.

He did not know whether the separation would actually make the leak harder.

But at least it forced him to decide, each time information moved, what he was moving and for what reason.

He picked up the work phone and called Jonas again.

"Again?" Jonas said.

"Yes. The source."

"What does it want?"

Daniel was silent.

Jonas's tone sharpened.

"Daniel?"

"From now on, with every message we separate two things. Operational information, and information about what the source can know about us."

"Aren't we already doing that?"

"Not cleanly enough."

"What did it say?"

Daniel looked at the message.

"A detail about my access."

"Which detail?"

"Number of hits and the limitation."

Jonas was quiet briefly.

"I knew that afterward too."

They went through the obvious recipients: Lena, Berg, and probably the people who could see the access technically.

"Heller?"

Daniel looked at the empty space in his working document.

"Possible through coordination. Not established."

"So everyone."

"No. A small circle with several paths."

Jonas exhaled.

"And what do we do with that?"

"We don't change the operational picture because of a provenance hypothesis. But not everyone automatically gets the same full source picture anymore."

"You want to segment it."

"Yes."

The disadvantages were obvious: annoying, error-prone.

During the call, Daniel opened the existing distribution list. Four people currently received the same full source status. He did not simply remove a name. Instead he split the distribution into two groups: operational claims to the operations team; source knowledge only to the small review group, and only as needed for the specific review.

"What if that means you withhold exactly the information someone later needs?" Jonas asked.

"Then that's the cost."

"A bad one."

"And if the source notices we're reacting differently?"

Daniel paused.

"Then we've at least learned that it really is observing our response."

Jonas said nothing.

It was the first time Daniel's source review itself could begin to test the behavior of the other side.

No trick.

Just less uniform distribution.

Jonas remained quiet.

"What else did it say?"

Daniel read the second line.

"That I decided before it was too late."

"That's true."

Daniel looked out the window.

"That's the point."

Jonas said nothing.

"It's interested in how I decide," Daniel said.

The sentence sounded worse aloud than it had on the screen.

"Maybe it's just praising you," Jonas said.

"Maybe."

"You don't believe that."

"I don't know."

"And we still use its next tips?"

Daniel did not answer immediately.

That was the actual decision.

He could cut off the channel.

Block the number. Preserve messages only for technical analysis. Stop giving any operational response the source might read as feedback.

That would be cleaner for the source question.

And perhaps stupider for the case.

"We don't ignore verifiable information just because the source may want something from me," Daniel said.

"So yes."

"Yes. But we review the two layers separately."

"Double game."

Daniel disliked the term.

"Division of work."

Jonas laughed briefly.

"Sure."

"Seven-thirty."

"I know."

Daniel ended the call.

Then he opened the source file.

Until now it contained origin, timestamps, confirmed information, discrepancies.

He added two separate sections.

**A – operational claims**

**B – knowledge of internal processes / Daniel**

The new message went almost entirely into B.

Under A, there was only: no new operational claim.

Daniel added a new point.

**The source may be observing: the situation and decision behavior.**

He read it.

Too abstract.

He deleted it.

Then he wrote:

**The source is interested in how I decide.**

He left that sentence.

He locked the computer.

The jacket was still on the chair.

Before picking it up, he looked at the private phone one last time.

No new message.

For the first time that evening, that was not a relief.

The question was no longer only how the source knew what it knew. Just as important was what it wanted Daniel to do with that knowledge.
