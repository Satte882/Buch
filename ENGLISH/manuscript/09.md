## 9

"What can we verify without believing anything the sender says?"

Lena had read only the first three lines of the message.

Daniel had not brought the sentence about the mailbox.

They were sitting in a small conference room. Jonas was outside working through the delivery data. Printouts from the warehouse complex lay on the table beside Daniel's notes.

Lena pushed the sheet back toward him.

"Not: what sounds plausible. What can we verify?"

Jonas listed them: the pickup; the connection between the delivery address and the gray van, if they could identify it; and whether Unit 17 was connected to a service provider for the event.

Lena looked up.

"What event?"

"It doesn't say."

"Then we can't verify that right now."

Daniel nodded.

Lena took a pen and drew three columns on a blank sheet.

**Claim. Verification. Result.**

"The source doesn't get its own column," she said.

"Why?"

"Because the source isn't a result."

Daniel looked at the first line of the message.

"North door and yellow strip?"

"Confirmed as an observation. Not confirmed is the claim that it wasn't accidental."

Daniel wrote it down.

"Pickup?"

"Open."

"Public event?"

"Too vague."

Lena pushed the sheet toward him.

"There. Less thriller, more work."

"Disappointing."

"You'll survive."

The door opened.

Jonas came in with the laptop under his arm.

"The booking doesn't exist."

Daniel looked at him.

"You sure?"

"For Unit 17: no pickup tomorrow between two and two-thirty. Nothing entered today either."

Lena leaned back.

"Then we have our first point."

Jonas set the laptop on the table.

"Not yet."

He turned the screen around.

"The property manager sent me the schedule for the whole section. Unit 16 has a pickup tomorrow at 2:20 p.m. Courier service. One package."

Daniel read the line.

"What's Unit 16?"

"Importer of tool accessories. Been there nine years. Nothing unusual."

"Booking time?" Lena asked.

"This morning. 10:46."

Daniel looked at the anonymous message.

It had come in at 11:18.

"Could the booking be public?"

"No. Landlord portal."

"Could someone at Unit 16 be the source?"

Jonas shrugged. "Can someone at Unit 16 make a phone call? Yes."

"So nothing yet."

"Wait."

Jonas clicked on.

"The courier didn't get the order from Unit 16."

Lena looked at him.

"From who?"

"The company in Unit 17."

The room went quiet.

Daniel pulled the laptop closer.

"Where did you get that?"

"Not just from the portal. I called the courier back."

"Which number?"

"Public switchboard. Not the one in the booking."

"Who gave you the information?"

"Dispatch. I gave them the job number. No source, no threat context."

Daniel nodded.

Better.

"Why is the pickup under 16, then?" Daniel asked.

"The order says: access via north loading ramp, customer Unit 16. But the billing party is the trading company from 17."

Daniel left both interpretations open: mistake or intent.

"What does the courier say?"

"Customer specified the ramp that way. To them it's just pickup location plus billing party."

"Who booked it?"

Jonas turned the screen back. "Added by phone after the order was created online. The name matches one of the contacts from Unit 17."

Lena raised her pen.

"Matches, or confirmed?"

Jonas paused.

"Matches. Not independently confirmed yet."

"Then write exactly that."

Daniel looked at Lena.

She did not look back.

Jonas typed.

Daniel picked up the sheet with the message.

**Tomorrow between 2:00 and 2:30 p.m., a pickup will take place there that is not booked to Unit 17.**

Not approximately.

Not sometime in the afternoon.

2:20 p.m.

Wrong unit. Right billing party.

"One layer deeper," Daniel said.

Jonas knew what he meant.

He called the property manager again and had him confirm that Unit 16 was in fact allowed to share the loading ramp for external pickups. No secret access point. No unusual entry route. Just an administrative convenience used by several tenants.

That made the booking more ordinary.

It did not make the prediction any less precise.

"Does the company in 17 regularly book through 16?" Daniel asked.

Jonas checked the previous three months.

"Once before. Six weeks ago. Similar setup."

Lena looked at Daniel.

"That argues against the wrong unit being especially significant."

"Yes."

"And for the source actually having information."

"Also yes."

That was the problem.

With every harmless detail, the pickup itself became less suspicious.

With every confirmed detail, the sender became harder to ignore.

Jonas looked at the timestamps.

"The message came thirty-two minutes after the booking. So not before."

"Right. The source doesn't have to have planned the pickup. They may simply have seen it very quickly."

Daniel nodded.

That was an important boundary.

"Exactly. We know the source had access to a fresh detail. We don't know why that detail exists."

Beside the three-column sheet, Lena made a small separate note under **Source**.

**Information proximity: high. Motivation: open.**

"That's all we know about them," she said.

"Not yet," Jonas said.

Daniel looked at him.

"Not yet isn't a finding."

Jonas raised his hands. "Got it."

Lena had Jonas show her the order data again and walked through the possible information paths. Landlord portal. Courier dispatch. Unit 16. Unit 17. At each point, several people could have seen the order.

"That's important," she said.

"Why?" Jonas asked.

"Because insider doesn't automatically mean someone inside the security apparatus. We have a narrow information space. Not a narrow group of people."

Daniel did not write the sentence down. He needed it anyway.

Lena looked at him.

"That makes the information true."

"Part of it."

"Exactly."

Jonas sat down.

"So the source has access to the landlord portal?"

"Or the courier," Daniel said.

"Or Unit 17."

"Or someone who knew about it."

Jonas exhaled.

"Extremely helpful."

"It only stops you from turning the first explanation into the only one."

Lena tapped a finger on the message.

"Informed is not the same as trustworthy."

Jonas looked from one to the other.

"Sounds like something you've both said before."

"Because it's true more than once," Lena said.

Daniel took Lena's three-column sheet.

Under **Result** for the pickup he wrote:

**Time window confirmed. Booking not under 17. Billing party 17. Significance open.**

Beside it, under the source question:

**Access to nonpublic booking details highly likely. Origin unclear.**

He read the two lines one after the other.

The first concerned the investigation.

The second was a new problem.

Daniel opened the existing case notes.

"We elevate the matter. Through regular channels. We cover the pickup tomorrow, then establish properly where the route leads."

"Surveillance?" Jonas asked.

"Only what the current facts support."

"What does that mean for tomorrow?"

"Keep the courier and the pickup point under observation within the authorized scope. No search just because the source claims there's more behind it."

"And if the hard-shell case is gone after that?"

Daniel looked at him.

"Then a hard-shell case is gone, as long as we don't have more than that."

Jonas did not like the answer. He nodded anyway.

Lena stayed seated after he left the room.

"What didn't you show me?"

Daniel looked at her.

"What makes you think that?"

"Because you printed the sheet even though you normally bring everything digitally. And because there's three centimeters more margin at the bottom than at the top."

Daniel looked at the printout.

He had removed the last line before printing.

"Private reference."

Lena waited.

"Nothing yet that I want to circulate widely."

"Threat?"

"No."

"Sure?"

Daniel thought about the mailbox.

"No."

Lena said nothing for a while.

"Is the private part connected to the claim about tomorrow?"

"Not demonstrably."

"Then don't treat it as if it is."

"I'm not."

"But you removed it before showing me."

Daniel looked at the cropped margin.

"Because I don't know how far the information path reaches."

"That can be a good reason."

"Doesn't sound like one."

"It's not a blank check either."

Daniel leaned back.

Lena looked at him.

"Is the private part affecting your assessment?"

Daniel looked at the three columns.

"It increases the pressure on me."

Lena nodded.

"Then that belongs in our working relationship. Not as evidence. As a possible distortion of your threshold."

Daniel nodded.

The private line did not suddenly become part of the case.

But the way he was handling it was no longer purely private.

"Then separate it cleanly," she said. "Case and source."

Daniel opened two new entries in the case file.

For the first, he set the status to **substantiated lead for review**.

For the second he wrote only:

**Source unknown. Proximity of access unclear. Separate risk.**

He did not quote the private line there. He noted only that there was a personal reference involving his private life and that it had been preserved separately.

Then he saved the two entries separately.

Lena's three-column sheet remained on the table.

Claim. Verification. Result.

For the source itself, there was still nothing that justified trust.

Only truth.
