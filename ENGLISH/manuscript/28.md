## 28

"What do you know?"

Jana did not say hello.

Daniel stood in the empty corridor.

Beyond the fire door at the far end, he could hear a cart rolling across the floor. Nothing else.

"That someone saw you leave the practice through the rear exit today at 6:32 p.m."

Silence.

"Who?"

"I don't know."

"Why would someone tell you that?"

Daniel leaned one shoulder against the wall.

"It came with a tip connected to my case."

"Is the tip real?"

"Part of it."

"Which part?"

"A new delivery authorization exists. We don't know whether it has anything to do with a threat."

Jana was quiet briefly.

"And what do you think?"

Daniel closed his eyes.

It was exactly the question he asked other people.

"That the sender wants to put pressure on me. And that a real threat could still exist behind it."

"That's not an answer about me."

"No."

"So again: what do you know about me?"

Daniel opened his eyes.

"That someone knew about the rear exit and saw you there today."

"More?"

"No."

"What do you think?"

"That it's connected to my case."

"How sure?"

"I'm not."

"Good."

Daniel felt immediate resistance to the word.

"That doesn't mean you should ignore it."

"I didn't say that."

"Don't go home alone tonight."

"I already did."

"Then don't stay there."

"Why?"

"Jana."

"No. What do you know that makes my apartment a problem?"

Daniel pressed thumb and forefinger against his forehead.

"Nothing specific."

"Then don't tell me to leave my apartment as if that were a fact."

"Someone knows your routes."

"Then I change my routes."

"That might not be enough."

"A hotel might not be enough either."

Daniel said nothing.

Jana was not being reckless.

That would have been easier.

She was making exactly the distinction he had just defended in the conference room: known, assumed, open.

"What were you planning to do tonight?" he asked.

"Go home, eat, first appointment tomorrow at eight."

"Cancel it."

"Already did."

Daniel straightened.

"When?"

"While you were probably still deciding whether to call me."

He was silent.

"Claudia noticed I was bothered," Jana said. "I told her someone might be watching my routines."

"You told her about the case?"

"No. About me."

The correction landed.

"What are you doing?"

"She's picking me up. I'm not driving my car tomorrow. I canceled the first appointment; she's taking the second. I'm not sleeping at home tonight."

"Who knows besides Claudia?" Daniel asked.

"No one who needs to know where I'm staying."

"Staff?"

"They only know I'll be late tomorrow or not in at all."

"Family?"

"No."

"And if Claudia can't drive you?"

"Then I take a cab and change the destination if anything feels wrong."

Daniel noticed himself automatically testing her answers for weaknesses.

They were not bad answers. Daniel kept searching anyway for a place where he could take control again.

"Do you have cash?" he asked.

A short silence.

"What?"

"In case you have to turn off the phone—"

"Daniel."

He stopped.

"Yes."

"You're on escape plan number seven now."

"Three."

"That doesn't make it better."

He exhaled briefly.

"Okay."

Jana said, "I don't need a complete operational plan from you. I need an honest update when you know more."

"You'll get one."

"Even if the update is: we still know nothing more about me."

Daniel was silent for a moment.

"Even then."

Daniel let out some of his breath.

Not enough.

"Where?"

"With someone I know."

"Claudia?"

"Maybe."

"Jana."

"No."

"No what?"

"I'm not giving you the address."

Daniel pushed away from the wall.

"Why?"

"Because you just told me you don't know who is getting information about me."

The sentence stayed between them.

"My phone isn't the source file."

"I didn't say it was."

"Then what?"

"That having fewer people know my location while nobody knows where the information is coming from isn't completely crazy."

Daniel walked three steps down the corridor and back.

"I'm your brother."

"Yes."

"And the person trying to protect you right now."

"Also yes."

"Then I need to know where you are."

"No."

The word was not loud.

It reminded him of their mother's apartment. Jana arranging the realtor appointment while Daniel was still trying to postpone a decision. Back then he had mistaken her speed for impatience.

Now the danger was real.

And still he ran into the same boundary.

"You're doing it again," Jana said.

"What?"

"Responsibility turns into jurisdiction very quickly for you."

Daniel laughed once without humor.

"This time it isn't just an apartment."

"I know."

"Someone watched you today."

"I know that too."

"Then—"

"Stop."

Daniel was silent.

Jana continued more calmly.

"I'm taking it seriously. That's why I'm changing my routine. I'm not alone. Claudia knows. My phone stays on. I'm not going to the practice at the normal time tomorrow."

"And if someone follows you?"

"Then I don't go to the destination and I call the police."

"And if you notice something before that?"

"Same thing."

"I can arrange protection for you."

"If you can tell me I'm specifically a target, we can talk about that."

"By the time I can tell you that, it could be too late."

"And until you can, it could also be wrong."

Daniel stopped.

She was right.

He hated it.

"You could at least give the address to me only."

"And if your environment is exactly the problem?"

Daniel felt his jaw tighten.

"That's not fair."

"No."

Jana sounded tired.

"But you asked me to deal reasonably with limited information. That's what I'm doing."

Daniel wanted to argue.

He could not find a factual point, only his need to know where she was.

That was not enough.

"Okay," he said.

Jana was quiet briefly.

"Okay?"

"I think it's shit."

"I believe you."

"But okay."

He heard a door and voices in the background.

"Claudia's here," Jana said.

"Let her drive."

"That was the plan."

"Phone on."

"Yes."

"No routine routes."

"Yes."

"And text me when you've arrived. No address. Just arrived."

A short pause.

"I will."

Daniel rested his head against the wall.

That was the compromise.

It was her compromise, not his.

Daniel remained there with the phone in his hand and went through the facts as if Jana were a case file, even though that was exactly the wrong way to think about her.

She was not alone.

She was changing route and vehicle.

She had canceled the first appointment.

She was keeping her phone on.

She had a clear response agreed if she noticed someone following her.

That was more protection than Daniel would have been able to justify for many people he had advised professionally without a specific threat indicator.

What was missing?

Only what Daniel himself wanted: the address.

He did not rewrite that in his head as a security gap.

At his mother's building, he had still believed a joint decision meant Jana had to wait for his final check. Now the situation was more serious and the temptation stronger to turn concern into jurisdiction.

Jana had still drawn the same boundary.

Information, yes.

Decision over her daily life, no.

Daniel put the phone away.

He could think her decision was wrong.

He could not reasonably call it unreasonable.

That was the part that took the most control away from him.

"And if I can tell you more later—"

"Then you tell me more."

"Not just: do this."

Jana laughed softly.

"That would be new."

"Don't exaggerate."

"Never."

For a moment they sounded like they used to.

Then the lightness was gone again.

"Daniel?"

"Yes?"

"Don't turn me into evidence for anything just because you're afraid."

He closed his eyes.

"I'm trying not to."

"Good."

She hung up.

Daniel looked at the phone.

Jana had acted reasonably.

She had canceled her appointment, changed her routine, involved another person, taken her own car out of the plan, and agreed on a clear response if anything seemed wrong.

Daniel could barely have justified more control sensibly.

His sense of risk did not fall at all.

Maybe it even rose.

Because now he did not know where she was.

He remained in the corridor for another minute.

Then Jana's message arrived.

**On the way. Claudia's driving.**

No address.

Daniel typed **Okay**.

Deleted it.

Wrote **Let me know when you're there.**

Sent it.

After the call, Daniel stayed in the corridor, looking at the display.

He wanted to text Jana to turn off location services. Then he wanted to know whether Claudia still had the same last name as before, whether her car could be known, whether the route was too predictable.

He wrote none of it.

Instead he opened the working view for the source message and checked whether Jana's line had been carried over anywhere as operational confirmation.

It had not.

**Private knowledge – separate from threat assessment.**

Daniel left the marker in place.

For the first time that evening, not knowing was not something he could close immediately.

Then he went back into the operations room.

His sister was not a variable he had closed.

She was a person who had made her own decision.

That did not make the situation calmer.

Only more honest.
