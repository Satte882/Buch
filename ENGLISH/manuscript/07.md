## 7

The yellow plastic strip was wedged so deep into the latch that Daniel did not see it until the door failed to close for the third time.

Jonas pushed it open again.

The heavy metal door swung back, slowed, and stopped with a narrow gap still open.

"Is it supposed to do that?"

"No."

Daniel crouched.

They were at the rear of Warehouse C. Concrete, gray loading doors, two dumpsters, then a fence and a strip of wet grass beyond. From the road, the door was barely visible.

The property manager who had led them across the site looked down at his keys.

"It normally closes."

"Normally is a difficult word," Jonas said.

Daniel took a pen from his jacket pocket and pushed the plastic strip out of the latch. It was maybe two inches long, folded several times, frayed along one edge.

The door swung shut.

This time it latched.

Daniel pulled the handle.

Closed.

He pressed the lever, let the door fall shut again, and looked at the latch. No visible damage. No bent metal. The strip had simply kept it from engaging fully.

Daniel let the door close twice more.

The first time, the closer pulled it cleanly into the latch. The second, he held it open a crack with two fingers and let go only when the closer had almost no force left.

It latched then too.

"So no defect," Jonas said.

"At least none we can reproduce right now."

The property manager was standing beside them now, looking at the lock as if it had personally disappointed him.

Farther down the row, a reversing pallet truck beeped from one of the units. A radio was playing somewhere. A man in a high-visibility vest pushed a cart of empty boxes across the yard and barely glanced at them.

Daniel disliked the place precisely because it was so easy to turn it into the setting for a finished story. Packing material on the ground. Doors left open during loading. People using shortcuts that appeared on no plan.

"If I go out for a smoke and leave my access fob upstairs," Jonas said, "maybe I jam something like this in the door."

"Yes."

"If I want somebody else to get in later without being seen, same thing."

"Also yes."

Jonas looked at the door.

"Helpful."

Daniel stood up.

"That's why for now we secure only what we actually have."

"How long could that strip have been in there?" Jonas asked.

The manager shrugged. "An hour. A day. No idea."

"When were you last back here?"

"This morning. But I don't inspect every door one by one."

Daniel looked at the narrow gap between the door and frame. From outside, the plastic would be hard to see while the door was open.

"Who uses this door?"

"Anyone who needs to go out back. Trash, deliveries, smokers. We call it the north door."

"Access?"

"Fob from outside. From inside you just leave normally."

"And when it's blocked like this?"

"Then anyone who pulls can get in."

Jonas looked at Daniel.

That sentence immediately sounded bigger than the yellow strip itself.

Daniel stayed by the door.

"Did anyone report an unsecured access point today or yesterday?"

"No. No lock fault either, as far as I know."

The manager looked less bored now. Not alarmed yet. More as if the door had begun to make him personally uncomfortable.

Jonas pointed at the plastic.

"Is this from the site?"

The manager bent down. "Could be strapping. There's stuff like that around the pallets all the time."

Daniel looked toward the dumpsters. Two crushed boxes lay beside them, along with a piece of yellow banding wider than the strip in his hand.

"So there's an ordinary explanation right here on site," he said.

Jonas did not answer immediately.

"Possible."

"Nothing more than that."

"At the moment."

Daniel photographed the strip on the ground, then the latch.

A mundane violation was still a violation. That did not make it anything more.

They were here because of Unit 17.

Twelve battery packs for commercial radios. A delivery address that had appeared seven months earlier at the edge of another case. A company that, according to the register, dealt in technical equipment and paid its bills.

Until ten minutes ago, Daniel had seen nothing that justified coming all the way out here except the desire to close a thin case eventually.

Now there was an unsecured door.

That was more than before.

It still was not a story.

Unit 17 was twenty meters farther down.

The loading door was closed. A clean company sign, black on white, was fixed beside the entrance. No improvised name. No label pasted over another. No cables running somewhere they should not.

The property manager unlocked the unit.

Inside were metal shelves, boxes, two empty pallets, and a desk with a coffee mug ring dried onto the surface. It smelled of cardboard and cold concrete.

Daniel remained in the doorway.

One shelf was almost empty. The next held cable reels, several smaller boxes, and two open packages containing chargers. None of it had to belong to the ordered batteries. None of it looked improvised.

Jonas looked around.

"Twelve batteries would be nice right now."

"Why?"

"Then at least we'd have found what we drove out here for."

"If you find them, you have twelve batteries."

"You know what I mean."

Daniel did.

The manager showed them the paperwork for the unit. Lease in place for almost two years. No payment arrears. Two registered contacts. Deliveries irregular, mostly during the day.

Daniel did not look only at the current page. The previous weeks looked much the same. Boxes in, boxes out. No unusual late-night traffic. No sudden spike in use.

"Anything unusual yesterday?" Daniel asked.

"Not that I know of."

"Vehicles?"

"Vehicles come through here all day."

"Evening?"

"Yes. Some tenants work late."

Jonas nodded toward the rear. "And the door?"

The manager shook his head. "First time I've seen that."

"Anyone have a reason to keep it open?"

"Convenience."

"Anything else?"

"What else would there be?"

Daniel looked at the shelves.

There was no reason to believe the man.

There was no reason not to.

"Access logs?" Daniel asked.

"For the exterior doors, yes. Not for the individual units."

"Video?"

"Front side. At the back, only the yard up to the loading ramp."

"Does the camera cover the north door?"

"Not directly. Part of the area in front of it."

Jonas looked at Daniel. "Then we pull it."

"We preserve it," Daniel said.

The difference was small. He still wanted to hear it.

"And the fobs?" Jonas asked.

Daniel looked at the manager.

"Can you tell us which regular entries there have been at the north door since noon yesterday?"

"Yes."

"Then just that window, please. No expansion yet."

Jonas put his hands in his jacket pockets.

"If someone came through the open door without a fob, of course we won't see them."

"Correct."

"Which makes the list pretty useless."

"No. It tells us who came in normally. Nothing more."

Daniel nodded to the manager.

"Please preserve the period starting at noon yesterday. And have the door checked."

The manager became cautious. "Did something happen?"

"We don't know."

"What does that mean?"

"That we don't know."

On the way back to the car, Jonas stopped at the metal door once more.

He pulled the handle. It stayed closed.

"If somebody deliberately kept that open, that's already more than twelve batteries."

"Yes."

"But not much more."

"Right."

"And if the strip just came off a pallet?"

"Then someone broke the building rules and we saw an unsecured door."

"Boring."

"I'd be fine with that."

Jonas looked at the yellow strip Daniel had sealed in a small evidence bag.

"You're taking it with you anyway."

"Otherwise tomorrow we'll be arguing about whether it was yellow or gray."

"It was yellow."

"Today."

Jonas grinned briefly.

In the car, Daniel entered the incident into the case record.

**Rear exterior door at Warehouse C failed to close properly because of a plastic strip inserted into the latch. Responsible person unknown. Ordinary on-site explanation possible. No other anomalies observed on site.**

He read the last sentence again.

*No other anomalies observed.*

The wording was accurate. It did not mean nothing had happened. Only that they had found nothing else.

Daniel added the preserved video and access-log review as an open check.

Then he saved the entry.

"No escalation?" Jonas asked.

"For what?"

"An unsecured exterior door at a warehouse that's already part of a weak lead."

"One weak lead plus another doesn't automatically make a strong case."

Jonas started the engine but left it idling.

"What would have to happen?"

Daniel looked at him.

"For it to become more than two weak leads to you."

Daniel thought for a moment.

"An independent connection. Someone using the door deliberately. A vehicle that's here for more than coincidence. A delivery that doesn't fit the business. Something that doesn't just tell the same suspicion a second time."

Jonas nodded.

"So a second anchor. And if that comes tomorrow?"

"Then tomorrow we have more."

Jonas put the car in gear.

"Just wanted to hear whether you're still the same person today."

Daniel looked out the side window at Warehouse C.

The yellow strip was now in his pocket. The north door was closed again. Behind the gray walls, the ordinary things that always happened in places like this kept happening.

"Drive."
