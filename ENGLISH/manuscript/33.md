## 33

"You said we'd scale it back."

Lena stood on the other side of the table.

Berg sat between them without making the arrangement feel like mediation.

Jonas had placed three documents side by side.

The source message.

The project picture for the new delivery authorization.

Daniel's own note defining the return boundary.

Daniel knew every document.

Together they looked worse.

Lena tapped the source message.

**Don't check only the name. Check the access around it.**

Underneath was the identifier for the new delivery authorization.

Then she tapped the internal project status.

"The identifier in exactly this form first appears internally in a version that already states the new delivery service had been formally checked and added through regular procedure."

Daniel read the timestamps.

"Sure?"

"Yes."

"The identifier could have come through another route."

"Possible."

Lena pushed the next printout toward him.

"But so far we haven't found an internal version that contained only the identifier without the reassuring context."

Berg took both pages.

"So the source probably had both."

"Probably," Lena said.

"And gave us only the part that looked like an open gap."

"That's how it looks."

Daniel said nothing.

He remembered the three models he had written after the message.

Real threat plus pressure tactic.

Real fragments plus deliberate steering.

Harmless factual point plus psychological pressure.

None had disappeared completely.

But the second had gained weight.

"The delivery service still wasn't fully cleared," Daniel said.

"No," Lena said.

"The driver lists were incomplete. And the wider review exposed a real threat strand."

"Both true."

Daniel looked at her.

Again she refused him the easy opposing position.

Berg put the pages down.

"Then the uncomfortable version is: the source took a genuine point, omitted the reassuring context, and pushed us in a direction where we still found something real."

"Yes," Lena said.

"That's pretty effective."

No one disagreed.

Daniel pulled Weber's correction note onto the screen.

Then Lena's.

Two wrongful burdens.

One unknown person where Daniel had weighted a harmless counter-reading too lightly.

One colleague where he had wanted to rescue the same kind of counter-reading immediately.

Beside them was the current report from the storage area.

**Concrete threat. Further preparatory step interrupted in time.**

Four things.

None erased any of the others.

Lena slid Daniel's project note toward him.

**No automatic continuation once the acute case-specific basis ends. Continuation requires renewed documented justification and authorization.**

Underneath:

**Exceptional decisions and expansions will be independently reviewed after completion.**

"We have a solid indication of manipulation," she said. "We have an acute strand that has been stopped. This is exactly the kind of moment you wrote the boundary for."

Berg leaned back.

"The larger structure isn't resolved."

Lena looked at him.

"Then we keep working. But narrower."

"How much narrower?"

"Continue confirmed strands in a targeted way. Suspend broad contact and timing reviews. Review source steering independently."

"And if the next storage unit is sitting in the remaining broad hits?" Berg asked.

Lena was silent for a moment.

"Then that's the risk."

Berg looked at Daniel.

Daniel knew why.

Berg was not handing him the decision. Daniel had written the boundary himself.

He opened the decision note for the broader review.

**Counter-risk: Weber misattribution; correlation does not establish individual involvement.**

Underneath was his decision to review the twenty-seven-person group anyway.

It had hit Lena.

It had found a real threat strand.

Both were now in the past.

The next decision was not.

"What's still open in the confirmed strand?" Daniel asked.

Jonas answered.

"Connections of the two people. Origin of some of the documents. Two more logistical touchpoints we still haven't assigned cleanly."

"Do we need the broad review for those?"

"Not for the two people. Possibly for unknown cross-connections."

"Possibly," Lena repeated.

Jonas raised his hands. "That's all I know."

Daniel nodded.

At the beginning, that would have been enough for him.

He did not actually pull up the old Ahrens case. He only needed the motion he had made back then.

Weak indicators.

Real counterhypothesis.

No intervention while the harmless explanation still carried enough weight.

Back then, the central error he wanted to avoid was a false intervention.

Now there was another experience on the table as well.

A security guard injured after defensible waiting.

A preparatory step stopped after broader review.

Then another under the same logic.

And beside them Weber.

Lena.

A source able to choose genuine fragments in ways that raised Daniel's sense of risk.

Daniel wrote two sentences one beneath the other.

**Harm from continuation: further wrongful burdens, wider reach, susceptibility to manipulation.**

**Harm from scaling back: possible real cross-connections seen later or not at all.**

Neither sentence contained a probability he could responsibly quantify.

That was the difference between this and a calculation.

He had to weight them.

Lena read both lines.

"And why does the second win now?"

Daniel looked at the threat report.

"Because I now have two real cases where time and connection made a difference."

"And two real wrongful burdens."

Daniel nodded.

"So again, no clean balance."

"No."

Daniel heard himself answer and realized he was no longer trying to minimize the first column.

He knew its price.

He simply no longer weighted it first.

That was the shift.

The shift was not yet an action against anyone or an expansion of a review.

Before, he had asked: what do we have that is solid?

Now another question kept arriving before it.

What happens if we scale back and we're wrong?

He recognized the shift.

That did not make it smaller.

"The source isn't trustworthy," Daniel said.

Lena waited.

"It's probably steering us."

"Yes."

"And the Jana reference was pressure."

"At least that."

"Weber was wrong."

"Yes."

"You were wrong."

Lena answered after a moment.

"Yes."

Daniel looked at the threat report.

"And that was real."

"Also yes."

Berg said nothing.

Daniel stood and walked to the window.

It was already dark outside. In the glass he could see the room reflected behind him. Berg at the table. Lena on the other side. Jonas at the screen.

No state of emergency.

Just a debrief with too many correct sentences.

"If we scale back broadly now," Daniel said, "and tomorrow one of the open cross-connections turns out to have been the next operational access point, then we've deliberately recreated the gap."

Lena answered immediately.

"And if you don't scale back, you deliberately keep more people inside the review group even though you know the source can push you toward exactly that wider reach."

Daniel turned around.

"Yes."

The word was out before he could soften it.

Lena was silent.

Daniel returned to the table.

"Source review continues."

"Daniel."

"Independent review too."

"That's not the scale-back."

"No."

"Documentation?"

"Remains complete."

"That's not the scale-back either."

"I know."

Lena pulled the note closer to him.

"Then say it cleanly."

Daniel looked at his own wording.

No automatic continuation.

New justification.

Authorization.

He had written the words so a later Daniel could not simply keep going.

Now he was sitting here.

"I'm not fully scaling back the broad capabilities right now," he said.

Berg did not move.

Jonas looked at his screen.

Lena asked, "Why?"

Daniel could have given a long answer.

Open structure.

Time.

Success.

Potential harm.

He said only:

"Because right now I weight the risk of losing the next real connection more heavily."

"More heavily than what?"

Daniel looked at her.

"Than the harm of keeping the wider review running temporarily."

Lena leaned back.

"The harm wasn't temporary for me."

Daniel nodded.

"I know."

"Or for Weber."

"I know."

"Then don't call it abstract."

Daniel was silent.

Berg said, "We can keep the criteria narrow. No expansion beyond the existing group. No action without a second anchor."

Lena turned to him.

"That makes the decision more controlled. It doesn't make it a scale-back."

"I didn't claim it did."

Daniel looked at Berg.

The proposal was reasonable.

Of course it was.

Reasonable intermediate solutions like that had brought them this far.

"Existing defined group," Daniel said. "No expansion without new authorization. No action without independent confirmation. Source review separate."

Lena said nothing.

Daniel opened the project note.

He did not delete his old boundary.

He did not change it either.

Underneath, he added the current decision with date and rationale.

**Scale-back currently suspended. Open real threat strand and unresolved cross-connections. Suspected source manipulation remains explicitly acknowledged.**

He read the sentence.

Then saved it.

Jonas looked at the new note.

"What does that mean practically tomorrow morning?"

Daniel did not answer immediately.

The question was good because it forced every moral formulation into working rules.

"The existing group stays in place," he said. "No expansion without another documented decision. Hits alone trigger nothing. Measures still require an independent anchor."

"And if the source sends something again tonight?"

"Review separately. No automatic increase in scope."

"Who gets to change that?"

Daniel looked at the note.

"Not me silently."

Berg raised an eyebrow.

Daniel added, "Any new expansion comes back to the group as a documented decision. With the counter-risk."

Jonas nodded and put the points into the working view.

Daniel's decision was no longer only a refusal to scale back.

It became a procedure for the next morning.

Daniel opened the version history.

At the top was his old self-imposed return boundary.

Below it was the new decision.

Two timestamps. Same author.

He could have edited the first version so the contradiction looked smaller.

He did not.

If someone later wanted to review when his boundary had shifted, the answer should not depend on memory.

It now existed in the system.

Lena looked at the timestamp of the change.

"Then the boundary was worthless," she said.

"No."

"When is it worth anything?"

"When the real threat is resolved enough that we know the cost of scaling back."

"That's not the boundary you set."

Daniel looked at her.

"No."

This time he said it without trying to repair the contradiction.

It was the first moment in the conversation when Lena had no immediate reply.

Daniel did not push the note away.

He knew what it said.

He also knew what it had said the day before.

Both were his.

Lena closed her folder.

"And that's exactly why it works."

Daniel felt irritation.

"What?"

She shook her head.

"Nothing."

She stood.

Berg asked, "Seven-thirty?"

"Yes," Daniel said.

Lena went to the door.

Just before it, she stopped as though she had one more thing to say.

She did not.

The door closed behind her.

Berg remained seated after Lena left.

"Do you want me to take the decision?" he asked.

Daniel looked at him.

"Why?"

"Because you just decided against your own note."

Daniel waited.

"That wasn't criticism," Berg said. "Just asking whether you want a second decision level for the continuation."

Daniel looked at the two versions.

It would have been convenient.

Not professionally. Personally.

If Berg confirmed continuation, afterward it would have been their decision.

Daniel shook his head.

"The authorizations stay as designed. But the weighting here is mine."

Berg nodded.

"Then document exactly that."

Daniel added one final sentence to the note:

**Decision on temporary continuation: Reuter.**

No grand declaration.

Only a name beneath a decision he did not want to blame later on the situation, Berg, or the source.

Daniel looked at the two versions of his own note.

The old boundary was still there.

He had not forgotten it.

He had decided against it.
