## 46

The case was small enough that Berg had not wanted to give it to Daniel at first.

"Two nighttime accesses, one contact, and a bad gut feeling."

Jonas put the documents on the table.

Daniel looked at him.

"Whose gut feeling?"

"Mine."

"Then we can cross that out first."

Jonas grinned.

"Missed you too."

The case involved a technical subcontractor with access to a federal government building. One of its employees had used a side entrance twice within a week outside his normal shift. There was also an old, weak contact with a person from an extremist support environment.

No concrete attack warning.

No acute alert.

A matter that once would have received a few open checks and then either grown or been closed.

Daniel read the times.

"Work reason for the nighttime access?"

"Still open. Technically valid authorization. Only the time is unusual. The contact is two years old."

"Then for now it's two years old."

Jonas nodded.

Lena came in, sat down, and took the short version.

Berg followed.

"What's the question?"

Jonas said, "Whether we start an integrated review early."

Daniel looked at him. What bothered him was precisely how normal the proposal sounded. It did not sound wrong at all.

Berg asked, "Scope?"

"Employees with technical access to the affected section of the building plus known current contact overlaps."

Lena turned to the last page.

"Threshold?"

"Two unusual access events and the old contact aren't enough for the whole group yet."

"Not for me either," Daniel said.

Jonas nodded.

"Then smaller. First the employee and direct functional access around the two time windows."

Lena said, "That carries better. Time-limited, existing data only, no automatic consequence from hits."

Berg looked at the clock.

"How fast?"

"An hour," Jonas said.

The guidance was not even open on the table.

Jonas knew the thresholds well enough to recite them.

"Current case nexus, defined group, limited period, no automatic consequence from hits."

Lena asked, "Who approves the expansion if you want to go beyond the direct person?"

"Second office."

"Which one?"

Jonas named it.

Lena nodded.

The conversation lasted barely twenty seconds.

Daniel remembered how much friction even the idea of his first access had created.

The friction had not disappeared. It had acquired forms, roles, thresholds, and responsibilities. That made the work better than improvisation and showed at the same time that the instrument was no longer an improvisation at all.

Only when Lena marked one of the nighttime access events did Daniel notice that no one in the room was still asking whether this kind of linkage was fundamentally permissible. They were discussing only the scope.

"There's a maintenance window in the building here," she said.

Jonas looked.

"Officially not until one."

"The access was at 12:44."

"Sixteen minutes."

Berg shrugged.

"Could be preparation."

"Could also be someone arriving early," Daniel said.

Jonas nodded.

"I'll check."

Jonas called the technical operator.

The first nighttime access was explained faster than expected.

A maintenance job had been moved forward by phone at 12:31 a.m. The employee had been in the building at 12:44. The written change had not been entered into the system until later.

"Who confirms that?" Daniel asked.

"Shift supervisor and external client."

"Independently?"

"Yes."

"Then the access isn't suspicious. The documentation is just bad."

Jonas removed the marker. No one objected.

Daniel liked that.

The second access remained open.

There was nothing dramatic about it. It simply did not yet have a clean explanation.

Berg said, "Then the small integrated review on the person and direct functional access."

Lena asked for the time period and data categories.

Jonas answered.

Daniel listened.

Again, no fundamental dispute.

Only a professional choice between tools that were now available.

The meeting moved on.

That unspectacularly.

Daniel looked at the guidance beside Lena's laptop.

**Integrated review.**

No one had to explain the term.

It simply lay on the table.

A coordinator slid a draft form across the table.

"I only need the threat nexus in one sentence."

Jonas dictated it.

She crossed out two words.

"Too broad."

Jonas read the revised version.

"Works."

Daniel looked at the sentence.

Possible misuse of technical access in combination with a real but weak contact connection.

No major situation.

No state of emergency.

This was exactly the kind of case the new routine had now been built for.

Jonas collected the first work assignments.

Half an hour later, the first results from the small integrated review came back.

They arrived as a list, not as a red alert.

Four access contacts inside the relevant time window.

Three were explained within minutes: shared equipment cart, documented handover, one late shift change.

The fourth remained open.

Jonas showed it on the screen.

"Current contact with our employee, but professionally plausible. They both work on the same system."

"Then the contact alone means nothing," Daniel said.

"Yes."

Lena asked, "What's left independently of that?"

"The second nighttime access."

"So exactly the same open point as before."

Jonas nodded.

The integrated review had made more visible in thirty minutes.

It had not automatically made the case bigger.

Not this time.

Berg said, "Then we clarify the access and close it if nothing new comes in."

The instrument did not have to find something every time in order to become normal.

"I'd prepare another proposal in parallel," Jonas said.

Berg asked, "What proposal?"

Jonas looked at Daniel.

"For the case where the nighttime access can't be explained cleanly."

Daniel leaned back.

"Do it."

Jonas opened a new page.
