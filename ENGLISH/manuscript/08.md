## 8

The message arrived forty-three minutes later on Daniel's private phone.

He noticed it in the elevator.

No number he knew. No name.

Only four lines.

**North door, Warehouse C. The yellow plastic strip in the latch was not an accident.**

**The delivery to Unit 17 is only the beginning. Tomorrow between 2:00 and 2:30 p.m., a pickup will take place there that is not booked to Unit 17.**

**The target is not at the warehouse. This is about the logistics of a public event in Berlin.**

Underneath was a time.

**8:11 p.m. Your mother's building. You pressed the name label on the mailbox back into place.**

Daniel read the message twice.

The third time, the elevator stopped on the ground floor and the doors opened.

He did not get out.

A woman with a shopping bag was waiting outside. She looked at him.

Daniel pressed two.

The doors closed again.

Daniel looked at the last line.

Your mother's building.

His thumb was still resting on the button. He took it away.

Then he read the message again from the top.

North door.

The property manager had used that term. Jonas had heard it. So had Daniel. At that point the system contained only *rear exterior door, Warehouse C*.

The yellow plastic strip had been photographed and bagged. Not analyzed yet. No media report. No radio traffic that had gone through any larger operational group.

Daniel opened the timestamp on his own note.

The message had arrived before the full entry was saved.

That did not rule out an internal information path. Jonas had made calls. So had the property manager. Someone could have seen what happened. A photograph could travel faster than a case entry.

But the simple explanation was getting worse.

Daniel read the second line.

Tomorrow. 2:00 to 2:30 p.m. Pickup. Not booked to Unit 17.

Testable.

The third line was different.

Public event in Berlin.

Large enough to sound threatening. Vague enough to fit almost anything.

Daniel did not mark it as information in his mind.

Claim.

Then the mailbox.

8:11 p.m.

He had not looked at the time when Jana took the realtor's call. It could have been roughly right.

That meant nothing.

A time could be guessed. A shared hallway could be watched. The name label was visible to anyone inside the building.

But nobody from Warehouse C had anything to do with his mother's mailbox.

Only when his chest tightened did he realize he was holding his breath. He let it out slowly.

The elevator opened again on the second floor.

This time he stepped out.

In the hallway he stopped short of a glass door and opened Jana's contact.

He typed nothing.

If he asked now whether she had told anyone about his visit, he would turn one unexplained sentence into a private security situation immediately. It could be real. Or exactly the reaction the sender wanted to provoke.

He closed the contact again.

The yellow plastic strip.

The property manager had seen it. Jonas. Daniel himself. Maybe someone in an office with access to the new case entry, if it had already propagated through the system.

But the message had arrived before Daniel had fully entered the incident.

He checked the time again.

Daniel went back to the second floor.

Jonas was at his desk wearing headphones. He noticed Daniel only when Daniel stopped beside him.

"Do you have the yard footage yet?"

Jonas pulled one earcup aside. "The property manager's sending it."

"And the deliveries?"

"Working on it."

Daniel did not show him the message.

"Also check every pickup scheduled for tomorrow. Unit 17 and the units directly next to it. Regular channels only. No shortcuts."

Jonas looked at him.

"Why?"

"Because I want to know whether anyone is coming tomorrow between two and two-thirty."

"Where did the time come from?"

"A tip."

Jonas waited.

Daniel said nothing more.

"Okay," Jonas said finally.

Daniel went into his office and closed the door.

He did not put the private phone on the desk. He kept it in his hand while he unlocked the computer.

He did not search for the pickup first.

He searched for ways someone could have reconstructed the first line without having been directly involved in the incident.

Was the north door labeled publicly? Not on the landlord's website. Maybe on a site plan hanging openly in the entrance. Had someone posted a photo of it online? Nothing obvious.

The yellow strip was worse. Visible only to people who had been at the door or had seen a picture of it.

Daniel wrote down no conclusion.

Only routes.

**Seen on site.**

**Learned from someone involved.**

**Internal information path.**

**Targeted observation.**

Four possibilities. None of them comfortable.

Then he took a sheet of paper and wrote three questions.

**1. Is the warehouse information real?**

**2. Is the threat interpretation correct?**

**3. Who knows my private space?**

He crossed out *space* again.

Too broad.

His mother's hallway was not private space. Not really. Any neighbor could stand there. The realtor. The building superintendent. A delivery driver.

He wrote underneath:

**Who knew I was there?**

That was no better.

Jana. Mara. The realtor. Anyone who had followed him.

Daniel opened his calendar.

The visit to the apartment was not there. Only a private block with no address. Nothing at all on his work calendar.

That proved nothing. Mara knew where he had been. Jana obviously did. The realtor might have known his name. A neighbor could have recognized him.

Daniel opened the messages with Jana from that evening.

No address. Nothing about the mailbox. Only her asking when he was coming, and later a link to the realtor's documents.

He realized what he was doing.

He was already trying to shrink the number of possible observers.

Too soon.

Daniel closed the messages again.

The question was not who it could have been.

Not yet.

The question was only whether the detail was real enough that he needed to preserve it separately.

It was.

He placed the phone beside the sheet of paper and forced himself to think of a mundane information route for each of the four possibilities. A random observer in the building. An employee of the property company who had talked to someone. A technical access path that said nothing yet about motive or identity. None explained everything. None was impossible.

It did not reduce the pressure.

It only prevented pressure from becoming certainty.

Daniel looked at the private phone.

The message connected those three levels as though they naturally belonged together.

That was exactly what he could not adopt.

The warehouse incident could be real and the interpretation wrong.

The pickup could be real and unrelated to any threat.

The mailbox could have been observation without Jana being in danger.

Three truth questions. Not one story.

Daniel put down the pen.

The obvious reaction would have been to circulate the message immediately. Source, IT, security, the full group.

The obvious reaction was not automatically the right one.

If someone inside his own system was siphoning information, broad circulation would only show them what he knew.

If the message was a hoax, there was no reason to turn four lines into an operational situation.

If it was real, he had until the next afternoon to test part of it.

He copied the first three lines into a separate working file.

Not the last one.

His finger hovered over the keyboard for a moment.

That was already a decision.

Not to conceal the information. Only not to put it into the same circle yet as the testable claim.

He wrote beside it:

**Private reference separate. Origin unclear.**

Daniel did not photograph the screen with his work phone. He did not export the message into the normal case file either.

Instead he recorded the time received, the device, and the wording of the private line in a separate locally secured working note.

Not because he thought that solved anything.

Only so he would not later have to decide from memory what exactly it had said.

Then he wrote three conditions underneath.

**Concrete indication of danger to Jana.**

**Second independently verifiable private reference.**

**Necessary to clarify the information path.**

If any one of them occurred, he would stop keeping the private part to himself.

Until then, withholding it was also a risk.

Lena would not know part of the pressure under which Daniel was assessing the tip. Neither would Jonas. If that pressure made Daniel move faster without realizing it, they would be missing exactly the information that explained the shift.

On the other hand, he did not know whether the source had an internal information channel. Every additional disclosure showed which part of the message he was taking seriously.

Daniel read the three conditions again.

None was comfortable.

He preferred that to a rule that only confirmed his first decision.

He closed the note and reopened the operational message without the private line.

The pickup could be checked.

The mailbox could not. Not yet.

There was a knock.

Jonas stuck his head in.

"Yard camera's here."

Daniel stood.

"What do we see?"

"Not the door. Fourteen vehicles, two dogs, and a man who apparently thinks a shopping cart is an approved transport device on a loading ramp."

Daniel followed him.

"Vehicles first."

"I liked the shopping cart."

The footage from the previous day played on Jonas's screen.

Daniel watched without sound.

Delivery vans. Small commercial vehicles. A black station wagon. A man in a hood pulling a pallet. Nothing that told a story unless you made it tell one.

They jumped between time windows. Morning. Afternoon. Early evening.

Jonas had marked the known tenant vehicles, but Daniel had him hide the markings.

"Why?"

"Because I want to see what actually stands out first."

"And?"

"Nothing yet."

At 18:07, a gray van stopped for four minutes at the edge of the frame.

Jonas paused the video.

"Not one of the regular plates."

"Could still be anyone."

"Sure."

Daniel leaned closer to the screen.

The rear of the vehicle was blurred. The plate unreadable.

The driver was not visible. Once, the rear door opened and closed again, partly outside the camera angle.

"Any clean time link to the open north door?" Daniel asked.

"No. Camera doesn't cover the door."

"Then don't connect them."

Jonas looked at him.

Daniel knew how the sentence sounded. Almost overly cautious.

"Not yet."

"Don't extrapolate," he said.

"I wasn't going to."

"You were just about to."

Jonas did not smile.

"What did the tip say?"

Daniel looked back at the image.

"Enough that we check it."

"And not enough that you show it to me?"

Daniel straightened.

"Not yet."

Jonas nodded slowly.

He did not like that.

Neither did Daniel.

He went back to his office and picked up the private phone again.

The yellow strip was now in a bag inside a cabinet two rooms away.

The name label on his mother's mailbox had been flush again since the night before.

Someone knew both.

Only one of them belonged to a case.

And even there, Daniel still did not know what it meant.
