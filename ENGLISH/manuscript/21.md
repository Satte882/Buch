## 21

Berg did not call it a special route.

He called it a project structure.

"Temporary," he said. "Case-specific. Only the offices we actually need. No more five phone chains and three people checking the same question one after another."

A page with six points was displayed on the screen behind him.

Purpose.

Participating offices.

Data categories.

Access roles.

Logging.

End date.

Daniel liked that the end was there.

Apparently Lena did too.

"Who can extend it?" she asked.

Berg looked at the page. "We're talking about the start."

"I'm talking about the end."

"Of course."

"Who?"

Berg named the responsible level.

Lena wrote it down.

"Automatic?"

"No."

"New justification?"

"Yes."

"Documented?"

Berg pushed the pen away. "Yes."

Jonas sat at the edge of the table trying to look as though he was not the youngest person in the room.

Heller was connected by video. His image froze for half a second every few minutes.

Berg clicked on.

"In practical terms: defined case scope, defined roles, faster combination of information we already have. No new magic query."

"And no new authority to collect data," Lena said.

"Yes."

"If one office isn't allowed to collect something, the project structure can't suddenly produce it for them."

"Yes."

"And that separation has to remain visible in the access model."

"Yes."

"And if the urgent version starts today, the formal steps still have to be completed afterward."

Berg looked at her.

"Lena, if you want to hear yes one more time, just say so."

Jonas became intensely interested in his documents.

Lena did not smile.

Daniel almost did.

"What about access?" he asked.

Berg switched to a role overview.

The views were separated by role.

Operational analysis.

Technical review.

Coordination.

Documentation.

Daniel recognized a cleaner version of what he had improvised himself the previous evening.

"Who sees the source contents?"

"Only the defined group," Berg said.

"Who sees the provenance review?"

This time Lena answered. "Even smaller."

Daniel looked at her.

She held his gaze.

Her secret had not disappeared.

But the new structure made visible that withholding information could also serve a legitimate function.

Heller's video moved again.

"The idea isn't to make Reuter's old access look prettier," he said. "The idea is that no one has to improvise the same mess again."

Daniel looked at him.

"Mess?"

"You know what I mean."

"I think I do."

Heller lifted a hand briefly. "The access worked. It was still a bad process."

"Because it wasn't authorized."

"That too. And because everything depended on one person having three minutes to decide which data environment to open."

Daniel said nothing.

Heller continued.

"If the system is properly prepared beforehand, the next Reuter doesn't have to make the same decision."

"The next Reuter?" Jonas asked.

Heller looked briefly confused, then smiled. "The next person in the same situation."

His image froze. When it resumed, Heller was no longer looking into the camera.

"I was once in a case where every office had a defensible reason to wait," he said. "Afterward, we could explain in great detail why nothing had been possible sooner. People were still dead."

The room grew quieter.

Lena looked at the role overview.

"That's not an argument against purpose limitations."

"It's not meant to be," Heller said.

"Good."

"It's an argument for not making the permitted routes unnecessarily slow."

After a moment, Lena nodded. "I can live with that."

Daniel heard the difference.

Heller wanted speed.

Lena wanted limitation and auditability.

Berg wanted something that worked and could be explained upward.

All three could want the same project structure for entirely different reasons.

Berg slid a second page across the table.

Daniel skimmed it.

Two of his decisions from the previous few days appeared as examples of lost time and successful countermeasures.

"Who wrote this?"

"Working draft," Berg said.

"For whom?"

"Upstairs."

"How far upstairs?"

"Far enough that we're not spending today debating it."

Daniel looked at him.

"You passed the case upward as a reference."

"Of course."

"Without telling me how much of it."

Berg put down the pen.

"Daniel, I'm your supervisor. If a process almost runs too late and an improvised workaround protects people, I don't turn that into a private learning exercise between the two of us."

"I didn't ask you to."

"It sounds like you are now."

"You're not just using the process. You're using my rule violation as justification for a new structure."

Berg looked directly at him.

"I'm using the fact that the old process was too slow and your improvised access bought time. The fact that I dislike the rule violation is in the same record."

"Upstairs too?"

"Yes."

Daniel could not verify whether every version gave both parts equal weight.

Berg noticed the look.

"If you think I'm selling you as a hero, read the draft."

Daniel picked up the second page again.

It did say: **noncompliant cross-case use of existing data; operationally effective; structural action required.**

No hero.

But an argument.

That was almost more uncomfortable.

Daniel thought of the source.

**You decided before it was too late.**

Berg had a real interest in making precisely that decision visible.

And that interest was open.

He was turning it into something the institution could use.

"How long has this been running?"

"Since the access."

"Who has the documents?"

Berg named a small group.

Heller's area was included.

Lena too.

No answer.

Only more paths.

Daniel looked at Heller on the screen. "And your interest?"

Heller raised an eyebrow.

"That the next time we have a credible time window, we don't start by searching for jurisdiction."

"Nothing more?"

"That's enough for me."

The answer was ordinary enough to be believable.

Daniel could still have read almost anything into it.

He did not.

Lena tapped the first page.

"Duration."

Berg gave the date.

"After that?"

"Ends or gets a new justification. Data returns under the defined rules. No silent permanent pool. Logs remain."

Daniel read the six points again.

What convinced him was not greater access.

It was that Jonas would not have to call three offices one after another again.

That vehicle ownership and an access-authorization question could be checked in parallel.

That a legitimate piece of information would not have to travel through five distribution chains before it could sit beside another one.

It did not feel like more power.

It felt like less friction.

Daniel had Jonas run an ordinary matter through the new route.

No urgent situation. No source tip. Just a vehicle question from the existing case complex.

Before, Jonas would have requested the keeper information, waited for the answer, then contacted the authorization office separately. Now both questions went simultaneously to clearly assigned roles. The answers appeared side by side with timestamp and provenance.

Eleven minutes later, the matter was closed.

No dramatic finding.

Just a vehicle belonging to a legitimate subcontractor with valid authorization.

"And now more people have seen the same case context," Lena said.

Jonas nodded. "Yes."

"And we were faster."

"Also yes."

Daniel looked at the closed item.

Cases like this made the structure more convincing than the spectacular success had.

It worked even when they found nothing.

It worked as a better organized space for questions that were already permissible, not as a miracle tool.

The price remained real: more simultaneous visibility, more roles, more logs, more people who could know what they were working on.

So did the benefit: less waiting, less duplicate work, faster rejection of false connections.

Daniel understood why institutions rarely forgot routes like this completely once they had worked.

The reason was ordinary: eleven minutes felt immediately reasonable in daily work.

That was probably why it was so attractive.

"I want the structure," Daniel said.

Lena looked at him.

"Under conditions."

"Which ones?" Berg asked.

"Case-specific. Fully logged. Clear end date. No automatic carryover into the next case complex."

Berg exhaled through his nose.

"It's on the page."

"Then we don't have a problem."

"And independent review of exceptional decisions," Lena said.

Daniel looked at her.

"Even if it slows the process?"

"Yes."

Daniel nodded. "Then that too."

Heller said nothing.

Berg moved to the next point.

"Then we start."

Jonas opened his task list. The old improvised route was still there as a manual note.

He did not delete it. He marked it **do not use** and placed the new project route beneath it.

"Better?" he asked.

Daniel looked at the two lines.

"Cleaner."

"You don't sound excited."

"I'm not supposed to."

No one marked the moment.

No music. No sentence about boundaries.

Just Jonas crossing out an old access route in the task list and replacing it with a new project path.

And Daniel noticing that the new path immediately felt more reasonable than anything they had improvised over the previous few days.
