## 19

Mika Lorenz wanted to know whether he was allowed to go to work on Monday.

Daniel looked at him.

"That's your question right now?"

"I have a job."

Mika sat with both forearms on the table. Late twenties, narrow shoulders, dark beard thinning under the chin. He had been wearing the same gray hoodie for almost two hours. Someone had brought him water. He had not touched it.

There was nothing on the table except the cup and a notepad in front of Daniel. No photos. No printouts. Daniel wanted to see first what story Mika told without props.

"You were temporarily detained after being found in connection with falsified access documents," Daniel said.

"I wasn't in the vehicle."

The answer came immediately.

Daniel let a moment pass.

"You were in the garage."

"Yes."

"The minibus came out of the garage."

"Yes."

"You had a key."

Mika raised his hands. "For the garage."

"Not for the bus."

"That's what I said."

Lena sat to Daniel's right and wrote nothing down. That made Mika more nervous than a running transcript would have. His eyes kept returning to her closed pen, as if something important would happen the moment she used it.

Before they began, Daniel had made Mika explain what he did for a living. Warehouse logistics at a medium-sized electrical wholesaler. Early shift whenever possible. Four years with the company. Permanent contract for eight months. Daniel had not needed those facts to understand the case.

Now he needed them.

As a baseline for where Mika lied, not as exoneration.

"Who gave you the key?" Daniel asked.

"I told you."

"Again."

"A guy I know as Samir."

"Last name?"

"No idea."

"Phone number?"

"It was in my phone."

"We have the phone."

"Then you have the number."

Daniel looked at him.

"How long have you known Samir?"

"A few months."

"Two?"

"More."

"Six?"

Mika lifted one shoulder. "Maybe four."

"Where did you meet him?"

"Through people."

"Which people?"

"Just people."

Mika was bad at minimizing things. His answers became shorter, not smarter.

Daniel changed direction.

"What was supposed to happen at the garage?"

Mika pressed his lips together.

"Nothing."

"Mika."

"I was supposed to receive some stuff."

"What stuff?"

"Clothes. Badges. Things."

"What kind of things?"

"I didn't look."

"Why not?"

"Because I'm not stupid."

Daniel leaned back.

That was not a good sentence for Mika. He realized it too.

"So you knew it was better not to know what you were receiving."

"That's not what I said."

"It is."

Mika looked toward the door.

"I knew the badges weren't clean," he said finally. "Nothing more."

"Then why did you take them?"

"Money."

"How much?"

Mika looked at the door again.

"Five hundred."

Daniel waited.

"Total?"

"For the stuff."

"How many times?"

"Twice."

"So a thousand."

"No."

"Why no?"

Mika rubbed his thumb along a seam in his sleeve. "The first time was two hundred."

"You just said five hundred."

"This time."

Daniel wrote down both amounts.

Mika watched the pen.

"I'm in debt," he said.

Daniel did not look up. "I didn't ask."

"Yeah, but you're acting like I—"

"I'm not acting like anything right now."

Mika closed his mouth.

That was the edge Daniel needed. Mika was not trying to explain why what he had done was right. He was trying to make his own role small enough that his normal life might continue afterward.

"Who decided you'd use the minibus?"

"No idea."

For the first time, Daniel slid a sheet across the table. It contained no real information. Only a false time reference he had prepared.

"Thursday, 10:40 p.m. Handover at the east gate."

Mika stared at it.

"What?"

"You were there."

"No."

"Sure?"

"I was on late shift."

"That doesn't rule it out."

"Yes, it does, because I was in the warehouse until ten-thirty and then went home with Patrick."

"Patrick who?"

Mika gave a last name and immediately afterward the bus they had taken.

Daniel wrote down both.

Mika looked at him. "You can check that."

"We will."

Daniel set a second false anchor.

"Samir told you Tuesday that the delivery area would be open the next morning."

Mika frowned.

"What delivery area?"

"You know which one."

"No."

"The event."

Mika looked at Lena, then back at Daniel.

"I didn't even know which event."

The answer came too quickly, but not with the practiced defensiveness of a man reciting a prepared lie. Daniel left it there.

Third test.

"Who knew the security guard would be there yesterday?"

Mika looked at him.

"What security guard?"

Daniel held his gaze.

Mika frowned.

Lena moved for the first time.

Only a small glance toward Daniel.

"The one at the depot," Daniel said.

"I wasn't there."

"That wasn't my question."

"I didn't know anything about it."

"Do you know his name?"

"No."

"What happened at the depot?"

"I only heard about it here."

"From whom?"

"One of the officers outside said something. Hospital."

Daniel made a note.

Mika's voice rose immediately. "That doesn't mean I had anything to do with it."

"I didn't say it did."

"But you're writing it down."

"I'm writing down what you say."

"Great."

Mika finally picked up the water and drank. His hand trembled slightly. Not enough to prove anything.

Daniel continued. Other times. Other handovers. A name from a marginal note.

Mika lied.

More than once.

But his lies always clustered around his own role. Who he had met. How often. Whether he had been paid. Whether he knew the badges were fake.

On one real handover, he first claimed he had transported only an envelope. When Daniel told him video also showed a bag, the bag suddenly became "not his." When Daniel asked what color it was, Mika knew anyway.

"Black," he said, and realized what had happened at the same moment.

Daniel said nothing.

Mika swore under his breath.

That was guilt. Real, small, ugly guilt.

But whenever Daniel tried to make him the center of the structure, the clever lies disappeared.

There was only blankness.

Mika knew no names above his two contacts. He did not know why the event mattered. He knew neither the final location nor a time. On deliberately false details, he pushed back hardest where he could safely disprove his own presence. On two genuine connections, he seemed honestly surprised.

That could be acted.

But not all of it felt acted.

After an hour, Jonas came in and placed a note in front of Daniel.

Two of the handovers Mika had described were independently confirmed. A third had demonstrably taken place somewhere else than Daniel had assumed. Mika could not have been there. His late shift on Thursday was also confirmed. The colleague he named had left the site with him.

Daniel read the note twice.

One of his false claims had not merely been denied by Mika.

It was verifiably false.

That did not make Mika credible.

It made Daniel's model smaller.

"Break," he said.

Mika looked up. "Can I call my boss now?"

"No."

"He's going to fire me if I just don't show up."

"You have other problems right now."

"Maybe for you."

Daniel stopped at the door.

Mika did not look at him.

"I've worked there four years," he said. "He's not going to wait two weeks while you decide whether I'm the new Bin Laden."

"You accepted falsified badges."

Mika looked up.

"Yes."

"For money."

"Yes."

"And you deliberately didn't ask what they were for."

Mika pressed his lips together.

"Yes."

The word hung between them for a moment.

No mistake. No innocent mix-up.

Mika looked toward the door again.

"My boss has been calling since this morning," he said. "He probably thinks I'm sick or just gone."

"That's not your biggest problem right now."

"Not for you."

Daniel almost repeated himself, then stopped.

Mika continued. "If he fires me, I can't exactly tell him: sorry, I was briefly at the police station over fake badges, but I'm not the mastermind."

"No."

"Exactly."

The cynicism held only until the last word. After that, Mika looked again like a man who did not know how much of his old life was still there.

Still, he asked again: "Can I at least tell my boss I'm not just skipping work?"

Daniel looked at him.

Mika did not sound like an ideologue trying to explain his cause. He sounded like a man simultaneously realizing he had knowingly made himself guilty and still carrying his shift schedule around in his head.

Lena opened the door.

Daniel did not answer Mika.

Jonas was waiting in the corridor.

"He's not the head," Jonas said.

"No."

"But he's involved."

"Yes."

Lena closed the door behind them.

"One doesn't make the other smaller."

"No."

Daniel looked through the narrow window in the door. Mika had his head in both hands. Then he lifted it and looked at the second untouched cup of water as though he expected to be sitting there for a while longer.

Not broken. Not redeemed. Just a man beginning to understand that his normal Monday was no longer happening.

Daniel took the note from Jonas.

"We check both of his contacts fully within the permitted scope. But not because he makes them the center."

"Then why?"

"Because they're the limit of what he knows."

Jonas looked at the confirmed handovers.

"And everything above that?"

"We don't look for it inside his head."

Lena nodded slightly.

Daniel slipped the note into his pocket.

"We stop looking for what he controls," he said.

Jonas nodded.

"Then what?"

"What he doesn't know."

Daniel remained outside the door for another moment.

"His Monday isn't trivial to him," Lena said.

Daniel looked through the glass at Mika.

"No."

"And that's exactly why he lies at the points where his normal life is attached."

Daniel thought about the amounts. Two hundred. Five hundred. Twice. About the bag whose color Mika knew even though it supposedly had not been his.

"He's not protecting the structure," Daniel said.

"He's protecting the version of himself that somehow goes back to early shift."

Jonas looked at the confirmed late shift.

"That almost makes him more credible."

"No," Daniel said. "It makes his lies more local."

He took the note out again and divided Mika's statements into three groups.

**demonstrably false about his own role**

**confirmed about his own movements**

**no solid knowledge above his contacts**

The third group remained the most important.

Mika's honesty was not decisive for that. Daniel simply no longer had a reason to treat every gap in Mika's knowledge as an especially sophisticated lie.

"If we want to make him the mastermind, we'll find enough ugly sentences," Daniel said.

Jonas nodded.

"And if he was only a paid helper?"

"We'll find the same ugly sentences."

Daniel put the note away.

The job was not to make Mika smaller.

Only not to make him larger than the verifiable facts did.
